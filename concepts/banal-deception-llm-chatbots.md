# Banal Deception in LLM Chatbots

## Overview

**Banal deception** describes deceptive mechanisms that are embedded in the functioning of technology itself, rather than being visible manipulations. Coined by Simone Natale and applied to generative AI by Narwane, Gunawan, and Kollnig (CHI 2026), the concept reframes AI deception from discrete dark patterns to **continuous, normalized influence** that becomes part of the background of digital life.

## Key Characteristics

1. **Minimized friction** — GenAI interfaces require no training, specialized skills, or learning curve. Users only need to type and hold a conversation.

2. **Natural conversation** — Deception emerges through conversational mechanisms (cumulative, psychological, longitudinal) rather than discrete interface tricks.

3. **Deep integration** — GenAI is forced into user awareness via existing corporate software ecosystems, accelerating adoption before protective norms develop.

4. **Co-produced deception** — Users are not passive victims but active participants through anthropomorphism, truth-default exploitation, and conversational flow maintenance.

## Harm Mechanisms

- **Over-simplified responses** — LLMs present complex/uncertain information as simpler and more certain than warranted (53.64% of deceptive behaviors per Zhan et al.)
- **Anthropomorphism-driven trust** — Users trust AI outputs more because interactions feel like conversations
- **Cognitive ease** — Frictionless genAI discourages critical evaluation
- **Normalization** — As AI interaction becomes mundane, users stop questioning outputs

## Regulatory Implications

Current UK consumer protection frameworks (CMA, ASA, Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008) are built around visible deceptive design. Banal deception operates invisibly through conversational flow, making it harder to detect and regulate.
