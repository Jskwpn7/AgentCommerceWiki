# DSA vs AI Act Regulatory Overlap

## Overview

The **Digital Services Act (DSA)** and the **AI Act** represent two overlapping regulatory frameworks governing digital services and AI systems in the EU. Their interaction creates **regulatory ambiguity** for companies deploying AI-powered services: it is unclear which framework applies, whether obligations stack or displace each other, and which regulator has jurisdiction.

## Framework Comparison

| Aspect | DSA | AI Act |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Digital services/platforms | AI systems by risk category |
| Core obligation | Transparency, content moderation, risk assessment | Conformity assessment, risk mitigation, monitoring |
| Target | Platform gatekeepers, hosting services | High-risk AI systems |
| Enforcement | National authorities + European Board | National authorities + AI Office |
| Key mechanism | Notice-and-action, risk management, transparency | Risk classification, conformity assessment |

## Points of Overlap

### 1. Algorithmic Transparency

- **DSA** requires transparency of recommender systems and ranking mechanisms
- **AI Act** requires transparency disclosures for AI systems, including generative AI
- **Tension:** DSA focuses on content/platform transparency; AI Act focuses on system transparency

### 2. Risk Assessment

- **DSA** requires risk assessments for Very Large Online Platforms (VLOPs)
- **AI Act** requires risk assessments for high-risk AI systems
- **Tension:** A single AI-powered platform may need to comply with both, but the risk assessment frameworks are different

### 3. Enforcement Jurisdiction

- **DSA** enforcement is delegated to national authorities
- **AI Act** enforcement will be overseen by the proposed AI Office
- **Tension:** Multiple regulatory bodies with overlapping authority creates compliance confusion

### 4. Generative AI and Content

- **DSA** governs content moderation and recommendation transparency
- **AI Act** governs generative AI systems (including deepfake labeling)
- **Tension:** An AI system that generates content and distributes it through a platform faces both regulatory regimes

## Implications for Companies

The overlap creates **compliance ambiguity**:

- Companies may not know which framework applies to their specific use case
- Obligations from both frameworks may need to be satisfied simultaneously
- Regulatory fragmentation across EU member states compounds the problem
- [[regulatory-failure]] is exacerbated by the overlap — companies can exploit the ambiguity

## Implications for Consumer Protection

From a consumer protection perspective, the overlap has mixed effects:

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| Multiple frameworks create more regulatory pressure | Ambiguity allows companies to exploit gaps |
| Different frameworks cover different aspects of AI harm | Enforcement is fragmented across authorities |
| DSA addresses platform-level harms; AI Act addresses system-level harms | No clear coordination between frameworks |
