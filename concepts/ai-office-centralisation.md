# AI Governance Centralisation — AI Office Powers

## Definition

The Digital Omnibus proposes changes to Articles 75 and 77 of the AI Act that significantly reshape enforcement by expanding the AI Office's direct supervisory and enforcement powers while introducing intermediary requirements between national authorities and AI providers.

## How it Manifests in the UK

The UK could adopt a similar centralised enforcement model under its proposed AI regulatory authorities, potentially creating single points of failure for enforcement and reducing the effectiveness of sectoral regulators (FCA, Ofcom, CMA) that have direct industry relationships.

## Examples and Evidence

- **Direct powers expansion (Article 75):** AI Office gains direct supervisory and enforcement powers over general-purpose AI-based systems and AI systems integrated into VLOPs/VLOSEs under the Digital Services Act [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
- **Commission delegated power:** The Commission would be empowered to define the AI Office's enforcement powers (including fines) through implementing acts, and to organise/delegate pre-market conformity assessments [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
- **Resourcing gap unaddressed:** AI Office is still becoming operational and already struggles with current tasks — adding direct powers requires "significant additional expertise, staffing and financial resources" [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
- **National authority role at risk:** Consumers' first point of contact are national authorities; centralisation must not weaken their role [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
- **Intermediary bottleneck (Article 77):** Fundamental rights authorities would have to submit "reasoned requests" through market surveillance authorities instead of directly contacting providers, creating delays and gatekeeping risk [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
- **Cross-border complexity:** Multiple market surveillance bodies with different procedures and priorities could significantly delay investigations in cross-border cases [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
- **Unspecified conditions:** Article 77(1a)'s obligation to "grant" access to information is "subject to unspecified conditions" risking legal uncertainty and inconsistent application [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
