# Beyond Nudges: AI Agents, Antitrust, and Complexity Economics

## Source

- **Author:** Thibault Schrepel (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam)
- **Date:** 25 March 2026
- **Type:** ALTI Working Paper / Preprint
- **SSRN:** [[https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6527238|SSRN Abstract]]
- **Pages:** 21

## Definition

This working paper argues that behavioural economics (BE) is the intuitive but insufficient framework for analysing AI agents and chatbot-induced challenges in antitrust. Instead, complexity economics (CE) provides a more complete analytical lens.

The analytical puzzle AI agents pose for antitrust has **three dimensions**:

1. **How recommendations shape consumer beliefs** about products and markets.
2. **How AI agent architectures interact with and reshape the preferences** that guide consumer choice.
3. **How recommendation-driven markets alter market dynamics** in ways that may or may not produce competitive harm.

Behavioural economics sees primarily the first. Complexity economics sees all three.

## Key Arguments

- Classical antitrust assumptions about rational market participants break down when AI agents mediate transactions.
- AI agents' hypersensitivity to choice architecture (documented empirically by [[ai-agents-sensitive-to-nudges-cherep-2026]]) creates novel competitive dynamics that traditional BE-based frameworks cannot capture.
- CE captures the emergent, systemic effects of agent-mediated markets — feedback loops, path dependence, and network effects — that BE's individual-focused approach misses.
- The paper bridges the gap between empirical findings on nudge sensitivity and antitrust enforcement, arguing that AI agent manipulation of choice architecture could constitute anti-competitive coordination or exploitative abuse.

## UK Competition Law Implications

This framework is directly relevant to UK competition law under the [[CA98]] prohibition on anti-competitive agreements and abuse of dominant position. Specifically:

- **Algorithmic collusion:** If AI agents on competing platforms independently converge on similar pricing or market strategies through interaction with choice architecture, this may constitute coordinated effect even without explicit agreement.
- **Exploitative abuse:** Dominant platforms could design choice architectures that AI agents — given their documented hypersensitivity — systematically exploit, to the detriment of consumers.
- **Market structure investigation triggers:** The CMA's market investigation powers may need to extend to agent-mediated market dynamics under [[cma-ai-and-consumer-markets-2026]].
