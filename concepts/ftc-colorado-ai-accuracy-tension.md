# FTC vs Colorado AI Act — Accuracy vs Fairness Tension

> **Cross-jurisdictional analysis** of the emerging regulatory fault line between federal AI accuracy requirements and state-level AI fairness mandates.

## Overview

The FTC's July 1, 2026 proposed policy statement establishes that AI companies that steer system outputs toward undisclosed ideological objectives — including suppressing accuracy to avoid disparate impact liability — engage in deceptive acts under Section 5 of the FTC Act. The FTC explicitly singles out Colorado's AI Act as an example of state law that may pressure AI companies to suppress accuracy, concluding such state requirements are **impliedly preempted** by federal consumer protection law.

This creates a direct federal-state contradiction: state laws incentivize one behaviour (accuracy suppression for fairness) while federal law declares that same behaviour deceptive.

## Regulatory Positions

### Federal (FTC) — July 1, 2026

| Position | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| **AI accuracy as consumer right** | Consumers have a "reasonable expectation" that AI systems provide truthful, accurate outputs |
| **Steering as deception** | Systematically steering AI outputs away from accuracy for undisclosed objectives violates Section 5 |
| **Colorado AI Act** | Explicitly named as an example of state law pressuring AI companies to suppress accuracy; concluded to be impliedly preempted |
| **Scope** | Applies to all AI providers operating in the US market |

### State (Colorado AI Act)

| Position | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| **Algorithmic discrimination prevention** | Requires AI companies to test for and mitigate disparate impact across protected classes |
| **Accuracy suppression mechanism** | Some provisions may incentivize reducing accuracy in certain outputs to achieve statistical parity |
| **Preemption vulnerability** | FTC argues these provisions conflict with federal consumer protection obligations |

## Implications for AI Providers

### Compliance Dilemma

AI companies face an impossible choice:

1. **Follow Colorado requirements** → Risk FTC enforcement for deceptive accuracy suppression
2. **Follow FTC position** → Risk state enforcement for failing to mitigate algorithmic discrimination
3. **Follow both** → May be technically impossible if accuracy suppression is the only path to compliance

### Strategic Response

The FTC position forces AI providers toward **transparency-first approaches**:

- Disclose when accuracy trade-offs are being made for fairness objectives
- Make fairness-accuracy decisions explicit rather than implicit
- Provide consumers with information about AI system limitations and biases
- This reduces the risk of both federal and state enforcement
