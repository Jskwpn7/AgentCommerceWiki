# FTC AI Accuracy and Deceptive Steering Policy (July 2026)

**Status:** Proposed policy statement — public comment period closed July 31, 2026
**Relevant Regulators:** Federal Trade Commission (FTC), US Department of Justice (DOJ)
**Primary Source:** [FTC.gov press release (July 1, 2026)](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/07/ftc-seeks-public-comment-policy-statement-addressing-ai-accuracy)

## Overview

On **July 1, 2026**, the Federal Trade Commission published a proposed policy statement addressing whether AI companies that steer their systems' outputs toward undisclosed ideological objectives — rather than toward the objectives that consumers request or reasonably expect — may be engaging in deceptive acts or practices in violation of § 5 of the FTC Act.

The proposed statement was authorised by a 2-0 Commission vote and published for public comment in the Federal Register. Comment submissions were accepted through **July 31, 2026** (docket: FTC-2026-0859).

## Key Provisions

### 1. AI Systems Subject to FTC Act § 5

The proposed statement explicitly states that **AI products and services are not exempt from Section 5 of the FTC Act**, which prohibits deceptive conduct in business and which the FTC has the power to enforce.

### 2. Deceptive Steering Definition

The statement articulates that AI companies that **"steer the outputs of their AI systems toward unexpected objectives and away from the objectives set by or reasonably expected by users"** are likely to deceive consumers, absent adequate disclaimers or qualifications.

The FTC states that **"consumers have a reasonable expectation that the objectives of AI systems are to produce truthful and accurate outputs."**

### 3. Conflict with State Laws

The statement singles out **Colorado's Artificial Intelligence Act** as an example of a state law that may pressure AI companies to suppress output accuracy to avoid disparate impact liability. The FTC concludes that such state laws are **"impliedly preempted to the extent they conflict with the federal regulatory scheme established by § 5 of the FTC Act."**

### 4. Presidential Executive Order Mandate

In a December executive order, President Trump directed the FTC to issue a policy statement addressing the legal implications of state laws that require alteration of the **"truthful outputs of AI models."**

### 5. Chairman Ferguson Statement

> "The FTC wants to hear from businesses and consumers about their experiences and concerns regarding the subversion of AI systems for ideological ends. This crucial input will help the Commission formulate a final policy that advances President Donald Trump's goal of expanding America's global dominance in artificial intelligence."
> — Chairman Andrew N. Ferguson

## Consumer Harm Vectors Addressed

This policy statement targets several consumer harm vectors in the AI-driven commerce ecosystem:

| Harm Vector | Mechanism | Relevance |
|-------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Output manipulation** | AI systems deliberately distorted to produce biased or misleading recommendations | Directly impacts AI shopping agents, financial advice AI, and search assistants |
| **Ideological steering** | Undisclosed bias toward specific ideological outcomes in AI-generated content | Affects consumer trust in AI as a neutral decision support tool |
| **Accuracy suppression** | State-mandated or voluntary suppression of truthful AI outputs | Creates market failure where consumers cannot distinguish accurate from manipulated outputs |
| **Lack of transparency** | No disclosure of AI system objectives or steering mechanisms | Violates consumers' reasonable expectations of AI transparency |

## Regulatory Tension

The FTC's proposed statement creates a direct regulatory conflict with several state AI laws, particularly Colorado's AI Act. This tension represents a significant legal question for the future:

- **Federal (FTC):** AI companies must provide accurate, truthful outputs as expected by consumers; state laws that require suppression of accuracy are preempted
- **State (Colorado):** AI companies must mitigate disparate impact, potentially requiring output modification that the FTC views as deceptive

This conflict has direct implications for:
- AI companies operating across multiple states
- Consumer protection agencies at state and federal levels
- The enforceability of state-level AI consumer protection frameworks

## Significance for AI Consumer Protection

The FTC's July 2026 proposed policy statement represents:

1. **The first federal policy statement** explicitly applying the FTC Act to AI output accuracy and transparency
2. **A direct challenge to state AI regulations**, creating a federalism question about which framework governs AI consumer protection
3. **A foundation for future enforcement**, providing a framework for identifying deceptive AI conduct
4. **A consumer-centric framing** — "reasonable consumer expectations" of truth and accuracy as the legal standard

This complements the UK CMA's parallel approach, which focuses on competition-level harms (algorithmic collusion, market concentration), while the FTC focuses on consumer-level harms (deceptive output, accuracy manipulation).
