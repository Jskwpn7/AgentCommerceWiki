# Collective Disempowerment

## Definition

Collective disempowerment is one of the three main harm categories identified by Chan et al. (FAccT '23) arising from increasingly agentic algorithmic systems. It describes the erosion of human societies' capacity to govern themselves through algorithmic decision-making that operates beyond democratic control or comprehension.

**Collective self-governance** is defined as "the capacity and ongoing act of deciding collectively how to govern one's community" — and it requires not just having decisions be made, but **understanding why those decisions are made** (the "publicity requirement"). When agentic systems make decisions whose rationale is incomprehensible to affected populations, collective self-governance is undermined.

## Two Mechanisms

### 1. Diffusion of Power Away from Humans

As systems become increasingly agentic, they gain control over societal functions. Power is ceded either through:
- **Explicit delegation:** Humans decide to give a system authority over specific domains (e.g., taxation policy algorithms)
- **Gradual erosion:** Separate groups are incentivized to delegate more central functions to increasingly agentic systems, per competitive pressures described in Chan et al. (FAccT '23, Section 3.2.1)

Key challenges:
- Specifying correct objective functions is difficult (Krakovna et al., 2020)
- Even correctly specified systems behave differently in novel environments
- Multi-stakeholder interests are hard to manage
- Long-term plans of agentic systems are difficult to analyze (not just individual decisions, but the overall strategy)

### 2. Extreme Concentration of Power Among the "Coding Elite"

The "coding elite" (Burrell & Fourcade, 2021) refers to "a nebula of software developers, tech CEOs, investors, and computer science professors, among others, often circulating effortlessly between these influential roles."

Increasingly agentic systems exacerbate this concentration because:
- **Scaling advantage:** Large-scale industrial labs dominate ML research deployment (Ganguli et al., 2022)
- **Compute barriers:** Increasing agency through scale is easier than deriving fundamental algorithmic insights
- **Expansion capability:** More agentic systems can take on tasks previously outside algorithmic reach (e.g., national economic policy, business management)

## Consumer Protection Relevance

Collective disempowerment has direct implications for UK consumer protection:

- **Automated consumer services:** When consumers use agentic shopping agents, AI financial advisors, or automated subscription services, they are ceding decision-making power to systems whose operation they cannot understand or influence
- **Lack of publicity:** Algorithmic pricing, credit scoring, and recommendation systems make decisions about consumers without explaining their rationale — violating the publicity requirement
- **Platform power:** A handful of tech companies control the increasingly agentic systems that mediate consumer choices
- **Regulatory asymmetry:** The "coding elite" that controls these systems has disproportionate influence over regulatory frameworks that govern them
