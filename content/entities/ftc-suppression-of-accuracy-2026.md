# FTC Policy Action: Suppression of AI Accuracy

## Overview

On **July 1, 2026**, the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) voted unanimously (2-0) to publish a proposed policy statement titled **"Suppression of Accuracy in Artificial Intelligence Systems."** The statement establishes that systematically steering AI system outputs away from accuracy — whether for ideological, political, or commercial objectives — can violate Section 5 of the FTC Act (unfair or deceptive practices).

A comment period was opened running through **July 31, 2026**, allowing stakeholders to weigh in before finalization.

## Key Provisions

1. **Undisclosed steering = deception:** Manipulating AI outputs for undisclosed objectives violates Section 5 of the FTC Act.
2. **Reasonable expectation of accuracy:** Consumers expect AI systems to provide truthful, accurate outputs.
3. **Applies to all AI interfaces:** Search engines, chatbots, shopping agents, recommendation systems.
4. **Covers both ideological and commercial objectives:** The policy does not distinguish between political bias and sponsored content manipulation — both are treated as potential deception.

## Regulatory Significance

- First US federal regulatory action explicitly addressing AI accuracy suppression as a consumer protection issue
- Provides legal framework that complements the **AI AGENT Act** (Warner, June 29, 2026), which proposes fiduciary duties for custodial AI agents
- Signals a shift from voluntary frameworks (NIST AI RMF) to potential enforcement actions

## Connection to UK Context

- UK CPRs 2008 (misleading actions/omissions) operate on similar principles
- DMCC Act 2024 CMA powers provide complementary enforcement mechanisms
- Could inform CMA guidance on AI-enabled consumer harms
