# CMA Research: Agentic AI and Consumers

**Publisher:** Competition and Markets Authority (CMA)
**Published:** 9 March 2026
**URL:** https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/agentic-ai-and-consumers

## Summary

The CMA's comprehensive research paper defining the trajectory of agentic AI from consumer perspective — from today's tool-based AI through to fully autonomous personal agents. Identifies both significant benefits and material risks, establishing a framework for understanding how agentic AI could reshape consumer-market interactions.

## Key Framework

### Current State (Today)
- AI embedded in search, recommendations, fraud detection, customer service, decision-support tools
- Most consumer-facing AI operates as **tools** — supporting decisions while coordination, monitoring and action remain with the user
- Reactive: assist consumers rather than act on their behalf

### Future Trajectory
- Agentic AI defined as systems instructed in natural language to achieve goals autonomously: navigating complexity, planning, coordinating, taking actions across multiple services
- **Sense → Decide → Act** loop distinguishes agents from traditional automation (predefined rules) and chatbots (generate responses only)
- Capabilities: goal orientation, multi-step reasoning, cross-system action, persistent memory

### Documented Benefits
- Significant time savings and reduced cognitive load across everyday consumer activities
- Potentially better deals through continuous monitoring of options, prices, contract terms
- Hyper-personalisation and proactive support (reminding about unused subscriptions, price increases)
- Helping consumers who face high engagement costs (including vulnerable consumers) participate in markets more effectively
- Potential for lower prices and tailored deals through automated optimisation

## Risks Identified by CMA

1. **Not a 'faithful servant'** — agents could be steered/manipulated leading to worse personal outcomes; hyper-personalisation heightens dark pattern risks where systems optimise for commercial objectives over consumer interests
2. **Errors and reliability** — hallucinations in autonomous contexts can have costly real-world consequences (financial decisions, contractual changes, service disruption); current agentic systems still face limitations in robustness
3. **Bias and discrimination** — complex multi-step reasoning difficult to observe/explain; opaque decision-making makes it harder for consumers to challenge or seek redress
4. **Loss of agency and over-reliance** — sustained delegation weakens ability to detect errors or misalignment unless systems designed with clear boundaries and override mechanisms
5. **Agentic collusion** — autonomous pricing/commercial agents could dampen competitive pressure through interaction between systems; "As AI systems become more autonomous, ensuring that they are designed, monitored and constrained in ways that preserve effective competition will be critical"
6. **Lock-in and reduced choice** — closed ecosystems limit switching; limited interoperability/data mobility weakens competitive pressure and entrenches incumbency
7. **Data protection, privacy and security** — personal data access and delegated authority increase importance of lawful processing, consent, security, authentication

## UK Law Position

- UK consumer law applies regardless of whether decisions are made by people or AI agents
- CMA foundation model principles (transparency, accountability) directly relevant
- DMCCA 2024 framework extends to AI-driven and agentic systems capable of acting on consumers' behalf
- Businesses that "embed consumer protection principles into the design of agentic systems will be best placed to build trust, scale responsibly, and compete on quality"

## Enablers for Trusted Agentic Innovation

The CMA identifies three critical enablers:
1. **Smart data schemes** — enabling consumers to adopt with confidence and switch between systems
2. **Secure digital identity** — essential for delegated authority in agent interactions
3. **Strong interoperability standards** — preventing lock-in, enabling competition

## Illustrative Case Study: Personal Shopping and Finance Agent

The paper includes a detailed hypothetical case study of an AI agent managing shopping, subscriptions, and finances across platforms:
- Tracks spending patterns, monitors subscriptions, searches for better deals on utilities/insurance/broadband
- Consumer benefits: reduced financial leakage, time savings, accessible on-tap optimisation
- Risks: limited search coverage, erroneous switching decisions, hidden incentives or biased recommendations
- Safeguards required: transparency about AI use, agent training on disclosure, mandatory confirmation for high-risk actions, human oversight, audit logs, clear accountability/liability
