# AI and the UK Financial Conduct Authority — WilmerHale Analysis

**Publisher:** WilmerHale (legal analysis)
**Published:** 29 April 2026
**Authors:** Lloyd Firth (Partner), Imogen Makin (Counsel)
**URL:** https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260429-ai-and-the-uk-financial-conduct-authority

## Summary

Comprehensive analysis of FCA enforcement risks from AI use in financial services, structured around the FCA's principles-based approach: no bespoke AI rulebook — existing frameworks applied to AI systems.

## Key Framework Elements

### The Consumer Duty (as lens for AI)
- Where AI influences product design, distribution, pricing, eligibility/creditworthiness, servicing or customer communications: outputs must be tested for fairness, suitability and foreseeable harm
- FCA emphasises evidence-based supervision and close scrutiny of systems/processes — firms should document and monitor AI use, testing and remediation

### SM&CR and Governance Ownership for AI
- FCA/Bank of England 2024 joint survey: large proportion of firms have a person accountable for AI — aligned with FCA expectations
- Clear responsibility required for: approving AI use cases, managing model/data risk, overseeing third-party AI services, ensuring operational resilience

### Enforcement Risk Areas (7 Key Hazards)

1. **Consumer harm from opaque/biased AI decision-making** — credit, pricing, insurance underwriting, complaints handling, customer interaction
2. **SM&CR accountability failure for AI systems** — no clear ownership or approval processes
3. **Poor data governance** — models trained/operated with flawed data leading to unfair/misleading outcomes
4. **Over-reliance on third-party/black box AI providers** — unable to evidence testing/validation/oversight of deployed third-party models
5. **AI-driven market abuse or disruption** — trading, pricing or execution contributing to market abuse
6. **Misleading disclosures about AI capability or safeguards** ("AI-washing" compared to "green washing")

## FCA's Own AI Use (Transformative Development)

The FCA has engaged a third-party technology provider to trial AI tools analysing:
- Suspicious activity reports
- Customer complaints
- Live case files

This has potential to "transform the regulator's financial crime and regulatory breach detection, disruption, and associated supervision and enforcement capabilities."

## Best Practice Recommendations

1. Governance, accountability and documentation aligned to SM&CR expectations
2. Third-party AI services: contracting, access, exit and resilience per FCA outsourcing definitions
3. Operational resilience integration — AI components in IBS mapping, impact tolerances, scenario testing

## Relevance to UK Consumer Harms

- Confirms active enforcement stance (no new AI-specific regime needed — existing powers sufficient)
- Consumer Duty is primary regulatory lens for AI-related conduct in financial services
- Creates precedent: FCA enforcement through existing frameworks could inform CMA/ICO approaches under DMCCA 2024 and DUA 2025
