# CMA Guidance: Complying with Consumer Law When Using AI Agents

**Publisher:** Competition and Markets Authority (CMA)
**Published:** 9 March 2026
**URL:** https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/complying-with-consumer-law-when-using-ai-agents

## Summary

The CMA published the world's first regulatory guidance specifically addressing business obligations when deploying autonomous AI agents in consumer-facing contexts. The guidance establishes that:

- **Same rules apply** — consumer law requires treating customers fairly regardless of whether decisions are made by people or AI agents
- Businesses are **responsible for what their AI agents do**, just as they would be for employees; this extends to third-party-designed AI agents procured from other suppliers
- Breach penalties: fines up to **10% of worldwide turnover** and potential compensation orders for affected consumers

## Key Principles (7 Core Requirements)

1. **Disclose AI agent use** — tell customers they are dealing with an AI agent, not a person, where this might affect their decisions. Do not overstate the role or capabilities of AI involved in providing a service.
2. **Train agents to comply** — design prompts and workflows that make agents respect statutory rights (e.g., cancellation rights), avoid misleading claims, properly obtain consents required by consumer law. Testing (A/B testing, unit testing) is crucial before deployment.
3. **Monitor agent performance** — regularly check agents are delivering intended results, behaving as intended, complying with consumer law. Watch for hallucination and inaccurate outputs.
4. **Human oversight** — maintain a human in the loop actively checking that AI agents make correct decisions and generate expected results. "Regular human oversight is important to catch mistakes."
5. **Refine quickly on problems** — act swiftly if an agent is not performing as expected, especially when interacting with large numbers of people or vulnerable customers. Prompt refinement prevents legal liability.
6. **DMCCA 2024 design requirements** — marketing materials must provide accurate prices including all unavoidable charges; paid endorsements properly labelled; offers genuinely represented.
7. **Consumer rights compliance** — agents must account for Consumer Rights Act 2015, Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, and any extended returns policies when processing refunds or handling customer queries.

## Illustrative Examples Covered

- **Marketing campaigns:** AI-generated materials require human review; social media influencer endorsement labelling must be consistently checked
- **Refund processing:** Agents must accurately reflect consumer statutory rights and extended return policies
- **Customer service queries:** Accurate information about prices, products, and rights; do not make it difficult to exercise rights
- **Deal comparison services:** Results must match search criteria, with transparent disclosure of market coverage, data searched, limitations, ranking methodology, and supplier links

## Relevance to UK Consumer Harms Research

This is the definitive UK regulatory position on agentic AI: consumer law applies fully regardless of automation level. The CMA explicitly extends liability from human employees to AI agents (including third-party-designed ones), establishing that businesses cannot outsource compliance responsibility by procuring autonomous systems. This guidance directly supports enforcement under the DMCCA 2024 and forms the foundation for future regulatory actions against non-compliant agentic AI deployments.
