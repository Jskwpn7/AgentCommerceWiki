# Ada Lovelace Institute

## Overview

The Ada Lovelace Institute was established by the **Nuffield Foundation** in early 2018, in collaboration with The Alan Turing Institute, the Royal Society, the British Academy, the Royal Statistical Society, the Wellcome Trust, Luminate, techUK and the Nuffield Council on Bioethics.

The Institute is a UK-based independent research organization focused on data and AI policy.

## Mission

"To ensure that data and AI work for people and society. We believe that a world where data and AI work for people and society is a world in which the opportunities, benefits and privileges generated by data and AI are justly and equitably distributed and experienced."

The Institute recognizes "the power asymmetries that exist in ethical and legal debates around the development of data-driven technologies" and aims to represent people in those conversations. It focuses "not on the types of technologies we want to build, but on the types of societies we want to build."

## Funding

Funded by the **Nuffield Foundation**, an independent charitable trust with a mission to advance social well-being.

## Key Outputs

- **Delegation Nation** (February 2025): Policy briefing on Advanced AI Assistants and their implications for consumers, policymakers, and society. Identifies risks of market concentration, emotional dependency, and the gatekeeping power of AI systems. [[farmer-smakman-2025-delegation-nation]]
- **Foundation Models Explainer** (July 2023): Educational resource on foundation models.

## Research Priorities (as stated in Delegation Nation)

The Institute's project on Advanced AI Assistants is designed to help equip policymakers and will include:
- Further articulating and exploring issues and challenges posed by Advanced AI Assistants
- Surveying gaps in current legal and regulatory protections
- Understanding informed public opinion, including views of experienced users
- Putting forward concrete recommendations for the UK Government

## Relationships

- [[gillian-k-hadfield]] — co-author of foundational work on AI agents in markets (Hadfield & Koh 2025), also published by the Institute's network
- UK government AI policy — the briefing engages directly with UK Government's AI blueprint and calls for policy action

## Notes

- The Institute's name is a reference to Ada Lovelace, the 19th-century mathematician widely regarded as the first computer programmer
- Multiple prestigious UK institutions involved in its founding signals significant institutional weight behind its research
- Its interdisciplinary composition (science academies + tech industry + charitable trusts) gives it broad UK policy influence
