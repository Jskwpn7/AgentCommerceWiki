# EU AI Act — Artificial Intelligence Act

## Overview

The EU Artificial Intelligence Act is EU legislation designed to ensure safety, transparency, and ethical deployment of AI systems across the EU while protecting consumers' fundamental rights. It classifies AI systems by risk level (unacceptable, high, limited, minimal) and imposes corresponding obligations on providers and deployers.

## Key Dates and Facts

- **Adopted:** 2024
- **Scope:** Risk-based product regulation for AI systems
- **Key concepts:** High-risk systems, general-purpose AI models, Very Large Online Platforms (VLOPs), transparency obligations
- **Relevant articles cited in BEUC-X-2026-007:** Articles 4a, 6(3), 6(4), 11, 17, 49(2), 50, 63, 75, 77, 95, 96, 99

## Documented Harms or Failures

- **Self-exemption loophole (Article 6(3)):** Allows providers to unilaterally conclude their AI system does not pose significant risk, contested during legislative process as a critical loophole [[ai-act-self-exemption-loophole]]
- **Insufficient AI literacy requirements (Article 4):** Being weakened by the Digital Omnibus proposal — transformed from a binding obligation on providers to non-binding encouragement [[ai-literacy-reversal]]
- **Implementation timeline delays:** "Stop-the-clock" proposal would delay compliance obligations for high-risk systems, extending exposure to unsafeguarded AI [[stop-the-clock-ai-act]]
