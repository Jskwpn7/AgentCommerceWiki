# Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum (DRCF)

**Date established:** 2020
**Members:** CMA, FCA, ICO, Ofcom
**Nature:** Joint forum — not a legal entity in its own right, no independent regulatory powers

## Overview

The DRCF brings the UK's four key digital regulators together to coordinate regulation across their overlapping remits. It was created in response to the challenge that a single digital service (e.g. an AI deployment) can simultaneously touch data protection (ICO), financial services (FCA), consumer law (CMA), and online safety (Ofcom).

## Key Publications on Agentic AI

### "The Future of Agentic AI" (March 31, 2026)
The first cross-regulatory assessment of autonomous AI systems. Catalogs risks including:
- Price collusion between LLM-based agents
- Credential theft between agents
- Hidden message injection
- Cross-regulatory harm from single deployments

### "Consumer Interest and AI" Call for Input (June 3, 2026)
Two-phase consultation on consumer attitudes and protection mechanisms:
- Phase one (consumer attitudes): closes July 3, 2026 — closed
- Phase two (protection frameworks): closes September 2, 2026 — open
- Notable proposal: Consumer Duty style outcomes-based framework applied across all sectors for AI
- See also: [[DRCF Call for Input — Consumer Interest and AI (Phase 2)]]

## Regulatory Architecture

The DRCF operates within the UK's **sector-regulator model** established by the 2023 AI White Paper:
- No standalone AI statute
- Existing regulators apply existing law through five non-statutory principles
- Safety and security, transparency and explainability, fairness, accountability and governance, contestability and redress

The DRCF's role is to keep these sector regulators aligned — it is "agenda-setting rather than imminent rulemaking" according to legal analysis.

## Connection to Consumer Harms

- **Regulatory fragmentation** — The DRCF's existence reflects the fragmentation problem in UK digital regulation
- **Cross-regulatory harm** — A single agentic AI deployment can trigger all four regulators simultaneously
- **Consumer Duty extension** — The proposed cross-sector Consumer Duty framework would represent the most significant consumer protection development for AI in the UK
- **Enforcement gap** — Without independent powers, the DRCF relies on member regulators' existing authority
