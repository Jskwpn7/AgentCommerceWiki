# OECD AI Markets Policy Brief (June 2026)

> **Publisher:** Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD)
> **Date:** June 24, 2026
> **URL:** [PDF Download](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/07/artificial-intelligence-markets_cce1ac41/d531d73f-en.pdf)
> **Type:** Policy brief

## Overview

The OECD's first comprehensive assessment of AI market structure identifies structural competition risks across the AI value chain despite rapid performance increases, price declines, and more providers entering the market. The brief confirms that algorithmic pricing tools — including generative AI — may facilitate tacit coordination between firms without explicit communication, providing authoritative international policy validation for existing academic findings on algorithmic collusion.

## Key Findings

### 1. Structural Concentration Risks Despite Market Growth

The OECD confirms that while AI model performance is improving and costs declining, structural concentration risks persist at key layers of the AI value chain:

| Layer | Concentration Risk |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Hardware** | GPU supply concentrated among few manufacturers; creates bottleneck for AI development |
| **Data** | Training data access concentrated among well-resourced incumbents |
| **Foundation models** | Few providers control models that power downstream AI services |
| **Distribution** | Platform layer creates gatekeeping risks for AI services reaching consumers |

### 2. Algorithmic Pricing and Tacit Coordination

The brief explicitly confirms that:

- **GenAI-powered algorithmic pricing tools** may facilitate tacit coordination between firms without explicit communication
- This finding corroborates the academic literature on algorithmic collusion (Calvano et al., 2020; Lin et al., 2024; Tian 2026; Affonso 2026)
- The risk extends beyond traditional algorithmic pricing to **generative AI systems** used in pricing decisions
- **Cross-references CMA 2024 work** on AI and competitive dynamics in downstream markets, creating policy alignment between OECD and UK regulator

### 3. Implications for Consumer Welfare

The OECD identifies specific consumer harm vectors:

1. **Price elevation** through tacit coordination between AI pricing systems
2. **Reduced consumer choice** through incumbent entrenchment at critical value chain layers
3. **Market power consolidation** where AI advantage becomes self-reinforcing
4. **Innovation dampening** when incumbents control data and distribution advantages

### 4. Policy Recommendations

The OECD calls for:

- Active monitoring of AI market structure, not just passive observation
- Competition authority collaboration on AI-specific market analysis
- Focus on data access and interoperability as competition levers
- Recognition that traditional market concentration metrics may underestimate AI market power

## Relevance to UK Consumer Protection

The OECD brief directly supports UK consumer protection analysis in three ways:

1. **Validates algorithmic collusion theory** — Provides international policy backing for the academic evidence base on [[algorithmic-collusion-pricing]]
2. **Supports CMA enforcement position** — The CMA's July 2026 discussion paper flags concentration risks; the OECD brief confirms these are globally recognized concerns
3. **Cross-jurisdictional alignment** — Suggests coordinated international approach to AI market monitoring may be warranted
