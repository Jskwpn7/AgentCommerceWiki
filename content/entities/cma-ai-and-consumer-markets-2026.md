# CMA AI and Consumer Markets (Discussion Paper, July 2026)

**Publisher:** Competition and Markets Authority (CMA)
**Type:** Discussion paper (not binding)
**Date:** July 2026
**Related documents:**
- "Agentic AI and consumers" (research paper, March 9, 2026)
- "Complying with consumer law when using AI agents" (guidance, March 9, 2026)
- DRCF "The Future of Agentic AI" (foresight paper, March 31, 2026)

## Overview

The CMA's July 2026 discussion paper "AI and Consumer Markets" examines the competitive implications of agentic AI across consumer markets. It is the third major CMA publication on agentic AI in 2026, following the March research paper and compliance guidance.

This document is the first UK regulatory document to explicitly address how AI agents reshape market structure and competition — not just consumer-facing interactions.

## Key Findings

### 1. Agentic AI as a "Step Change"

The CMA explicitly identifies agentic AI as a "potential step-change" in how consumers interact with markets, with consequences for:
- Competition structure
- Consumer welfare
- Digital ecosystem architecture

### 2. Concentration and Gatekeeping Risks

The paper highlights concentration/gatekeeping risks at two layers:

| Layer | Risk |
|-------|------|
| **Foundation model layer** | Few providers control the AI models that power agents; creates vertical integration risks |
| **Platform layer** | Platforms that control both agent interfaces and commerce infrastructure (Google/Shopify UCP, OpenAI/Stripe ACP) become unavoidable trading partners |

### 3. Overlap with DMCC Act Regime

The discussion paper signals that agentic AI competition concerns may fall within the CMA's existing DMCC Act 2024 powers, particularly:
- Chapter 1: Prohibition of anti-competitive agreements
- Chapter 2: Abuse of dominant position
- Section 27: Investigative powers for digital status

### 4. Enforcement Trajectory Signals

The paper signals potential enforcement actions in Q3/Q4 2026:
- Market study into AI shopping agents
- Investigation of protocol duopoly (ACP vs UCP)
- Guidance on fiduciary-like duties for consumer agents

## Consumer Harm Relevance

### 1. Protocol Duopoly — ACP vs UCP

The CMA's identification of platform-layer gatekeeping directly implicates the competing protocol duopolies:
- OpenAI+Stripe (ACP) — product discovery + payment rail
- Google+Shopify (UCP) — search surface + cart + checkout

Both combinations create structural incentives to favour own-ecosystem merchants, raising potential competition law concerns under the DMCC Act.

### 2. Foundation Model Concentration

The CMA's concern about concentration at the foundation model layer connects to existing wiki entries on:

### 3. Expected Enforcement Actions

The CMA's trajectory (March guidance → July discussion paper → expected Q3 follow-up) follows a familiar UK regulatory pattern:
1. Research paper (establish concern)
2. Guidance (set expectations)
3. Discussion paper (signal enforcement approach)
4. Enforcement action or formal consultation

The expected Q3 2026 enforcement actions or consultations are a key monitoring point.

## Recent Research Adding to the Discussion

- **GT Law analysis (2026-07-07)** — "AI and Consumer Markets: UK Competition and Consumer Law Implications" identifies four specific competition law risk categories for AI agents in consumer markets: (1) algorithmic collusion via multi-agent systems, (2) abuse of dominance via agent preference, (3) consumer steering/exploitative design, (4) market structural investigation triggers. Cross-references FCA, ICO, Ofcom indicating coordinated regulatory escalation. See [[oecd-ai-markets-policy-brief-2026]].

- **Nudge sensitivity amplifies CMA gatekeeping concerns** — [[agent-nudge-sensitivity]] shows AI agents are hypersensitive to choice architecture (99–100% default compliance vs 88% for humans), meaning platform-layer gatekeeping identified in this discussion paper has amplified consumer harm effects when consumers use AI agents.
- **Complexity economics lens** — [[beyond-nudges-antitrust-schrepel-2026]] argues that the competitive dynamics created by AI agents interacting with platform choice architecture require a complexity economics framework beyond traditional antitrust, directly relevant to the CMA's gatekeeping concerns.
- **Agentic commerce authenticity crisis** — [[x402-agentic-commerce-audit-ling-2026]] finds 84.98% of x402 settlements are operator-internal, suggesting the "agentic commerce" growth narrative cited as evidence of market maturity may be significantly fabricated.
- **Platform governance over autonomy bans** — Kydyrbayeva et al. (2026) show that platform transparency and oversight matter more than restricting agent autonomy for preventing collusion, supporting a market-governance approach over autonomy restrictions.
