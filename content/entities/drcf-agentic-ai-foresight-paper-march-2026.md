# DRCF Agentic AI Foresight Paper (March 2026)

**Publisher:** Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum (DRCF)
**Published:** 31 March 2026
**Participating Regulators:** CMA, FCA, ICO, Ofcom
**URL:** https://www.drcf.org.uk/publications/papers

## Summary

The DRCF — the UK's cross-regulator forum bringing together four key digital regulation bodies — published a forward-looking exploration of agentic AI and how UK regulatory frameworks can enable its responsible development. This is the first coordinated inter-agency position on autonomous AI agents across the UK's fragmented regulatory landscape.

## Key Positions

### Regulatory Philosophy
- Regulation should act as **enabler of innovation**, not barrier
- Emerging technologies should develop in ways that promote economic growth and competition while protecting consumers and their rights
- Coordinated approach needed to prevent regulatory arbitrage between sectors

### Cross-Sector Relevance
The DRCF covers four major domains, all affected by agentic AI:
1. **Competition & consumer protection** (CMA) — pricing autonomy, market distortion
2. **Financial services conduct** (FCA) — automated financial decisions, Consumer Duty
3. **Data protection & privacy** (ICO) — personal data processing, consent, security
4. **Communications & media** (Ofcom) — information ecosystems, content moderation

### Thematic Innovation Hub
- DRCF's AI & Digital Hub pilot has moved to next phase based on lessons learned
- Focus: engaging innovators with cross-regulator implications, building collaborative oversight mechanisms
- Key insight: innovators face uncertainty when their products touch multiple regulatory regimes simultaneously

## Agentic AI Specific Concerns

The paper identifies agentic AI as a technology where cross-regulator coordination is essential because autonomous agents can operate across multiple regulated domains simultaneously:
- A personal finance agent touching FCA-regulated products, CMA-governed markets, ICO data processing, and communications platforms
- Cannot be effectively supervised by any single regulator in isolation
