# DRCF "Consumer Interest and AI" Call for Input

**Source:** Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum (DRCF)
**Date:** June 3, 2026
**URL:** https://www.drcf.org.uk/news-and-events/news/call-for-input-consumer-interest-and-ai
**Analysis:** https://bratby.law/ai-consumer-protection-uk-drcf-call-for-input

## Overview

A two-phase call for input on consumer attitudes and protection mechanisms for AI, opened by the DRCF (CMA, FCA, ICO, Ofcom). Builds on the March 2026 DRCF foresight paper "The Future of Agentic AI."

## Structure

### Phase One: Consumer Attitudes (Closes July 3, 2026)
Questions 1–16 examine:
- How much risk consumers will tolerate from generative and agentic AI in exchange for convenience and lower cost
- How far consumers understand the technology
- Whether disclosures and consent mean anything to consumers
- Who consumers expect to be accountable when AI causes harm

### Phase Two: Protection Mechanisms (Closes September 2, 2026)
Questions 17–28 ask what tools, frameworks, and obligations can deliver good consumer outcomes in practice.

## Key Innovation: Consumer Duty Style Framework

The most significant element is question 25 and footnote 7, which floats an **outcomes-based model analogous to Consumer Duty** as a cross-sectoral vehicle for AI consumer protection. This is a financial-services concept being tested as a general template across the whole economy.

This is notable because:
1. The UK has no standalone AI Act (unlike the EU's AI Act)
2. The 2023 AI White Paper relied on sector regulators applying five principles
3. Consumer Duty (FCA) already exists as an outcomes-based regulatory framework
4. Applying it to AI across all sectors would be unprecedented

## Legal Framework Mapping

| Consumer Concern | Existing UK Lever | Lead Regulator |
|---|---|---|
| Transparency about AI data use | UK GDPR Articles 13, 14; Articles 22A–22D (automated decisions) | ICO |
| Fair outcomes from AI services | FCA Consumer Duty (PRIN 2A) | FCA |
| Misleading/manipulative AI practices | DMCCA 2024 (unfair commercial practices) | CMA |
| Redress when AI causes harm | Article 22C right to contest + sector complaint routes | ICO, sector regulators |
| Harmful AI-generated content | Online Safety Act 2023 | Ofcom |

## Automated Decision-Making Regime

The call lands directly on:
- **UK GDPR Article 6** lawful basis
- **Articles 22A–22D** of UK GDPR (inserted by Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, in force from February 5, 2026)
- These govern automated decision-making and require: meaningful human involvement OR permitted ground + Article 22C safeguards (information, right to make representations, human intervention, right to contest)

## Connection to UK Consumer Harms

- **Regulatory architecture:** The UK's sector-regulator model vs. EU's comprehensive AI Act creates different consumer protection paths
- **Consumer Duty extension:** If adopted, would represent the most significant consumer protection development for AI in the UK
- **Consent erosion:** Questions whether consent is the right lever for AI consumer protection — directly relevant to [[banal-deception-llm-chatbots]]
- **Data protection:** Links agentic AI to UK GDPR automated decision-making safeguards
- **Cross-regulatory enforcement:** A single AI deployment engages all four DRCF members simultaneously
