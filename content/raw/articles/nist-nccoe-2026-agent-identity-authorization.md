# Accelerating the Adoption of Software and AI Agent Identity and Authorization

**National Cybersecurity Center of Excellence (NCCoE), National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)**

DRAFT — February 2026

> [!NOTE] Content extracted from NCCoE project page and referenced concept paper PDF.

## Abstract

AI agents offer the promise of improved productivity, efficiency, and decision-making in complex scenarios. But these benefits cannot be realized without the ability to understand how identity principles such as identification, authentication, and authorization can apply to agents to provide appropriate protections while enabling business value. This concept paper seeks stakeholder input to inform a NIST National Cybersecurity Center of Excellence (NCCoE) project focused on applying existing identity standards and best practices to software and AI agents.

## Proposed Demonstration Project

The paper proposes a demonstration project using commercially available technologies across four areas:

1. **Identification**: Distinguishing AI agents from humans, managing agent metadata.
2. **Authorization**: Applying OAuth 2.0/2.1, policy-based access control to agent systems.
3. **Access Delegation**: Linking user identities to agents for accountability and traceability.
4. **Logging and Transparency**: Ensuring auditability of agent actions and decisions.

## Implications for Consumer Protection

- **Accountability**: Without clear agent identity frameworks, consumers cannot determine who (which agent, under whose authority) is acting on their behalf.
- **Consent**: Access delegation mechanisms must preserve meaningful user consent and allow users to revoke agent permissions.
- **Transparency**: Logging requirements should ensure consumers can audit agent actions and dispute unauthorized or harmful decisions.
- **Security**: Agent identity standards must address both technical security (preventing unauthorized access) and consumer-facing transparency (allowing users to understand which agents are operating).
