# How AI Agents for Commerce Could Shape Future Markets

**Source:** MIT Initiative on the Digital Economy (IDE)
**Date:** 2026
**Authors:** Ben Manning, Peyman Shahidi (AI, Marketplaces and Labor Markets research group)
**URL:** https://ide.mit.edu/insights/how-ai-agents-for-commerce-could-shape-future-markets/

## Overview

Research paper from MIT IDE's AI, Marketplaces and Labor Markets research group examining how AI agents acting as autonomous market participants will reshape demand, supply, and market design.

## Key Findings

### The Transaction Cost Hypothesis

The paper theorizes that AI shopping will **lower transaction costs to almost nothing**. Human intermediaries (agents, brokers, personal shoppers) exist because they're:
1. Cheaper than doing it yourself
2. More expert than the average consumer
3. More experienced at the transaction

AI agents threaten all three: they are near-zero cost, can access vast product knowledge, and can complete transactions at machine speed.

### Demand-Side Implications

- Consumers delegate increasingly complex purchasing decisions
- Agents can process more information than any human
- "Preference expression" shifts from human language to machine-to-machine communication
- **Consumer harm risk:** agents may be optimized for merchant interests, not consumer welfare

### Supply-Side Implications

- Businesses must make product data **agent-readable** (structured data, APIs)
- Traditional e-commerce UI (product pages, search) becomes secondary to machine-readable interfaces
- **Competition risk:** agents may favor merchants with best agent-optimization, not best products
- **Data concentration:** platforms that agents use become new gatekeepers

### Market Design Implications

- **Example: AI apartment brokers replacing human brokers** — the paper illustrates how even non-ecommerce sectors are affected
- Price discovery becomes agent-mediated, not price-comparison-site-mediated
- **Consumer harm vector:** hidden preference manipulation through agent training data
- **Regulatory challenge:** competition law assumes market participants are human; agents create new categories of anti-competitive behavior

## Connection to UK Consumer Harms

| Vector | Description |
|---|---|
| **Gatekeeper power** | Platforms that agents use become de facto gatekeepers, raising CMA Digital Markets Unit concerns |

| **Transparency loss** | Consumers lose visibility into how agents make purchasing decisions |
| **Regulatory gap** | Current competition and consumer law assumes human market participants |
| **Data protection** | Agents process vast amounts of personal data for preference modeling |
