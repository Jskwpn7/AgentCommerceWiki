# Enabling Open Finance in EMDEs: Incentives, Liability, and Performance Measurement

**Cambridge Centre for Alternative Finance (CCAF), Cambridge Judge Business School, University of Cambridge**

June 2026

> [!NOTE] Original PDF (5.7 MB) rendered as Cloudflare block. Content extracted from JBS faculty landing page and SSRN abstract.

## Key Findings

* Open finance frameworks in emerging and developing economies (EMDEs) face unique incentive and liability challenges compared to advanced economies.
* Regulatory approaches vary significantly: mandatory data-sharing mandates vs. voluntary frameworks show different adoption patterns and performance outcomes.
* Liability allocation between data holders, data recipients, and consumers is a critical factor in building trust and enabling effective open finance ecosystems.
* Performance measurement frameworks need to account for local context, infrastructure gaps, and varying levels of financial literacy.

## Methodology

The research draws on comparative analysis of open finance implementations across multiple EMDEs, examining regulatory frameworks, stakeholder incentives, and liability structures.

## Implications for Consumer Protection

* EMDE consumers may face disproportionate risks in open finance ecosystems without adequate liability protections.
* Regulatory harmonization challenges are amplified in developing economies with fragmented supervisory frameworks.
* Consumer education and awareness are prerequisites for effective open finance participation.
