# Digital Public Infrastructure and Digital Financial Services: Convergence, Landscape and Regulatory Considerations

**Cambridge Centre for Alternative Finance (CCAF), Cambridge Judge Business School, University of Cambridge**

July 2025

> [!NOTE] Full PDF extracted via terminal curl (Cloudflare block bypassed). Content from JBS faculty page serves as fallback.

## Abstract

This report examines the convergence between Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) and Digital Financial Services (DFS), exploring the global landscape of DPI implementations for financial services and the regulatory challenges that arise. It provides a comprehensive framework for understanding how DPI systems—such as digital identity, data exchange platforms, and real-time payments—are reshaping the financial ecosystem.

## Key Chapters

### Chapter 1: Foundations of Digital Public Infrastructure and Financial Market Systems
- Explores the theoretical foundations of DPI and its relationship to financial market architecture.
- Identifies core DPI components: digital identity, data exchange, and payment systems.

### Chapter 2: An Exploratory Taxonomy of Digital Public Infrastructure for Digital Financial Services
- Proposes a new taxonomy for classifying DPI systems used in financial services.
- Distinguishes between foundational DPI (digital ID, payments) and enabling DPI (data sharing, credit bureaus).

### Chapter 3: Global Landscape and Case Studies of Digital Public Infrastructure for Digital Financial Services
- Comparative analysis of DPI implementations across India, Brazil, UK, EU, and several EMDEs.
- Examines governance models: government-led, private-led, and hybrid approaches.

### Chapter 4: Emerging Technologies and Regulatory Challenges in Digital Public Infrastructure for Digital Financial Services
- Analyzes the role of AI, blockchain, and open APIs in shaping next-generation DPI.
- Identifies regulatory gaps and emerging risks, including consumer protection challenges.

### Chapter 5: Digital Public Infrastructure Principles for Financial Regulatory Authorities
- Proposes a set of principles for regulators overseeing DPI-based financial services.
- Emphasizes interoperability, inclusion, privacy, and consumer rights.

### Chapter 6: Cross-Regulatory Governance of Digital Public Infrastructure
- Examines the challenges of coordinating multiple regulatory bodies over interconnected DPI systems.
- Proposes governance frameworks for cross-border DPI interoperability.

## Implications for Consumer Protection

- **Data Governance**: DPI systems often aggregate large amounts of personal financial data, raising concerns about data privacy, consent, and secondary use.
- **Inclusion vs. Exclusion**: While DPI aims to increase financial inclusion, marginalized populations may be excluded if systems are not designed with accessibility in mind.
- **Interoperability Standards**: Lack of harmonized interoperability standards can create fragmentation that harms consumers through vendor lock-in and reduced portability.
- **Regulatory Coordination**: Cross-sectoral nature of DPI requires new models of regulatory coordination to prevent gaps in consumer protection.

## Methodology

The report combines literature review, comparative regulatory analysis, and case studies from 12+ countries, supplemented by expert interviews with policymakers, regulators, and industry stakeholders.
