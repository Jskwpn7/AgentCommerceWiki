# Mills Review Assessment: Consumer Protection Policy Analysis

**Agent AI and the Future of Retail Financial Services**
**Date:** 16 July 2026
**Role:** Consumer Protection Policy Specialist Assessment

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The Mills Review, published by the FCA on 6 July 2026, is the first globally-commissioned regulator review into the long-term impact of agentic AI on retail financial services. It offers a forward-looking framework (to 2030 and beyond) built around an AI autonomy spectrum (Operator → Collaborator → Consultant → Approver → Observer), four system-wide shifts, and seven priority recommendations.

This assessment evaluates the Review's strengths, weaknesses, and gaps from a consumer protection standpoint, and identifies specific areas where lobbying of the UK Government and FCA should be pursued to maximise consumer benefits, protections, and oversight of agentic AI in financial services.

**Key finding:** The Mills Review is a structurally strong, forward-looking document that correctly identifies emerging risks and proposes a coherent set of recommendations. However, it is fundamentally cautious, underestimates several material consumer protection gaps, and leaves critical enforcement mechanisms and redress frameworks largely unspecified. The Review is better at diagnosing problems than prescribing actionable consumer safeguards.

---

## 2. Strengths of the Mills Review

### 2.1 Proactive, Forward-Looking Framing

The Review's most significant strength is its willingness to look beyond current AI use cases and model the trajectory to 2030+. The AI autonomy spectrum (L1-L5: Operator → Collaborator → Consultant → Approver → Observer) provides a conceptual scaffold that helps regulators, firms, and consumers map how risk and accountability evolve as AI gains autonomy. No other regulatory body globally has produced equivalent forward-looking analysis.

### 2.2 Correct Identification of System-Wide Risks

The four system shifts identified are well-calibrated:
1. **Transformation of firms** — AI becoming core operational capability
2. **New consumer journeys** — Agent-led, delegated financial decision-making
3. **Reshaped competition** — Customer interface as source of market power
4. **Amplified threats and defences** — Fraud, cyber, and systemic risk escalation

The Review correctly identifies that system-wide risks (concentrated model providers, correlated AI behaviour, herding, shared infrastructure dependencies) cannot be addressed through firm-level controls alone.

### 2.3 Recognition of the Regulatory Perimeter Gap

Recommendation 1 — securing and adapting the regulatory perimeter — is the Review's most concrete consumer protection contribution. The Review correctly identifies that general-purpose LLMs (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) are increasingly shaping financial decisions from outside the regulatory perimeter. The 3–6 month perimeter review is a positive short-term commitment. The Review's recognition that AI-mediated interactions can resemble regulated advice or guidance — even when no regulated entity is involved — is a correct diagnosis.

### 2.4 Agentic Supervisory Model (Recommendation 6)

The proposal for an AI-enabled supervisory model that combines:
- Continuous data flows (rather than periodic reporting)
- Cross-firm aggregated monitoring of outcomes and pricing
- Horizon-scanning for emerging harms
- Agent-to-agent communication protocols

This is a genuinely innovative proposal that would, if implemented, significantly strengthen the FCA's ability to detect systemic consumer harm before it becomes widespread.

### 2.5 Public-Interest AI Capability Service (Recommendation 7)

The proposal to develop a free, trusted AI-enabled financial capability service — accessible regardless of income — addresses the risk of a two-tier system where only affluent consumers benefit from high-quality AI support. Working with MaPS and a broad partnership approach is sensible. This acknowledges the equity dimension of AI adoption that many reviews ignore.

### 2.6 Empirical Foundation

The Review commissioned a survey of 5,026 UK consumers (Yonder Consulting, April 2026) and received 140 stakeholder responses. The survey data is informative:
- 16% of consumers already use AI for personal finance
- 20% likely to use autonomous AI within pre-set goals
- 67% concerned about lack of protection if something goes wrong
- Only 26% trust ChatGPT to provide reliable financial information
- 68% concerned about misuse of personal/financial data

This empirical grounding strengthens the Review's credibility.

## 3. Weaknesses and Gaps

### 3.1 CRITICAL: No Redress Framework for AI-Mediated Harm

The single most significant gap is the absence of any concrete redress mechanism for consumers harmed by AI agents acting autonomously. The Review acknowledges the problem — "67% [of consumers] lack of protection if something goes wrong" — but proposes no specific framework for:

- **Who pays when an AI agent acts autonomously on a consumer's behalf and causes financial loss?** The Review states firms "remain accountable for outcomes" but does not address situations where:
  - The consumer's AI agent acts without firm involvement
  - General-purpose LLMs give personalised financial guidance outside the perimeter
  - Multiple interconnected AI systems across different providers produce correlated errors
  
- **Is there a no-fault compensation fund?** The Review does not address this.

- **What happens when a consumer's own AI agent (not firm-provided) makes a harmful decision?** The Review identifies this as a perimeter gap but offers no pathway to consumer redress.

**This is the Review's most consequential blind spot.** Without a clear redress framework, all other consumer protections are undermined.

### 3.2 Over-Reliance on "Fit for Purpose" Existing Regulation

The Review's foundational assertion — "the regulatory framework remains fit for purpose" — is dangerously complacent. While the Consumer Duty and principles-based approach are strong, the Review's own analysis demonstrates that:

- Decision-making is becoming distributed across systems outside firm control
- General-purpose LLMs operate without model risk management frameworks
- The activity-based perimeter is already under pressure from AI-mediated interactions that resemble regulated activities
- 71% of responding firms expressed strong negative sentiments about the absence of a data feedback loop for AI systems
- Consumer understanding and informed consent become progressively harder to evidence as AI autonomy increases

Saying existing frameworks are "fit for purpose" while simultaneously recommending seven significant adaptations (including new powers, new supervisory models, new coordination frameworks, and a new public-interest service) is internally inconsistent.

### 3.3 SMCR Accountability Gaps Unresolved

The Review acknowledges that evidencing accountability under the Senior Managers and Certification Regime becomes more complex as AI gains autonomy. However, it does not propose:

- Specific SMCR expectations for AI governance at the senior management level
- Whether a "Chief AI Officer" should be a SMF-15 equivalent
- How "reasonable steps" under SMCR should be demonstrated for AI-mediated outcomes
- What evidence firms should maintain for regulatory scrutiny of autonomous AI behaviour

The House of Commons Treasury Committee (January 2026) specifically recommended that the FCA publish clear practical guidance by end of 2026 on SMCR application to AI. The Mills Review does not fulfil this.

### 3.4 No Specific Guidance on Consumer Duty Application to AI

The Consumer Duty is cited as an example of the FCA's principles-based approach being "fit for purpose." However, the Review provides no specific guidance on:

- How firms should demonstrate "good outcomes" when AI systems dynamically personalise pricing, recommendations, or product features in real-time
- Whether "fair value" assessments need revision when AI-enabled efficiency gains are retained by firms rather than passed to consumers
- How "consumer understanding" is assessed when consumers are interacting with AI rather than directly with firms
- Whether firms need to monitor not just their own AI systems but the AI tools consumers use to access their products

This gap is significant because the Consumer Duty is the FCA's strongest consumer protection tool, and its application to AI-mediated contexts is uncertain.

### 3.5 Cognitive Erosion Not Addressed as Consumer Risk

The Glossary defines "cognitive erosion" — "a reduction over time in a person's knowledge, judgement, confidence or decision-making capability associated with sustained reliance on AI-enabled systems" — but this concept is never developed as a consumer protection concern. Sustained cognitive erosion could:

- Undermine informed consent across financial products
- Create dependency on AI for basic financial decisions
- Make consumers increasingly vulnerable to manipulation
- Reduce financial capability in the population generally

This is a genuine, emerging harm that deserves explicit attention.

### 3.6 Deceptive Design / Dark Patterns Under-Specified

The Glossary includes "deceptive design" but the Review does not address how AI-generated interfaces, chatbots, or agent interactions could deploy dark patterns at scale. AI can now personalise persuasive messaging to individual consumers at levels previously impossible. The Review does not address:

- Whether AI-generated product comparisons could embed biased recommendations through interface design
- How deceptive design scales when AI personalises the deception
- Whether existing rules on misleading communications are sufficient for AI-generated content

### 3.7 Upstream Concentration Risk Under-Addressed

The Review identifies reliance on a small number of model providers and cloud platforms as a concern. Stakeholder responses note that around 41% of technology providers flagged this risk. However:

- No specific concentration thresholds or limits are proposed
- No requirement for firms to diversify AI model providers
- No "anti-hoarding" provisions for critical AI infrastructure
- The link between upstream concentration and systemic consumer risk is stated but not operationalised

### 3.8 Insurance Risk Pooling / Cross-Subsidisation Threat

The Review's engagement responses note that "more granular data and personalised pricing enabled by AI could weaken cross-subsidisation between consumer groups, potentially improving accuracy and efficiency but reducing affordability or access for higher-risk consumers." The Review acknowledges this but does not propose any consumer protection safeguard for the erosion of traditional risk pooling — a significant gap for financial inclusion.

### 3.9 Data Feedback Loop Gap

71% of responding firms expressed strong negative sentiment about the absence of a data feedback loop for AI competition. This is a systemic market failure: when firms cannot share outcome data about AI system performance (for competition and improvement purposes), the market becomes less transparent, consumers get worse outcomes, and regulators are blind to emerging problems. The Review does not mandate a data feedback loop.

### 3.10 "Consider" Language Undermines Actionability

The seven recommendations are peppered with "the FCA should **consider**..." language. For a document that is meant to drive regulatory action, this weakens its force. Consumers and industry need certainty, not "consideration." This is particularly concerning for:

- Recommendation 1: "should consider securing and adapting" (then proposes a review)
- Recommendation 4: "should consider scaling up the AI Lab"
- Recommendation 7: "should consider convening the development"

The gap between "consider" and "mandate" is where consumer protection is lost.

## 4. Lobbying Priorities: UK Government

### 4.1 Expand FCA Powers Under the Designated Activities Regime (DAR) and Digital Markets, Competition and Consumer Act (DMCCA)

**Lobby target:** HM Treasury (DAR powers) + FCA + CMA (DMCCA direct powers)

**Action:** Advocate for the Government to grant the FCA direct powers under the Designated Activities Regime (currently restricted to systemic/prudential issues) and direct DMCCA powers under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumer Act 2024.

**Rationale:** The Mills Review itself recommends this (long-term recommendation in Rec 1). Currently, the FCA can recommend activities to HM Treasury for designation, but cannot act directly. Given that general-purpose AI platforms (Google, Apple, Microsoft) are creating financially-equivalent services outside the FCA's perimeter, direct powers are essential.

**Specific asks:**
- Extend DAR to cover AI-mediated financial guidance that materially influences consumer financial decisions
- Grant the FCA direct DMCCA powers over "strategic market status" firms that control consumer AI interfaces affecting financial outcomes
- Require AI platforms providing financial-adjacent services to register with the FCA

### 4.2 Establish an AI Redress and Compensation Framework for Autonomous AI Actions

**Lobby target:** HM Treasury (financial services legislation) + BEIS

**Action:** Commission a legislative review to establish a clear redress framework for consumers harmed by AI agents.

**Specific asks:**
- Create a statutory liability regime for autonomous AI financial actions (who is liable when a consumer's AI agent causes harm?)
- Consider a no-fault consumer compensation fund for AI-mediated harm (similar to the FSCS model but AI-specific)
- Require all financial services firms deploying agentic AI to maintain AI liability insurance
- Mandate that AI agents acting on consumer behalf carry identifiable liability chains

### 4.3 Require AI-Specific Consumer Duty Guidance

**Lobby target:** FCA (primary) + HM Treasury (statutory backing)

**Action:** Press the FCA to issue specific guidance on Consumer Duty application to AI by end of 2026, as already recommended by the Treasury Select Committee.

**Specific asks:**
- Guidance on demonstrating "good outcomes" when AI dynamically personalises recommendations/pricing
- Updated "consumer understanding" expectations for AI-mediated interactions
- "Fair value" assessment frameworks for AI-enabled services
- Prohibition on AI systems that use personalised persuasive messaging to drive consumer behaviour (anti-dark-pattern rules)

### 4.4 Mandate AI Data Feedback Loops and Outcome Transparency

**Lobby target:** BEIS + DSIT + FCA

**Action:** Advocate for statutory requirements for AI outcome data sharing in financial services.

**Specific asks:**
- Mandate that regulated firms report AI system outcomes (including error rates, consumer harm incidents, bias detection results) to the FCA
- Create a sector-wide AI outcome data sharing framework (addressing the 71% firm concern about no feedback loop)
- Require AI model providers serving financial services to register and report to the FCA/PRA

### 4.5 Financial Capability Act: Public-Interest AI Service

**Lobby target:** HM Treasury + BEIS + DSIT + MaPS

**Action:** Advocate for legislative backing of a sovereign public-interest AI financial capability service.

**Specific asks:**
- Fund and mandate a MaPS-led public-interest AI financial capability service
- Require government departments to use only this service (or equivalent) for consumer-facing financial guidance
- Ensure the service is free, impartial, and not commercially influenced

### 4.6 AI Stress Testing and AI-Resilience Requirements

**Lobby target:** Bank of England (financial stability) + FCA + PRA

**Action:** Advocate for mandatory AI stress testing for financial services firms.

**Specific asks:**
- Require firms to conduct AI stress tests (similar to operational resilience tests)
- Establish AI-specific resilience standards for critical AI dependencies
- Mandate AI incident reporting to the FCA/Bank

## 5. Lobbying Priorities: FCA

### 5.1 Publish SMCR + AI Accountability Guidance (by end of 2026)

**Action:** The Treasury Committee already requested this. Apply pressure for delivery.

**Specific asks:**
- Named senior manager accountability for AI systems
- "Reasonable steps" evidence requirements for AI-mediated outcomes
- AI governance as a senior management function (new SMF?)
- Audit trail requirements at the interaction level

### 5.2 Accelerate the Perimeter Review (3–6 months is acceptable but should begin immediately)

**Action:** Ensure the perimeter review starts immediately and includes:
- Consumer use of general-purpose LLMs for financial decisions
- AI-generated investment recommendations that fall outside "advice" but functionally resemble it
- Paid placement, preferential routing, and commercial bias in AI-powered product comparisons

**Specific asks:**
- Explicitly examine how "by way of business" tests apply to general-purpose AI tools
- Clarify whether AI-mediated financial promotions require FCA approval
- Assess whether the advice/guidance boundary (PERG 8) needs updating for conversational AI interfaces

### 5.3 Scale the AI Lab with Consumer Protection Mandate

**Action:** The FCA's AI Lab should be scaled with explicit consumer protection objectives, not just innovation support.

**Specific asks:**
- Consumer testing of AI agent behaviour (red teaming) as a standard AI Lab activity
- Consumer-facing AI safety standards developed through the AI Lab
- Independent evaluation of consumer outcomes from AI deployments

### 5.4 Implement the Agentic Supervisory Model with Consumer Data Access

**Action:** Support development of the Agentic Supervisory Model but ensure it includes:
- Real-time consumer harm detection (not just lagging indicators)
- Consumer complaints data integrated with AI outcome monitoring
- Cross-firm pattern detection for emerging harm types

**Specific asks:**
- Require firms to provide structured, machine-readable data on AI-driven outcomes (including Consumer Duty metrics)
- Develop agent-to-agent protocols for consumer redress reporting
- Ensure supervisory tolerances don't inadvertently discourage responsible innovation

### 5.5 Enforce Consumer Protection in AI-Mediated Consumer Journeys

**Action:** Use existing powers (Consumer Duty, SMCR, enforcement) to send clear signals that:
- Firms remain fully accountable for consumer outcomes regardless of AI autonomy level
- Deceptive design in AI interfaces is enforceable under existing rules
- AI-generated personalised communications must comply with financial promotions rules

### 5.6 Develop AI Governance Standards for Regulated Firms

**Action:** Issue AI governance expectations that go beyond generic model risk management:
- End-to-end AI lifecycle controls (not just point-in-time validation)
- Continuous monitoring requirements for models that update dynamically
- Third-party AI provider due diligence expectations
- Explainability and intelligibility standards (distinguishing technical explainability from consumer intelligibility — the Review makes this distinction)

### 5.7 Strengthen DRCF Coordination on AI Consumer Protection

**Action:** Work with the ICO, CMA, and Ofcom through the Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum on:
- AI-enabled impacts on consumer redress and complaints
- Platform power and data concentration in AI-enabled consumer interfaces
- Vulnerable consumer protections in AI-mediated financial services
- Coordinated enforcement approaches for AI-related consumer harm

| **Two-tier AI access** | Rec 7 addresses via public-interest service | Vague "consider" language |

## 7. Strategic Recommendations Summary

### Immediate (0–6 months)
1. **Begin the perimeter review now** — every month of delay allows more unregulated AI-mediated financial activity
2. **Press Treasury Committee to hold FCA accountable** for the promised SMCR + AI guidance by end of 2026
3. **Commission redress framework review** — this is the single most important consumer protection gap

### Short-term (6–18 months)
4. **Establish the public-interest AI financial capability service** through MaPS partnership
5. **Mandate AI outcome data reporting** by regulated firms
6. **Launch AI stress testing** for systemic financial services firms

### Medium-term (18–36 months)
7. **Implement the Agentic Supervisory Model** with real-time consumer harm detection
8. **Grant FCA direct DMCCA powers** over strategic AI platform firms
9. **Establish AI liability/compensation framework** by statute

### Ongoing
10. **Monitor cognitive erosion** — treat as an emerging consumer protection concern requiring longitudinal study
11. **Track AI-mediated deceptive design** — develop detection capability for AI-generated dark patterns
12. **Maintain DRCF coordination** — AI consumer protection risks span FCA, ICO, CMA, and Ofcom remits
