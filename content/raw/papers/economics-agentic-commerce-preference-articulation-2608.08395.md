From Product Search to Preference Articulation: The Economics of Agentic Commerce

arXiv:2608.08395 (econ.TH / cs.AI / cs.GT), 9 August 2026.

Authors: Lingxiu Dong¹, Kaiwen Luo¹, Fasheng Xu²
¹Olin Business School, Washington University in St. Louis
²School of Business, University of Connecticut

## Abstract

Generative AI is moving digital commerce from browsing toward agentic search, in which consumers delegate product discovery to AI agents. We model manual search, which accurately evaluates a limited product set, against agentic search, which screens a broad catalog using noisy representations of preferences and products. Preference complexity measures the number of satisfaction-relevant dimensions that are difficult to articulate before search but readily evaluated upon inspection. Consumers hold a finite attention budget and choose both a search regime and the intensity with which to use it: the number of products inspected under manual search and the depth of preference refinement under agentic search. We obtain three main findings:

1. **Complexity collapse of manual search:** As preference complexity grows, manual search eventually collapses — inspection ceases, mismatch returns to its no-search level, and platform revenue falls to zero. Agentic search avoids this finite complexity collapse. Complexity raises the marginal value of refinement, so once refinement becomes worthwhile, it remains worthwhile at every higher level. Articulation effort and mismatch may increase, but mismatch remains below the no-search level and revenue remains positive.

2. **Platform vs consumer incentives misalignment:** The platform ranks search regimes by conversion revenue, whereas the consumer also bears search expenditure. When manual inspection is sufficiently inexpensive, agentic search becomes revenue-superior before it becomes privately optimal for the consumer — creating a divergence between platform interests and consumer welfare.

## Key Concepts

**Preference Complexity:** The number of satisfaction-relevant dimensions that are difficult to articulate before search but readily evaluated upon inspection (e.g., fit, feel, aesthetics in fashion).

**Manual Search vs Agentic Search:**
- Manual: accurate but limited evaluation of small product sets; collapses under high preference complexity
- Agentic: noisy but broad screening of large catalogs with continuous preference refinement

**Revenue Superiority:** The point at which agentic search generates more platform revenue than manual — can occur before it becomes privately optimal for the consumer.

## Relevance to Agentic Commerce Wiki

This is a foundational economics paper directly modeling agentic commerce's consumer welfare implications. It provides rigorous theoretical evidence that platforms may promote agentic search because of conversion revenue gains even when it harms consumers, and that preference complexity fundamentally changes the search equilibrium — with significant consumer protection implications for how regulators should evaluate AI-mediated shopping platforms.
