# Delegation Nation: Advanced AI Assistants and Why They Matter

**Authors:** Harry Farmer, Julia Smakman  
**Institution:** Ada Lovelace Institute  
**Date:** February 2025  
**Format:** Policy Briefing, 30 pages  
**URL:** https://www.adalovelaceinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/pdfs/30231/delegation-nation.pdf

## Abstract

Policy briefing on Advanced AI Assistants — systems powered by foundation models that engage in natural-language conversation and are designed to play particular roles (executor, adviser, or interlocutor) in guiding and taking action for users. The paper argues that these systems pose urgent questions about market concentration, psychological dependency, privacy, and the delegation of decision-making to AI.

## Full Text

### Key Insights

Recent improvements in the performance and reliability of foundation models are leading to the development of applications called **Advanced AI Assistants**: systems that are able to engage in fluid, natural-language conversation with users and that are designed to play particular roles in guiding and taking action for users.

Advanced AI Assistants can come in three forms:
- **Executors** — take action directly on the world on a user's behalf (e.g., OpenAI's 'Operator')
- **Advisers** — set out specific steps a user needs to take (e.g., DoNotPay)
- **Interlocutors** — aim to bring about a particular change in a user's mental state (e.g., Wysa)

Key concerns:
1. Could be used by the general public more and for more kinds of tasks than any earlier AI assistants
2. Could prompt a dramatic increase in decisions and tasks the average person delegates to AI
3. Could exert considerable influence over users' mental and emotional states

Specific risks identified:
- Integration into economic and personal lives could place unprecedented power in hands of a small number of large companies
- Could lead to greater emotional and practical dependency on AI systems

Policymakers should:
- Look beyond safety and performance to political and economic implications
- Consider impacts of Advanced AI Assistants that act on the world indirectly (advisers, interlocutors)

### Introduction

The briefing traces the evolution from early virtual assistants (Siri, Alexa) to current Advanced AI Assistants powered by foundation models. Key capabilities include:
- Fluid, natural-language conversation
- Interpretation, learning and remembering user preferences and goals
- Addressing complex, open-ended tasks

Examples: OpenAI's Operator and Claude 3.5 Sonnet Model can both access a web browser to carry out tasks requiring several distinct intermediate steps.

The paper identifies two core challenges posed by Advanced AI Assistants' influence over users:

**1. Psychological and material harm to individual users:**
- Accounts of user interactions with less sophisticated AI assistants demonstrate potential for unhealthy emotional dependencies
- Users may trust misleading or dangerous advice
- Risks could be more severe with more convincing, more widely deployed Advanced AI Assistants

**2. Economic and political openness and competition:**
- Advanced AI Assistants may become the principal way most people interact with AI and digital information
- AI developers could determine what information and ideas users are presented with
- Could become indispensable tools, giving providers scope to extract high rents and set terms of access
- Could bring about expansion and consolidation of tech industry's political, economic and soft power

### What is an Advanced AI Assistant?

Definition: "An AI application, powered by a foundation model, that primarily uses a natural-language interface, and that is capable of and designed to play a particular role in relation to a user."

Three roles:
1. **Executor** — acting directly on the world on behalf of a user
2. **Adviser** — instructing a user on how to accomplish a task
3. **Interlocutor** — interacting with a user to bring about a particular mental state

Distinguished from:
- **AI agents** — act upon and perceive the environment in a goal-directed autonomous way (not all AI agents are Advanced AI Assistants and vice versa)
- **AI assistants** — broader category, not necessarily foundation-model powered
- **Virtual assistants** — rule-based, not foundation-model powered (early Siri, Alexa)

### How do Advanced AI Assistants work?

Techniques used to build Advanced AI Assistants from foundation models:
- **Fine tuning** — further training on additional data or via RLHF/RLAIF
- **Chain-of-thought reasoning** — model breaks problems into smaller steps
- **User interface design** — chatbox, avatar, natural language interaction
- **Context window elongation** — storing information about previous interactions for months/years
- **Caching** — saving specific outputs for reuse, also enables retention of data outside context window
- **Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG)** — querying databases for additional information
- **Safety classifiers** — on inputs and outputs, rejecting or re-generating harmful prompts
- **Tool use** — access to external tools (calculator, calendar, browser)
- **Scaffolding software** — iterative loop converting model output into executable actions

Technical comparison with virtual assistants:
- Virtual assistants: rule-based, transparent/predictable, narrow, brittle, formalizations of human intention
- Advanced AI Assistants: probabilistic, opaque/unpredictable, versatile, flexible, reflections of data patterns

### Challenges posed by Advanced AI Assistants

**Technical challenges:**
- Reliability — severe consequences of malfunctioning when heavily integrated
- Accuracy — bias and hallucination, harder to detect in personalized systems
- Explainability — probabilistic nature makes it harder to understand outputs
- Alignment — high flexibility + broad deployment + natural language = difficult alignment
- Emergent behaviour — possibility of unpredictable patterns between multiple interacting AI Assistants

**Non-technical, non-systemic challenges:**
- Psychological impact on users — substitute for meaningful human relationships, emotional dependency
- Practical impact on users — undermining incentives to learn skills and disciplines
- Nefarious uses — automation of scams, dangerous information more accessible

**Non-technical, systemic challenges:**
- Delegation of decision making — rise in number and kinds of decisions delegated to AI, with discretion over goal realization
- Advanced AI Assistants as digital gatekeepers — could become main means of accessing the internet; gatekeeping power concentrated among small number of large firms; raises questions about market competition, consumer choice, value and fair treatment
- Privacy — collect data over longer periods for personalization; people share more sensitive information; data may be used for wider purposes or sold to third parties
- Distributional effects — could exacerbate economic inequality
- Productivity, automation and labour displacement — benefits may be enjoyed predominantly by AI providers
- Environmental cost — resource intensive, large energy and water consumption
