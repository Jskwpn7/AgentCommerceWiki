AI is the Strategy: From Agentic AI to Autonomous Business Models onto Strategy in the Age of AI

arXiv:2506.17339 (cs.CY / econ.GN), 24 June 2025. 17 pages, 6 figures, 3 tables. Under review at Strategy Science.

Authors: René Bohnsack* & Mickie de Wet
*Corresponding author: r.bohnsack@ucp.pt

## Abstract

This article develops the concept of Autonomous Business Models (ABMs) as a distinct managerial and strategic logic in the age of agentic AI. While most firms still operate within human-driven or AI-augmented models, the authors argue that we are now entering a phase where agentic AI (systems capable of initiating, coordinating, and adapting actions autonomously) can increasingly execute the core mechanisms of value creation, delivery, and capture. This shift reframes AI not as a tool to support strategy, but as the strategy itself. Using two illustrative cases — getswan.ai, an Israeli startup pursuing autonomy by design, and a hypothetical reconfiguration of Ryanair as an AI-driven incumbent — the article depicts the evolution from augmented to autonomous business models. It shows how ABMs reshape competitive advantage through agentic execution, continuous adaptation, and the gradual offloading of human decision-making. The transition introduces new forms of competition between AI-led firms, termed "synthetic competition," where strategic interactions occur at rapid, machine-level speed and scale.

## Key Concepts

**Autonomous Business Models (ABMs):** Business models in which agentic AI systems are the primary agents executing the firm's value creation, delivery, and capture logic, with minimal ongoing human intervention.

**Three core mechanisms of ABMs:**
1. **Agentic execution of core logic** — AI systems execute rather than merely support strategy decisions
2. **Minimal human intervention** — Human oversight becomes exception-based rather than routine
3. **Adaptive decision cycles** — Continuous sensing → deciding → learning loops without constant human direction

**Synthetic Competition:** A new form of competition where autonomous AI agents operate at ultra-fast speeds, learning and competing without direct human control — strategic interactions occur at machine-level speed and scale.

## Illustrative Cases

### Swan AI (getswan.ai) — Startup Native to Autonomy
- Israeli startup that aims to reach $30M ARR without hiring additional human employees
- Plans to scale by deploying an internal network of 20+ AI agents
- Offers AI sales agents that autonomously identify prospects, draft personalized outreach, follow up, and schedule meetings
- Once clients set initial parameters (target profile, campaign goals, messaging templates), the AI handles the entire lead generation and qualification funnel
- Cost structure dominated by computing resources and AI development rather than linear hiring
- Approaches autonomy at the edge: core operational processes are autonomous; humans handle quality assurance and business development

### Ryanair — Hypothetical Incumbent Evolution
- Analysis of how Europe's largest low-cost airline currently uses AI to augment (not replace) decision-making
- Current AI applications: dynamic pricing, yield management, route optimization, predictive maintenance, personalization algorithms, chatbot customer service
- Proposes a future "AI Factory" concept where Ryanair's AI would continuously ingest data and autonomously orchestrate fleet management, dynamic pricing, inventory, marketing — shifting management role to setting guardrails and governance principles

## Theoretical Framework

**Strategy vs Business Model vs Tactics:** Builds on Casadesus-Masanell and Ricart (2010) framework:
- Strategy = choosing a way to compete (high-level vision)
- Business model = the logic of value creation/capture mechanisms
- Tactics = actions within the model's bounds to improve performance

The paper argues that in ABMs, AI moves from executing tactics → operating business models → potentially adapting strategy elements themselves.

## Relevance to Agentic Commerce Wiki

This paper is directly relevant to understanding the business-model-level implications of agentic commerce — it establishes the theoretical foundation for when "AI becomes the strategy" rather than a tool. The concept of synthetic competition has significant consumer protection implications: if firms compete at machine speed using autonomous agents, human consumers may be unable to exercise meaningful choice or comparison.
