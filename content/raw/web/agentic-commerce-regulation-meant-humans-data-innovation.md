# Agentic Commerce is Coming, but Regulation Meant for Humans Will Slow It Down

Center for Data Innovation | By Eli Clemens | March 2026

## Content

**Core thesis:** Agentic commerce is poised to transform shopping and procurement, but existing laws built on assumptions about human-operated systems could slow adoption. Policymakers should update rules written for human-initiated transactions.

## Key Regulatory Gaps Identified

### Consumer Side: Regulation E
- Federal rule regulating consumers' right to dispute erroneous electronic fund transfers provides no clear framework for agentic commerce disputes
- If AI agent violates consumer instructions (ordering wrong item/quantity), Regulation E would leave consumers without dispute rights
- If AI agent purchases a product listed at an artificially high price that a human would recognize as obvious error, dispute resolution unclear

### CFPB Actions
- August 2025: CFPB sought comment on advance notice of proposed rulemaking on personal financial data rights regarding who can serve as "representative" acting on consumer's behalf
- Should clarify that consumer-authorized agents do not waive all error resolution rights
- Without regulatory clarity, businesses will build uncertainty into pricing and product design

### Enterprise Side: SOX Compliance
- Regulated industries (healthcare, food/beverage, financial services) operate under procurement requirements assuming human authorization
- Sarbanes-Oxley Act Section 302 requires executives to personally certify internal controls effectiveness
- Unclear whether AI agent operating parameters satisfy SOX requirements
- AI agents produce logs that look very different from traditional approval records
- No framework for evaluating model parameters and automated outputs as evidence of sufficient internal control

### Industry Position
Jodie Kelley (CEO, Electronic Transactions Association) testified to House Financial Services Committee January 2026: "Many existing principles—authorization, consent, liability, auditability—apply in the agentic context." Existing laws should largely apply; courts will set precedents.

## Policy Recommendation
Regulators should update rules written for human-initiated transactions to reflect the realities of the agentic age before unresolved policy questions slow momentum.
