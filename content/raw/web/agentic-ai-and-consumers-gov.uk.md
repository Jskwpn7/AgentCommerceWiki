# Agentic AI and consumers

UK Competition and Markets Authority (CMA)
Published 9 March 2026
Open Government Licence v3.0

## Full Document Content

The CMA's definitive analysis of agentic AI's impact on UK consumers. Covers:

### Executive Summary

**Current state:** AI embedded in search, recommendations, fraud detection, customer service and decision-support tools. Most consumer-facing AI operates as a tool — supports decisions while coordination, monitoring and action remain with the user.

**Potential future state:** Agentic AI could drive a step change. Definitions include AI agents instructed in natural language to achieve goals autonomously — navigating complexity, planning, coordinating, taking actions across multiple services. Agents sense (perceive environment), decide and act. They can:
- Assess goals, break into subtasks, plan end-to-end workflows
- Retrieve real-time data from other agents, databases and services
- Execute actions autonomously, including making payments on behalf of user
- Store memory of past interactions to improve over time

**Benefits:** Reduced friction, improved personalisation, potentially lower prices and tailored deals. Save people time, reduce cognitive load. Help vulnerable consumers participate in markets more effectively. New opportunities for UK businesses to bring agentic apps to market.

**Risks:** Greater autonomy increases consequences of errors, heightens risks of manipulation and loss of consumer agency, could lead to worse outcomes. People steered toward products that are more profitable but less suited to needs, paying higher prices. Questions about transparency, incentives, accountability. Undermine trust in AI and consumer markets.

**Direction of travel:** Technology at early stage. Most implementations bounded and cautious. Interest and investment risen sharply. Progress depends on real-world performance and sustained confidence.

**Application of consumer law:** UK consumer law applies whether decisions are made by people or AI. CMA's foundation model principles (transparency, accountability) remain directly relevant. Published guidance to help businesses using agentic AI comply with consumer law. Wider enablers needed: smart data schemes, secure digital identity, strong interoperability standards.

### Section: From Tools to Agents

AI has increasingly become part of everyday life — communication, information access, decision-making. Foundation models and generative AI brought AI into the foreground. ChatGPT adopted rapidly by millions. But AI remains reactive for most consumers: search engines retrieve, recommendation systems suggest, chat assistants respond — but leave burden of decision-making with the user.

Interest spiked in agentic AI driven by advances in foundation models, falling costs, and evidence that AI can plan and execute multi-step tasks. The concept: agents given higher-level objectives to pursue over time, breaking goals into steps, adapting to changing circumstances, acting across multiple services — including initiating actions and executing transactions.

What makes AI "agentic" is not a single technical feature but combination of capabilities:
- Degree of autonomy from continuous human supervision
- Goal-orientation (pursuing outcomes rather than isolated tasks)
- Multi-step reasoning across complex decisions
- Ability to act across systems, platforms and data sources

Unlike traditional automation executing predefined processes, agentic AI may make context-dependent judgements about when to act, seek confirmation, or balance competing objectives.

### Section: Agentic AI Today and Potential Future Opportunities

**Current deployments:** Bounded and controlled — customer operations, service, commerce and sales workflows, software/IT operations, internal business process automation. Consumer-facing authority remains limited; escalation to humans common. High-stakes fully autonomous contexts remain limited.

### Key Harms Identified (Executive Summary)

1. **Agent as unfaithful servant:** Agents executing on behalf of consumers may not faithfully represent consumer interests
2. **Errors and reliability:** Mistakes made by autonomous agents have greater consequences than human errors alone
3. **Bias and discrimination:** Agents trained on biased data perpetuate discriminatory outcomes at scale
4. **Loss of agency:** Consumers ceding decision-making to agents lose control over choices
5. **Agentic collusion in pricing:** Autonomous seller agents optimizing pricing may coordinate implicitly, dampening competition
6. **Lock-in and reduced choice:** Platform-specific agents create switching costs
7. **Data protection and privacy:** Agents access personal data across services, increasing exposure
8. **Transparency gaps:** Consumers don't understand how their agents make decisions

### Section: Compliance Requirements for Businesses

Businesses using agentic AI must comply with existing consumer protection law. The CMA's compliance guidance (published separately) covers:
- Robust training of systems
- Monitoring agent behaviour
- Appropriate human oversight
- Transparency about agent involvement
- Accountability structures

### Wider Enablers Needed

- Smart data schemes for interoperability
- Secure digital identity
- Strong interoperability standards enabling consumers to switch between agent systems
- Positioning the UK at forefront of trusted agentic innovation

## Notes

This is the CMA's authoritative position document on agentic AI and consumer protection. It explicitly states that UK consumer law applies to AI agent actions. It is the primary reference for all subsequent wiki pages covering agentic commerce harms in the UK context. The separate compliance guidance (Complying with Consumer Law When Using AI Agents) provides detailed business obligations.
