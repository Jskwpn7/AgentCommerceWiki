# Agentic AI and Consumer Protection (CERRE)

CERRE | Christoph Busch | 16 July 2026 | [Full paper](https://cerre.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/CERRE_CERRE_Agentic-AI-and-Consumer-Protection.pdf)

## Summary

EU-focused analysis asking whether consumer law — built on the implicit assumption that purchasers are human — remains fit for purpose when AI agents sit between consumer and trader. Three critical findings:

1. **Disclosure rules break down:** Built around limits of human attention; agents don't share these limits
2. **Liability gap:** When agent buys wrong product or authorizes unauthorized payment, existing rules say little about risk allocation between consumer, agent provider and trader — intersects with contract law, AI Act and product liability rules
3. **New harm: agent-specific dark patterns:** EU law protects consumers from dark patterns but offers no protection for agents acting on behalf of consumers (agent manipulation via prompt injection)

## Policy Recommendations (for Digital Fairness Act Q3 2026)
1. Clarify contract validity and consumer law applicability to agentic transactions
2. Move from compliance-by-design to compliance-by-protocol (embed duties in technical protocols)
3. Differentiate: agent requiring confirmation ≠ autonomous agent; marketplace-embedded ≠ independent

## Consumer Protection Significance
Directly supports the CMA's eight harm vectors with EU legal framework analysis. The "agent as unfaithful servant" and "dark patterns for agents" concepts are novel additions to the harm taxonomy.
