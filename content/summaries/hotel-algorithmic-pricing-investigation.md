# Hotel Algorithmic Pricing Investigation — Progress Update

**Date published:** 11 August 2026 (review update)
**Original investigation launched:** 26 February 2026
**Expected outcome:** H2 2026

## Overview

The CMA investigation into three hotel chains and their data analytics provider for suspected algorithm-enabled information sharing represents the UK's first major algorithmic collusion enforcement action. The investigation examines whether hotel pricing algorithms engaged in prohibited information sharing that facilitated coordinated pricing.

## Key Developments

### CMA Guidance Publication (4 March 2026)

The CMA published "AI and Collusion: Frontiers, Opportunities and Challenges" setting out four scenarios of algorithmic collusion:

1. **Algorithm as a messenger** — firms use pricing algorithms to communicate and implement collusive terms
2. **Algorithm as a tacit collusion facilitator** — algorithms enable tacit collusion through repeated interaction
3. **Algorithm as a collateral damage** — independent algorithm use leads to supracompetitive outcomes
4. **Algorithm as a common third party** — competitors use shared algorithm that facilitates coordination

### Leniency Policy Update

The CMA updated its leniency policy to cover conduct via shared algorithms. Updated leniency policy now available for "exchanging competitively sensitive information through a shared algorithm."

**Updated whistleblower reward:** up to £250,000 for tips on algorithmic collusion (see [[CMA Whistleblower Statistics]])

### International Context

- **US Third Circuit** (30 July 2026): Revived algorithmic pricing claims against casino-hotels
- **European enforcement**: Growing enforcement activity on algorithmic pricing across the EU

## Consumer Harm

- **Supracompetitive pricing:** Consumers pay higher prices due to algorithm-enabled coordination
- **Reduced transparency:** Consumers cannot distinguish between legitimate algorithmic pricing and collusive behaviour
- **Switching costs:** Hotel booking platforms and loyalty programmes increase switching barriers

## Regulatory Response

The investigation signals a shift from theoretical concern to active enforcement of algorithmic collusion. The CMA's guidance and leniency policy update provide businesses with clearer expectations about prohibited conduct.
