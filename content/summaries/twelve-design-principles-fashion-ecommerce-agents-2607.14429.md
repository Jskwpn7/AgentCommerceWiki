# From Product-Centred Retrieval to Experience-Led Commerce: Twelve Candidate Design Principles for Fashion E-Commerce User Experience

arXiv:2607.14429 (cs.HC), 15 July 2026 | Khan, Habiba & Khan | CC BY 4.0

## Summary

An HCI-focused paper proposing a transition from the traditional product-centric e-commerce model to an **experience-led commerce** paradigm, specifically for fashion retail. The paper identifies risks when AI search, recommendation, and generative assistance are deployed without proper design safeguards — including the collapse of consumer hard requirements into inferred preferences.

## Key Problem Statement

Traditional e-commerce assumes: product query → listing browsing → individual evaluation → checkout

But in fashion, **demand is increasingly formed elsewhere:**
- Creator and social-media content
- Short video and represented looks
- Upcoming situations and occasions
- Owned garments and personal style history
- Values and partially authored outfit compositions

## Identified Risks of AI-Driven Fashion Commerce

The paper identifies five specific risks when generative AI assists fashion shopping:

1. **Preference collapse:** Hard consumer requirements (size, fit, fabric, budget) get collapsed into inferred preferences by the AI system
2. **Commercial priority amplification:** AI systems may amplify platform commercial interests over genuine consumer needs
3. **Product-seller obscurity:** Consumers cannot verify whether a presented item actually corresponds to the current seller's product
4. **Feasibility gaps:** A seemingly suitable candidate is presented before stock and delivery feasibility are established
5. **Preference hijacking:** AI may reshape what the consumer wants rather than help them find what they actually want

## Theoretical Contribution: Experience-Led Commerce Design Framework

The paper proposes a transition from product-centred retrieval to experience-led commerce, grounded in HCI theory including:
- Embodied interaction design
- Context-aware recommendation systems
- Participatory design of AI interfaces
- Value-sensitive design principles

## Consumer Protection Implications

This paper is significant because it:
- Provides HCI-grounded evidence that agentic shopping assistants can actively distort consumer preferences rather than merely failing to find good matches
- Identifies the specific failure modes where AI assistance becomes preference manipulation
- Offers a design-framework approach to consumer protection (preventive by design vs. reactive after harm)
- Is relevant to CMA/OCA guidance on dark patterns and algorithmic manipulation in e-commerce
