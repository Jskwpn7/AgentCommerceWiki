# Agentic AI and consumers (UK CMA)

UK Competition and Markets Authority, published 9 March 2026 | [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/agentic-ai-and-consumers/agentic-ai-and-consumers)

## Summary

The CMA's definitive regulatory guidance on agentic AI's impact on consumers. Establishes that **UK consumer law applies whether decisions are made by people or AI agents**, and identifies eight major harm vectors specific to agent-mediated commerce.

## Key Findings

**From tools to agents:** The transition from reactive AI (search, recommendations, chat) to agentic AI (autonomous goal pursuit, multi-step reasoning, cross-platform action) represents a qualitative shift in consumer-AI interaction.

**Eight harm vectors identified:**
1. Agent as unfaithful servant (not representing consumer interests faithfully)
2. Errors/reliability (greater consequences for autonomous mistakes)
3. Bias/discrimination (scale amplification of training data biases)
4. Loss of agency (ceding decision-making to agents)
5. Agentic collusion in pricing (seller agent implicit coordination)
6. Lock-in and reduced choice (platform-specific ecosystem creation)
7. Data protection/privacy (cross-service data exposure)
8. Transparency gaps (consumers unaware of agent decision processes)

## Consumer Protection Significance

This is the primary UK regulatory framework document for agentic commerce. It explicitly confirms that existing consumer law applies to agent-mediated transactions, providing legal basis for DMCCA 2024 enforcement against platform design choices that harm consumers through AI agents.
