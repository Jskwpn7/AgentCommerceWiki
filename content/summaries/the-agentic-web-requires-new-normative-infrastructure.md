# The Agentic Web Requires New Normative Infrastructure

> **arXiv:** 2606.10711v1 [cs.CY] — 9 June 2026
> **Authors:** Cameron Pattison (Vanderbilt), Matthew Boulos (Imbue AI), Noam Kolt (Hebrew U), Changbai Li (Oregon State), Tiziano Piccardi (JHU), Seth Lazar (JHU)

## TL;DR

The agentic web — where users interact with the internet largely through AI agents acting on their behalf — is technically feasible but obstructed by **outdated laws, terms of service, and practices** that allow platforms to block and degrade agent access, often secretly. Platforms currently treat agents indiscriminately as "malicious bots," triggering an arms race between agent developers and platforms. The authors propose a **normative triad** (delegation, transparency, proportional restriction) and advocate for an open societal conversation about agent access rights before it's locked in privately.

## The Normative Triad

The paper proposes three principles for governing user-authorized agent access to online platforms:

| Principle | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Delegation** | Users entitled to access a service should ordinarily be able to exercise that access through user-authorized agents |
| **Transparency** | Platforms should disclose how they treat agents, and agents should identify their identity and purpose |
| **Proportional Restriction** | Platforms should restrict access only to address concrete harms by the least restrictive means |

## Key Arguments

1. **Technical bottleneck is resolved, normative bottleneck is not**: Significant advances in model capability mean agents can now undertake tasks previously infeasible. The fundamental bottleneck is whether digital intermediaries allow agents access, not whether they can perform tasks.

2. **Current regime is broken**: Existing terms of service, laws, and practices long predate AI agents and permit "practically unlimited discretion to block agent access." No distinction is drawn between "malicious bots" and user-authorized agents.

3. **Arms race burns societal value**: Platform-antagonist relationships have turned "hot" with lawsuits (referenced cases from N.D. California). Both sides expend resources on evasion and blocking that could be directed to value creation.

4. **Cold war between platforms and agent developers**: The current state is described as a subterranean struggle where developers use technical expertise to evade platform restrictions — a "cold war" that needs to become an open societal conversation.

5. **Consumer autonomy argument**: AI agents have the potential to "disintermediate walled gardens" and "significantly enhance users' autonomy" — consumers should be able to delegate online actions to AI agents on their behalf.

## Consumer Harm Implications

- **Platform lock-in**: When platforms block agents indiscriminately, consumers lose the ability to use tools that could save them time and money
- **Lack of transparency**: Platforms that block agents "in secret" prevent users from knowing their agents are being degraded
- **Liability gaps**: The paper raises but doesn't fully resolve liability questions when agents cause harm — is it the agent developer, the platform, or the user?
- **Consumer choice vs. platform control**: The core tension is between consumer autonomy (choosing to delegate actions to AI) and platform control (deciding who accesses their services)

## Relevance to UK Context

- UK platform governance (DSA compliance, CMA Digital Markets Unit) should address agent access norms
- The normative triad provides a framework for CMA regulation of major platforms' agent access policies
- Connects to [[regulatory-failure]] — current regulatory frameworks don't distinguish between bots and user-authorized agents
- Relevant to UK digital economy policy and the Online Safety Act's scope

## Limitations

- This is a normative/policy paper, not an empirical study
- The authors have a stated advocacy position (pro-agent access) which may underweight legitimate platform concerns
- Liability questions are acknowledged but not resolved in this paper
- The "cold war" characterization is the authors' framing, not a measured assessment
