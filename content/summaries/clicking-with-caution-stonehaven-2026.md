# Clicking with Caution: How Scam Adverts Are Damaging the Online Economy

> Commissioned by Revolut and Stonehaven. Published by Public First, June 2026.
> Full report: [PublicFirst_Revolut_Clicking_with_Caution_Report_3MB.pdf](https://assets.nationbuilder.com/stonehaven/pages/5143/attachments/original/1782980463/PublicFirst_Revolut_Clicking_with_Caution_Report_3MB.pdf?1782980463)

## Executive Summary

This report examines the growth of online scam advertising, its impact on consumers and businesses, and the British public's preferences for systemic protection measures.

### Key Findings

**01. Scams are now commonplace online**

- **51%** of UK adults see scam adverts online "very often" or "fairly often"
- **1 in 4** UK adults have lost money after clicking a scam advert (≈13.5 million people)
- **65%** say social media is where they most commonly see online scams
- Scams have moved from "occasional nuisance" to "everyday risk"

**02. AI is making scams harder to detect**

- **80%** think scam adverts using AI-generated content have increased over the past year
- **56%** have encountered a scam advert they believe used AI or deepfake content
- **78%** see content they believe may have been AI-generated at least some of the time
- **71%** say AI-generated content is more convincing and harder to spot
- AI provides "glossy, polished" scams that anyone can create

**03. Scams have damaging implications for the online economy**

- **75%** say scam adverts make them less trusting of online advertising overall
- **72%** say online scams have made them more cautious about spending money online
- **61%** say they have reduced their online spending because of scam adverts
- Smaller businesses particularly harmed — they rely on cost-effective targeted advertising
- Consumer trust erosion damages legitimate businesses, not just scam operators

**04. The public want stronger protection**

Top priorities for action:
1. **Faster removal** of identified scam adverts (49%)
2. **Stronger identity checks** for accounts placing adverts (47%)
3. **Verification before ads go live** (47%)
4. **Financial penalties** for businesses that don't prevent scam adverts (41%)
5. **Better reporting mechanisms** for consumers (40%)

Cross-party support for verification exists across Con, Lab, Lib Dem, Green, Reform, SNP, and SNP voters.

## Methodology

- **Poll**: 2,008 online UK adults, fieldwork 29 May – 1 June 2026
- **Weighting**: Interlocking age & gender, region, and social grade
- **Focus groups**: 7 consumers and 7 business owners (28 May 2026)
- **Advert identification test**: Showed respondents genuine Revolut adverts alongside fake AI-generated versions
- Public First is a member of the British Polling Council (BPC) and Market Research Society

## Consumer Research: Can People Spot Scam Ads?

### Advert Identification Test

The study tested public ability to distinguish genuine from scam adverts using Revolut-branded material:

| Advert Type | Correctly Identified | Incorrectly Flagged |
|-------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Genuine Revolut adverts | 60% | 55% said at least one genuine advert was a scam |
| Fake AI-generated scams | 46% | 39% incorrectly identified real advert as scam |

**Average correct identification: 5 out of 8 adverts**

The public can identify scam adverts "up to a point" — but not reliably, especially as AI quality improves.

### Key Consumer Behaviour Insights

- Consumers increasingly "treat the online marketplace with more suspicion"
- The effect extends beyond those who lose money — everyone now approaches online shopping more cautiously
- **Smaller businesses disproportionately harmed**: they rely on targeted, cost-effective online advertising, which scam adverts are eroding
- Reimbursement of APP fraud "treats the symptoms, not the cause"
- Banks/payment providers can only act at the "final stage" — initial scam exposure occurs on platforms "outside the banking system"

## Public Policy Recommendations

### What the Public Wants

The research reveals four clear priorities:

1. **Regulators should set clear standards and enforce them** at the speed online advertising moves
2. **Companies selling ad space should strengthen checks** on who places adverts
3. **Platforms should improve detection** of scam adverts and removal speed once reported
4. **Coordinated system-wide response** across all actors

### Responsibility Allocation

The public assigns "a great deal of responsibility" to:
- **Platforms** (for detecting/removing scam adverts)
- **Regulators** (for setting standards and enforcing)
- **Banks/payment providers** (for reimbursing victims — but this is "treats the symptoms")
- **Advertisers/ad networks** (for verifying who places adverts)
- **Celebrities/influencers** (who promote scam products)
- **Government/law enforcement** (for broader action)

Cross-party political consensus exists across all major parties for verification and enforcement measures.

## Broader Context

### APP Reimbursement Rules

- Since October 2024, UK banks must reimburse APP scam victims within 5 business days, up to £85,000
- This is "unique to the UK"
- However, it operates at the "final stage of the fraud lifecycle"
- The initial exposure typically occurs on digital platforms, outside banking regulation
- UK Finance revealed APP fraud losses continue to grow despite reimbursement rules

### AI-Generated Content as an Enabler

- AI tools are now "routinely" available for creating convincing scam content
- Images, videos, voices, and deepfakes are increasingly "realistic, polished, and plausible"
- 84% of UK adults encounter AI-generated content regularly online
- Only 2% say they never see AI-generated content online
- The barrier to creating scam content has been dramatically lowered

### Implications for Legitimate Business

Scam advertising doesn't just harm consumers — it harms the businesses that legitimately use online advertising:

- **Trust erosion**: 75% trust online advertising less overall
- **Spending caution**: 72% more cautious about online spending
- **Reach degradation**: Legitimate businesses face higher skepticism and lower conversion
- **SME impact**: Smaller businesses are particularly vulnerable due to reliance on cost-effective targeted advertising
- **Competitive distortion**: Scammers free-ride on advertising infrastructure legitimate businesses pay for

## Consumer Harm Analysis

This report identifies several consumer harm vectors relevant to UK consumer protection frameworks:

| Harm Type | Description | UK Regulatory Framework |
|-----------|-------------|------------------------|
| Deceptive advertising | Scam adverts designed to mislead consumers | Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 (CPRs) |
| Platform liability | Platforms host scam content with limited proactive removal obligations | Digital Services Act (EU), UK Online Safety Act |
| Financial harm | £X billion in APP fraud losses annually; 13.5M adults affected | UK Finance APP reimbursement rules, FCA regulation |
| Trust erosion | Systemic loss of confidence in online commerce | Consumer Rights Act 2015 |
| AI-enabled fraud | Deepfake/scam content at scale lowers barrier to entry for fraudsters | AI Act (EU), UK AI governance framework (developing) |
| SME exclusion | Legitimate small businesses harmed by trust environment | Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) |
