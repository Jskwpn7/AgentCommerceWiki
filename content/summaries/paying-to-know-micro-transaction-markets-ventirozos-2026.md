# Paying to Know: Micro-Transaction Markets for Verified Product Information in Agentic E-Commerce

**Source:** arXiv:2606.24783v1 (cs.CL, cs.AI) | 8 pages, 1 figure | Vision paper, under review
**Authors:** Filippos Ventirozos, Matthew Shardlow (Manchester Metropolitan University)

## Abstract

Vision paper proposing agentic e-commerce as a micro-transaction market for verified product information. Proposes buyer agents spending fractions of a cent to unlock seller histories, third-party test reports, bills of materials (BOMs), and audited sales metrics under a freemium model. References Google's AP2 and x402 micropayments as enablers.

## Key Concepts

- **Micro-transaction gated information:** Verified product information (seller histories, test reports, BOMs, sales metrics) gated behind sub-cent payments — buyer agents pay sellers directly
- **Freemium trust scoring:** Reviewer trust scored reputationally by agents introduces new manipulation vectors
- **Machine-speed data extraction:** Sub-cent payments at machine speed enable hyper-granular data extraction from seller sites without human consent or awareness
- **Affiliate/bias manipulation via micro-transaction economics:** Sellers can buy better discoverability from buyer agents at scale

## Consumer Harm Signals

1. **Information asymmetry at scale:** Verified information gated behind micropayments creates a pay-to-know environment where price-sensitive consumers are disadvantaged
2. **Freemium trust scoring:** Reviewer trust scored reputationally by agents introduces new manipulation vectors
3. **Sub-cent payments at machine speed:** Enables hyper-granular data extraction from seller sites without human consent or awareness
4. **New attack surface for affiliate/bias manipulation:** Micro-transaction economics incentivize sellers to "buy" better discoverability from buyer agents
