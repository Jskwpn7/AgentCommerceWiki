# Agentic Inequality

arXiv:2510.16853 (cs.CY), 24 April 2026 (v3). Oxford Martin AI Governance Initiative / Access Partnership / University of Oxford.

## Summary

Defines and conceptualises "agentic inequality": the emerging dimension of social stratification driven by disparities in access to, capability of, and capacity for deployment of autonomous AI agents. Goes beyond the standard "AI divide" (which focuses on access to generative tools) to examine how **agent autonomy itself creates new asymmetries** — those who can field sophisticated agent swarms gain structural advantages over individuals or entities using only basic assistive AI.

## Key Findings

- Agentic inequality arises from three dimensions: **access** (who can deploy agents), **capability** (what the agents can do autonomously), and **volume** (how many agents can be operated).
- Economic effects include labour market displacement (agents competing for jobs at scale), asymmetric information advantages, and compounded inequality where those with existing resources can deploy more capable agents to amplify their advantage.
- Political dimension: agents deployed in advocacy or lobbying roles could systematically out-resolve information asymmetries faced by ordinary citizens vis-à-vis institutions.
- Calls for governance frameworks addressing not just individual AI use but the **collective deployment of agent populations**.

## Relevance to UK Consumer Harms

- Highly relevant to consumer protection: agents deployed by large corporations (financial services, energy, telecoms) can process complex information and negotiate terms that individual consumers cannot match, creating a **structural power asymmetry** in consumer-provider interactions.
- Directly relates to the CMA's concern about AI agents shaping consumer choices: if businesses deploy agent swarms while consumers rely on basic tools, the "level playing field" becomes unworkable.
