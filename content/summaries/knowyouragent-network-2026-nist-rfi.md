# NIST RFI: Security Considerations for AI Agents — Commentary

**Source**: [[knowyouragent-network-2026-nist-rfi|Raw source]] • **Author**: KYA Network • **Published**: February 13, 2026

## Overview
Commentary on NIST CAISI RFI ("Security Considerations for AI Agents", Jan 8, 2026) and companion NCCoE concept paper (Feb 5, 2026). Only 54 comments submitted by mid-February — far below typical regulatory engagement levels.

## RFI Scope (5 Categories)
1. **Security threats/risks**: unique threats to AI agent systems vs traditional software
2. **Security practices**: model-level (prompt injection), system-level (data/tool restrictions), human oversight
3. **Assessing security**: threat anticipation, incident detection, disclosure practices
4. **Deployment environments**: agent access constraints, production monitoring, agent-human/web/IoT/other-agent interactions
5. **Additional considerations**: government collaboration priorities

## RFI Definition of AI Agent
"at least one generative AI model and scaffolding software that equips the model with tools to take a range of discretionary actions" — explicitly excludes chatbots, basic RAG systems, and misuse of AI for cyberattacks.

## Global Regulatory Landscape
- **Singapore**: world's first governance framework for agentic AI (Jan 2026) — requires agent identity management
- **EU AI Act**: Article 50 transparency requirements (Aug 2, 2026) — web requests from AI agents may require labeling
- **UK AISI**: international joint testing exercise (July 2025) — agent safety rates significantly lower than conversational tasks
- **ITU**: new working group (Question 16/17) on AI security and digital identity for agentic AI

## Private Sector Infrastructure
- **Visa TAP**: agent-specific cryptographic signatures (IETF RFC 9421), launched Oct 2025
- **Mastercard Agent Pay**: cryptographically secure Agentic Tokens, launched Apr 2025
- **Google AP2**: tamper-proof Mandates for intent/cart/payment
- **CrowdStrike/SGNL**: $740M acquisition to transform identity security for AI agents

## Relevance
Critical policy engagement window for AI agent identity. Low public participation raises democratic legitimacy concerns. Decentralized identity approaches (DIDs, VCs) absent from NIST documents despite academic support.
