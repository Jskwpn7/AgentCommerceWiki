# Directors' Duties in the Age of Agentic AI — Ahern 2026

## Citation

Ahern, D. (2026) "Directors' duties in the age of agentic artificial intelligence: How can boards navigate corporate purpose and the stakeholder interests of employees around AI adoption?", *Cambridge Forum on AI: Law and Governance*, 2, e7, pp. 1–24. doi:10.1017/cfl.2026.10049. Available at: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6842800

## Overview

Review article examining the tension between directors' fiduciary duties and the welfare implications of AI-driven workforce displacement. The article argues that directors should adopt a "law in context" approach when making AI adoption decisions that affect employees.

## Method

Theoretical, doctrinal and real-world insights blended. Comparative analysis of four models of codified best interests duty across common law jurisdictions (UK, Canada, New Zealand, India). Examines the interaction between the EU AI Act and directors' duties.

## Four Corporate Purpose Models

| Model | Description | Scope for Employee Consideration |
|-------|-------------|----------------------------------|
| Shareholder primacy | Directors must act in company's best interests, traditionally aligned with shareholder value maximization | Minimal — AI-driven efficiency gains insulated from scrutiny |
| Enlightened Shareholder Value | Allows broader stakeholder consideration where it contributes to long-term shareholder value | Limited — employee interests only where they serve shareholders |
| Stakeholder friendly | More explicitly recognizes stakeholder interests as relevant to best interests duty | Moderate |
| Stakeholder value | Most inclusive approach, widest scope for directors to accommodate employee interests | Significant |

## Key Findings

1. **Legal insulation of directors**: Directors are exceptionally insulated from legal scrutiny when making AI adoption decisions. The best interests duty provides little enforceable recourse for displaced employees.

2. **"AI washing" risk**: Directors may misrepresent the extent of AI integration or its impact on the workforce, potentially breaching fiduciary standards — analogous to greenwashing practices.

3. **Enforcement gaps**: Employees' limited ability to operationalize the best interest duty through enforcement makes it an ill-fitting mechanism for redress of AI-driven displacement.

4. **Soft law contribution**: Corporate governance codes and soft law precepts offer meaningful pathways for employee engagement and voice in AI-related decisions.

## Novel Propositions

- **AI as stakeholder**: Provocative question of whether AI agents, given their functional replacement of human workers, might warrant stakeholder status.
- **Law in context approach**: Directors' duties should be shaped by emerging societal expectations, not just enforceable norms from case law.
- **Reskilling as fiduciary duty**: Providing reskilling opportunities could be framed as consistent with acting in the company's best interests.
