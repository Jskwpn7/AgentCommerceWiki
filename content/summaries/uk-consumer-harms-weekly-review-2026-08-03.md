## Section B — Regulatory & Policy Developments

### B1. CMA Agentic AI Guidance (9 March 2026)

**Core document:** "Agentic AI and Consumers" (research paper) + "Complying with Consumer Law When Using AI Agents" (compliance guidance).

**Key findings from the CMA's research:**
- Agentic AI defined as systems that can "sense, decide and act" — going beyond chatbots to execute actions autonomously across multiple services
- Current deployment is "relatively bounded and cautious" in consumer-facing contexts
- Major risks flagged: manipulation, bias, lock-in, loss of consumer agency, dark patterns deployed by AI agents
- The personal shopping/finance agent case study: consumer signs up for agent to manage shopping, subscriptions, and finances; risks include hidden incentives, erroneous switching, and limited data access

**CMA's four compliance steps for businesses:**
1. Tell customers if you use an AI agent
2. Ensure transparency about agent capabilities and limitations
3. Design for compliance (compliance by design)
4. Monitor performance and have human oversight / escalation

**Critical quote:** "The use of AI does not change consumer law obligations. The same rules apply whether a consumer interacts with a person or an AI agent."

### B2. AI & Collusion Blog (4 March 2026)

The CMA published detailed analysis on algorithmic collusion risks:

**Four collusion scenarios:**
1. Classic collusion via algorithms (already illegal)
2. Hub-and-spoke collusion (common pricing software as conduit)
3. Predictable agent (algorithms following price leadership)
4. Autonomous AI (LLM systems learning collusive outcomes without human intent)

**Research cited:** Fish et al. — LLM-based pricing agents "prone to colluding anonymously, with interesting sensitivity to 'prompts'"; Keppo et al. — more agent diversity reduces collusion risk.

**CMA's own capability:** DTI directorate now uses AI and agentic systems internally to detect breaches at "unprecedented pace and scale."

### B3. DRCF Call for Input on Consumer Interest and AI

The Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum (CMA, ICO, FCA, Ofcom) opened a call for input on consumer attitudes to AI on 3 June 2026:
- **Phase 1 (consumer attitudes):** Closed 3 July 2026

### B4. Law Commission — Consumer Class Actions (20 April 2026)

**Major new project:** Law Commission asked to consider introducing a consumer class action regime in England & Wales.

**Key details:**
- Would extend opt-out class actions beyond competition law (CAT) to general consumer law claims
- Initial Scoping Questionnaire deadline: 30 October 2026
- Consultation paper expected in 2027
- Would significantly amplify litigation risk for businesses facing DMCCA enforcement

### B5. EU AI Act Full Enforcement (2 August 2026)

**What activates today:**
- Article 50 transparency obligations for AI-generated content
- GPAI enforcement powers — AI Office can request technical docs, evaluate models, require corrective measures, issue fines
- Full penalty regime: up to €30M or 7% global turnover

**Impact on UK businesses:** The EU AI Act applies to any provider selling into EU, plus GPAI models with EU presence. UK-based AI companies serving EU consumers are directly affected.
