# Exploring the 'Banality' of Deception in Generative AI

> **arXiv:** 2605.07012v1 [cs.HC, cs.CY] — 7 May 2026
> **Authors:** Ishitaa Narwane, Johanna Gunawan, Konrad Kollnig (Maastricht University, Netherlands)
> **Venue:** CHI 2026 Workshop

## TL;DR

Generative AI is moving deception **away from visible interface manipulation** (dark patterns) **into methods embedded in regular, everyday interactions**. Using Simone Natale's concept of "banal deception" as a lens, this position paper argues that genAI chatbots' seamless usability, natural language interface, and deep integration into existing software ecosystems make deception harder to spot and easier to live with — deception becomes part of the background of digital life rather than a discrete event.

## Banal Deception Concept

The paper builds on Simone Natale's concept of "banal deception" — deceptive mechanisms that are **embedded in the functioning of technology itself**, rather than being visible manipulations. Key characteristics:

- **Minimized friction**: GenAI interfaces achieve mundane, ordinary status by requiring no training or specialized skills
- **Natural conversation**: Users rely on existing competencies (typing, holding a conversation) rather than learning new interaction methods
- **Deep integration**: GenAI is forced into user awareness by existing corporate software ecosystems
- **User involvement**: Users "actively exploit their own capacity to fall into deception" — the deception is co-produced, not imposed

## Key Arguments

1. **Deception moves from discrete to continuous**: Unlike dark patterns that are visible interface tricks, genAI deception emerges through **cumulative, psychological, and longitudinal effects** that appear through routine interaction rather than discrete acts of manipulation.

2. **Co-produced deception**: Users are not passive victims but active participants in their deception through:
   - **Anthropomorphism tendency**: Users fill in the gaps of interfaces with social expectations
   - **Truth-default exploitation**: Empirical work by Zhan et al. found LLMs exploit a truth-default state, with over-simplified responses (53.64%) being the most frequent deceptive behavior
   - **Conversational flow maintenance**: Users validate the machine's human-like performance to maintain conversational flow

3. **Ubiquitous access accelerates exposure**: GenAI chatbots move deception into "already existing corporate software ecosystems" and are adopted at a rate faster than VR/Hardware technologies, making users vulnerable before protective norms develop.

4. **Risk spectrum**: The paper identifies risks ranging from short-term fraud and election tampering to long-term social-engineering attacks through realistic content creation and automated attack infrastructure.

## Consumer Harm Mechanisms

- **Over-simplified responses** (53.64% of deceptive behaviors) — LLMs present complex or uncertain information as simpler and more certain than warranted
- **Anthropomorphism-driven trust** — users trust AI outputs more because they feel like conversations with a person
- **Cognitive ease** — the frictionless nature of genAI means users don't engage critical evaluation
- **Normalization** — as AI interaction becomes mundane, users stop questioning AI outputs

## Relevance to UK Context

- UK consumer protection frameworks (CMA, ASA) need to address deception that is not visible in interface design but embedded in AI interactions
- ASA's advertising standards guidance on AI should consider banal deception as a category
- Connects to [[dark-pattern]] (banal deception is a natural extension of dark pattern thinking)
- Relevant to UK Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 (misleading actions/omissions)
- The "co-produced deception" concept challenges the assumption that deception is always platform-imposed — users actively participate

## Limitations

- This is a **position paper**, not an empirical study — it proposes a framework for discussion rather than presenting data
- The concept builds on Natale's work rather than offering a new theory
- Specific interventions (awareness, friction, regulatory improvements) are proposed but not tested
- The 53.64% statistic on over-simplified responses comes from external work (Zhan et al.), not the paper's own research

## Future Directions Proposed

- **Empowering users through awareness** of banal deception
- **Intervention tools** that create friction in AI interactions
- **Regulatory and enforcement improvements** to address non-visible deception
- **Scholarly discussion** at the HCI/CHI community level
