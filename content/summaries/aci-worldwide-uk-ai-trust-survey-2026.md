# ACI Worldwide / YouGov UK AI Shopping Agent Trust Survey 2026

## Document Details

- **Publisher:** ACI Worldwide / YouGov
- **Type:** UK consumer survey
- **Fieldwork:** June 19–22, 2026
- **Release:** June 29, 2026
- **Sample:** 2,000+ UK adults
- **URL:** https://investor.aciworldwide.com/news-releases/news-release-details/six-ten-uk-consumers-would-stop-using-ai-shopping-agent-after

## Summary

ACI Worldwide commissioned YouGov to conduct a comprehensive survey of UK consumer attitudes toward AI shopping agents. The findings reveal an **enormous trust gap** that represents the primary barrier to AI shopping agent adoption in the UK.

## Key Findings

### Trust Deficit

- Only **19%** of UK consumers trust AI assistants to follow rules for everyday purchasing decisions
- **55%** trust a human expert or adviser (36pp gap)
- **69%** do not trust AI even when it follows rules the consumer sets
- This is a **trust gap of 50 percentage points** — the largest single finding

### Fragility of Trust

- **60%** of UK consumers say they would **stop using an AI agent after just one mistake**
- This extremely low error tolerance means AI shopping agents have virtually no margin for error
- A single high-profile incident (e.g., an agent purchasing the wrong product at the wrong price) could have disproportionate reputational damage

### Control and Money Concerns

- Consumers are open to AI helping them shop smarter, but only if they remain **firmly in control** of decision-making and their money
- They will not hand control of their finances to an autonomous system
- Fear over **control, money, and accountability** are the three dominant concerns

## Relevance to UK Consumer Harms

This is a **critical data point** for UK consumer protection research:

1. **[[ai-shopping-agents]]** — The 19% trust figure is the single most important metric for understanding agentic commerce viability in the UK. Compare with Checkout.com's global 27% "trust no organization" figure.

2. **[[agentic-commerce-tokenization]]** — The low trust levels suggest UK consumers would resist tokenized purchasing until trust mechanisms improve dramatically.

3. **Consumer protection framework:** The 60% "one mistake and I quit" finding means that even well-intentioned AI agents pose a consumer risk: any single error has outsized reputational consequences. Regulators should consider whether this creates a market failure where consumers are unfairly exposed to AI errors without adequate recourse.

4. **UK regulatory relevance:** The ACI/YouGov survey is UK-specific (not US/global), making it directly applicable to UK policy development. The FCA could use this data to justify AI consumer protection guidance in financial services.

5. **Treasury Committee pressure:** The UK Treasury Committee's call for FCA AI guidance by end of 2026 gains urgency from this data — UK consumers clearly need protection and guidance as AI shopping agents enter the market.
