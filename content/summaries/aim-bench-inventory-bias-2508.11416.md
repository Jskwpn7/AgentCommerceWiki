# AIM-Bench: Evaluating Decision-making Biases of Agentic LLM as Inventory Manager

## Paper Details

- **arXiv ID:** 2508.11416
- **Authors:** Xuhua Zhao, Yuxuan Xie, Caihua Chen, Yuxiang Sun
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11416
- **Date:** August 2025

## Summary

AIM-Bench is a novel benchmark designed to assess decision-making behavior of LLM agents in uncertain supply chain management scenarios through diverse inventory replenishment experiments. The paper reveals that different LLMs exhibit varying degrees of decision bias similar to those observed in human beings, including framing effects, pull-to-centre effects, and bullwhip effects.

## Consumer Harm Implications

- **Algorithmic supply chain manipulation:** Biased inventory decisions affect product availability, pricing, and ultimately consumer welfare.
- **Consumer impact:** Bullwhip effects in automated supply chains can lead to artificial scarcity or oversupply, harming consumers through price manipulation or product unavailability.
- **Decision opacity:** Consumers affected by supply chain disruptions have no way to identify whether LLM agents contributed to suboptimal outcomes.

## Key Findings

- Different LLMs exhibit varying degrees of decision bias similar to human biases.
- Cognitive reflection strategies and information sharing can mitigate pull-to-centre and bullwhip effects.
- Deployment of LLMs in inventory decision-making requires careful consideration of potential biases.
