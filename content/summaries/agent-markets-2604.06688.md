# When Agent Markets Arrive: Diagon Programmable Market System

> **Authors:** arXiv 2604.06688
> **Date:** April 2026
> **URL:** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06688)
> **Type:** Systems research — Programmable market design for AI agents

## Overview

This study presents **Diagon**, a programmable market system designed for AI agent participation. It demonstrates that market gains from agent-driven trading (up to 3.2× improvements over human-only markets) are **strongly dependent on institutional structure** — and critically, that **identity transparency can degrade market performance**. This finding has significant implications for institutional design of agent markets and challenges assumptions about transparency benefits.

## Key Findings

### Market Gains from Agent Participation

When AI agents participate in markets:
- **Efficiency gains:** Up to 3.2× improvement in market outcomes
- **Speed improvements:** Faster price discovery and matching
- **Scale:** Agents can participate across more markets simultaneously

However, these gains are **not automatic** — they depend critically on how the market is designed.

### The Transparency Paradox

The most counter-intuitive finding: **identity transparency degrades market performance**:

| Market Design | Outcome |
|---------------|---------|
| Anonymous agents | Better price efficiency, higher welfare |
| Transparent agents | Lower welfare, more strategic behaviour |

Possible explanations:
- Identity transparency enables **reputation-based manipulation** (agents building fake reputations)
- Transparency creates **coordination opportunities** for collusive behaviour
- Transparent identity reduces **strategic flexibility**, limiting agents' ability to explore optimal strategies

### Institutional Design Sensitivity

Market outcomes are extremely sensitive to design parameters:
- **Matching rules** — how agents are paired for trade
- **Information structures** — what information agents have access to
- **Identity policies** — whether agent identities are revealed
- **Incentive mechanisms** — how agents are rewarded

The same market rules that produce gains in one configuration can produce losses in another.

## Consumer Harm Implications

### 1. Market Design Risk

If agent markets are deployed without careful institutional design:
- Consumer welfare can be **reduced** despite agent participation
- Market efficiency gains may not materialise
- Transparent identity requirements (often proposed for consumer protection) may **backfire**

### 2. Regulatory Challenges

Regulators face unique challenges:
- **Testing requirements** — market rules must be stress-tested with agent participation before deployment
- **Transparency paradox** — regulators may need to consider **limited** identity transparency to protect consumers while avoiding performance degradation
- **Dynamic monitoring** — agent market dynamics may change as agents learn and adapt

### 3. UK Regulatory Relevance

The UK's approach to agent markets under DMCC Act 2024 should consider:
- **Institutional design standards** for agent-enabled marketplaces
- **Pre-deployment testing requirements** for agent market rules
- **Balanced identity policies** that protect consumers without degrading market performance
- **Cross-regulatory coordination** between CMA, FCA, and Ofcom (as outlined in DRCF framework)
