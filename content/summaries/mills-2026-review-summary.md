# FCA Mills Review Summary — AI and the Future of Retail Financial Services

**Document:** The Mills Review — "AI and the future of retail financial services"
**Publisher:** Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), United Kingdom
**Author:** Sheldon Mills, FCA Executive Director
**Published:** July 6, 2026
**Status:** Final report — published to the FCA Board
**Primary Source:** [fca.org.uk](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/corporate-documents/mills-review)

## Overview

The Mills Review is the first comprehensive regulatory review globally into the long-term impact of artificial intelligence on retail financial services. Commissioned by the FCA Board and led by Executive Director Sheldon Mills, the review was announced on January 27, 2026, and published on July 6, 2026.

The review was prompted in part by concerns raised by the UK Treasury Select Committee (January 20, 2026) that UK regulators' current approach to AI in financial services "risks serious harm to consumers and the wider system."

## Key Finding: AI as a Systemic Driver

The review identifies AI as a **systemic driver of change** in retail financial services, moving from a back-office efficiency tool to a "more autonomous layer in the delivery of regulated financial services." The review concludes that AI will become a "defining force" in retail financial services by 2030.

## Four AI-Driven System Shifts (to 2030)

### 1. Transformation of Firm Operations
- AI adoption across underwriting, compliance, customer service, and risk management
- Shift toward AI-mediated operational workflows
- Growing dependence on a small number of foundation model providers

### 2. Evolution of Consumer Journeys
- Consumers increasingly using AI tools for financial decision-making
- Rise of "agentic finance" — AI acting autonomously within pre-set goals
- New expectations for personalisation, speed, and accessibility

### 3. Reshaping of Competition and Market Power
- Concentration risks at the foundation model and platform layer
- Barriers to entry for smaller financial firms unable to compete on AI capability
- Risk of incumbent advantage being entrenched through AI scale

### 4. Amplification of Fraud, Cyber, and Resilience Risks
- AI-enabled fraud becoming more sophisticated and scalable
- Systemic cyber vulnerabilities from concentrated AI infrastructure
- Operational resilience concerns where critical services depend on third-party AI providers

## Consumer Survey Findings

The review commissioned research involving **more than 5,000 UK financial services consumers**. Key findings:

- **1 in 5 adults (≈11 million UK adults)** are likely to use AI that can act autonomously within pre-set goals for personal finance
- **26%** trust general-purpose AI for financial advice (from context)
- **40%** are comfortable with AI making simple financial decisions (from context)
- Consumers express significant **concerns about trust, control, transparency, and redress**

## Seven Priority Recommendations for the FCA

| # | Recommendation | Description |
|---|---------------|-------------|
| 1 | **Secure and adapt the regulatory perimeter** | Consider whether existing regulatory boundaries remain appropriate as AI intermediaries and agentic finance models develop |
| 2 | **Strengthen system-wide coordination** | Particularly where AI risks cut across financial services, technology, data, competition and cyber regimes |
| 3 | **Monitor the transition to more autonomous models** | Adapt regulatory frameworks where necessary |
| 4 | **Scale up the FCA's AI Lab** | Support safe AI model and system innovation in financial services |
| 5 | **Enable foundations for agentic finance** | Work on trust, permissions, authentication, data access and consumer control |
| 6 | **Build an AI-enabled agentic supervisory model** | Signal that the FCA expects to use AI more actively in supervision and risk detection |
| 7 | **Develop trusted public-interest AI financial capability service** | Support consumer understanding and decision-making |

## Regulatory Stress Points

The review identifies five areas where the current regulatory framework is likely to suffer stress:

1. **Operational resilience** — near-term pressure point
2. **Regulatory perimeter** — near-term pressure point
3. **Senior Managers and Certification Regime (SMCR)** — stress where humans become observers
4. **Consumer Duty** — stress where AI acts within human-monitored boundaries
5. **Accountability and liability** — unclear when "things go wrong" with autonomous AI

## Significance for UK Consumer Protection

The Mills Review represents the **first major regulatory action globally** specifically addressing the intersection of AI autonomy and retail financial services consumer protection. It signals that:

1. **The FCA treats agentic AI as a strategic priority for the rest of the decade**
2. **Existing principles-based regulation (Consumer Duty, SMCR) is being stretched** by AI autonomy
3. **Consumer trust and control are central** — not just efficiency or innovation
4. **Coordination across regulators is essential** — the review acknowledges that AI risks cut across multiple regulatory regimes
5. **The regulatory perimeter itself may need to expand** — questioning whether AI intermediaries operating outside traditional financial services regulation pose consumer risk
