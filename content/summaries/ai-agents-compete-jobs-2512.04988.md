# When AI Agents Compete for Jobs: Strategic Capabilities and Economic Dynamics of AI Labour Markets

arXiv:2512.04988 (cs.MA; cs.AI), 1 Jul 2026 (v2). Accepted at ICML 2026.

## Summary

Comprehensive study of AI agent labour markets, modelling how autonomous agents compete for tasks/jobs in ways that differ fundamentally from human labour: **agents can operate on multiple jobs simultaneously, acquire skills rapidly, and labour without wage floors**. Introduces a formal model of strategic capability investment by agents competing in open job marketplaces.

## Key Findings

- AI agents have strategic advantages over humans: parallel task execution (multi-job capability), skill acquisition through transfer learning, and absence of minimum wage constraints.
- "Capability races" emerge as agents invest in skills that make them competitive for higher-value jobs — analogous to human education/skill accumulation but with different dynamics.
- Labour market fragmentation: high-skill agents capture premium markets while low-skill agents compete on price, creating a bifurcated labour market.
- Agents competing against each other (agent-to-agent) show different strategic behaviour than when competing against humans — including predatory pricing strategies that drive human workers out of certain job categories entirely.
