# Shopping in the Age of AI: Redefining Stores for a New Era

**Source:** McKinsey & Company / ICSC  
**Authors:** Colleen Baum (Senior Partner, McKinsey)  
**Published:** April 27, 2026  
**Format:** Report + webinar (July 13)  

> **Note:** Original McKinsey page behind paywall; content derived from ICSC press release and industry coverage. Report was ranked in McKinsey's Top 10 Reports — Second Quarter 2026 list.

## Summary

A major joint report by McKinsey & Company and ICSC examining how AI is reshaping consumer shopping behavior and the role of physical retail stores. Based on interviews with retail/real estate leaders and a survey of **3,004 US consumers** across grocery, fashion, health/beauty, home, electronics, and food/beverage sectors.

The report identifies three structural forces transforming retail and argues that physical stores are not becoming obsolete — rather, AI is raising the bar for what stores must deliver to justify consumer visits.

## Key Findings

### The $1 Trillion Opportunity

- McKinsey projects up to **$1 trillion in U.S. B2C retail revenue** from agentic commerce by 2030
- AI-mediated search and purchase automation are fundamentally reshaping consumer expectations
- Store visits may become less frequent but more purposeful

### The Convenience vs. Discovery Divide

As AI agents handle routine shopping, retailers must clearly commit to one of two strategies:

| Strategy | Focus | Key Metric |
|----------|-------|------------|
| **Convenience-Optimized** | Speed, intuitive navigation, in-stock reliability | 37% of consumers cite convenience as top driver |
| **Discovery-Led** | Experiential retail, digital storytelling, personalized service | 40%+ of Gen Z/millennials say experience drives store visits |

### Consumer AI Adoption (Pre-Purchase Journey)

- **62%** use AI to compare brands, models, prices or reviews
- **54%** use AI to learn about a category/product (feature prioritization)
- **~50%** turn to AI for discovery and inspiration
- **68%** used at least one AI-enabled tool in the past three months

AI usage is concentrated in early shopping journey stages — discovery and comparison — pushing the "last mile" of physical store visits toward more deliberate, experience-driven trips.

### Economic Stakes

The winners are pulling away sharply: McKinsey estimates the **top decile** of retailers executing this strategy will capture **more than 85% of sector economic profit**. The gap between those who adapt their store systems and those that don't is widening rapidly.

## Three Forces Reshaping Retail

1. **Growing use of AI in the shopping journey** — AI agents increasingly handling discovery, comparison, and even autonomous purchase
2. **Rising expectations for transparency and convenience** — consumers demand faster, clearer, more reliable experiences
3. **Shifts in consumer spending power and behaviors** — evolving demographics altering retail dynamics

## Strategic Recommendations

1. **Define each store's mission clearly** — every location must have a specific strategic purpose (convenience OR discovery, not both)
2. **Align layout, assortment, technology, and talent** to deliver on that chosen role
3. **Use technology as an enabler** — reduce friction in convenience-led stores; enhance service/personalization in discovery-led environments
4. **Curate tenant mixes** — blend retail, dining, and services into cohesive destinations (for shopping center operators)
5. **Operational redesign + disciplined capital allocation** required to execute

## Consumer Harm Relevance

This report is directly relevant to UK consumer protection research for several reasons:

- **Agentic commerce scale:** The $1T projection underscores the urgency of regulating autonomous agent purchasing — AI agents making purchases on consumers' behalf without full transparency or control
- **Consumer autonomy erosion:** 68% AI tool usage before purchase means most shopping journeys now involve algorithmic mediation. Consumers may not realize their agent is optimizing for convenience over discovery, or that a single strategy (convenience) is being applied across their entire portfolio
- **Dark pattern risk to agents:** If GUI agents are vulnerable to dark patterns on retail sites (as documented in IEEE S&P research), autonomous shopping agents could be manipulated at scale — purchasing products based on manipulated interfaces rather than genuine consumer preference
- **Transparency deficit:** The report's emphasis on "clear mission" for stores implicitly acknowledges the confusion consumers face when their agents encounter inconsistent store strategies. UK regulators should require transparency about how retail environments are designed to influence both human and agent shoppers
