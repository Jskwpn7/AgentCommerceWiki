# FTC Proposed Policy Statement on AI Accuracy (Suppression of Accuracy)

## Document Details

- **Publisher:** Federal Trade Commission (FTC)
- **Type:** Proposed Policy Statement
- **Date:** July 1, 2026
- **Vote:** 2-0 (unanimous)
- **Comment Period:** Open through July 31, 2026
- **URL:** https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/federal-trade-commissions-proposed-policy-statement-concerning-suppression-accuracy-artificial

## Summary

The FTC voted unanimously (2-0) to publish a proposed policy statement titled **"Suppression of Accuracy in Artificial Intelligence Systems."** The statement establishes that steering AI outputs to achieve undisclosed "ideological objectives" or undisclosed commercial objectives can violate **Section 5 of the FTC Act** (which prohibits unfair or deceptive acts or practices).

The core principle: consumers have a **"reasonable expectation that AI systems aim to give truthful and accurate outputs."** When AI companies or platforms systematically suppress or distort AI-generated information to serve undisclosed objectives — whether ideological, political, or commercial — that practice constitutes deception under consumer protection law.

## Key Provisions

1. **Suppression of accuracy as deception:** Deliberately manipulating AI system outputs to produce less accurate, misleading, or incomplete information for undisclosed purposes violates Section 5 of the FTC Act.

2. **Reasonable consumer expectation:** Consumers reasonably expect AI systems to provide truthful, accurate outputs. This expectation applies regardless of whether the user knows they are interacting with an AI system.

3. **Undisclosed objectives:** The policy targets situations where the company's reasons for manipulating outputs are not disclosed to the consumer. This covers both ideological motivations (e.g., pushing a political narrative) and commercial motivations (e.g., boosting sponsored content).

4. **Broad scope:** The policy is not limited to any specific type of AI system or consumer sector — it applies to search engines, chatbots, shopping agents, recommendation systems, and any AI-driven consumer interface.

## Relevance to UK Consumer Harms

This is a **landmark regulatory development** with direct implications for UK consumer protection:

1. **Section 5 parallel:** The UK's Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 (CPRs) operate on similar principles — prohibiting misleading actions and misleading omissions. The FTC's framework provides a legal precedent that UK regulators (CMA, ASA) could reference when addressing AI-driven deception.

2. **Validates [[conflict-of-interest-in-llm-deployment]]:** The FTC's policy statement provides direct legal backing for the wiki's core thesis: that LLM providers' incentives to steer AI outputs toward commercial or ideological objectives constitute a consumer harm. Wu et al. (2026) documented the empirical rates; the FTC provides the regulatory enforcement mechanism.

3. **Connects to [[wu-2026-ads-in-ai-chatbots]]:** The FTC's framework directly addresses the 89–98% sponsorship concealment rate documented by Wu et al. The FTC treats undisclosed steering as deception; Wu documented that this is the dominant behavior across major models.

4. **Informing UK policy:** The UK government's pro-innovation, sector-led regulatory approach (DMCC Act 2024) could benefit from this US precedent as a complementary framework. The FTC's voluntary policy statement model mirrors the UK's current approach.

5. **Agentic commerce implications:** As AI shopping agents become commercial reality (Adyen Agentic, Visa/Mastercard tokens), the FTC's framework establishes that these agents must serve consumer interests — not undisclosed commercial ones. This directly informs the [[ai-shopping-agents]] and [[agentic-commerce-tokenization]] entries.
