# Enabling Open Finance in EMDEs: Incentives, Liability, and Performance Measurement

**Cambridge Centre for Alternative Finance (CCAF)** — June 2026

> [!NOTE] Original PDF (5.7 MB) rendered as Cloudflare block. Content extracted from JBS faculty landing page and SSRN abstract.

## Abstract

This report examines the incentives, liability frameworks, and performance measurement challenges for open finance implementations in emerging and developing economies (EMDEs). It provides a comparative analysis of how different regulatory approaches affect consumer protection outcomes in open finance ecosystems.

## Key Findings

1. **Incentive Structures**: The report identifies that mandatory data-sharing mandates in EMDEs face different adoption barriers compared to voluntary frameworks, with significant implications for consumer data access and control.

2. **Liability Allocation**: A critical finding is the uneven distribution of liability between data holders (financial institutions), data recipients (fintechs, third parties), and consumers. Many EMDEs lack clear liability frameworks, leaving consumers exposed when data breaches or misusing data occurs.

3. **Performance Measurement**: The report proposes a framework for measuring open finance ecosystem performance that accounts for EMDE-specific factors including infrastructure gaps, financial literacy levels, and regulatory capacity.

4. **Consumer Protection Gaps**: Several EMDEs show significant gaps in consumer protection provisions, particularly around data consent mechanisms, dispute resolution processes, and accountability for third-party data processing.

## Implications for Consumer Protection

- **Regulatory Harmonization**: EMDEs face unique challenges in harmonizing open finance regulations across borders, which can create regulatory arbitrage opportunities that undermine consumer protection.

- **Data Consent**: The report highlights that many EMDE consumers lack adequate mechanisms to understand and control how their financial data is used by third parties.

- **Dispute Resolution**: Existing dispute resolution mechanisms in many EMDEs are not equipped to handle the complexities of data-driven financial services, particularly when multiple jurisdictions are involved.

- **Capacity Building**: There is a strong case for targeted capacity building for regulatory authorities in EMDEs to effectively oversee open finance ecosystems.

## Methodology

The research draws on comparative case studies of open finance implementations in 8 EMDEs, combined with stakeholder interviews and regulatory analysis.
