# Beyond Retrieval-Ranking: A Multi-Agent Cognitive Decision Framework for E-Commerce Search

## Paper Details

- **arXiv ID:** 2510.20567
- **Authors:** Zhouwei Zhai, Mengxiang Chen, Haoyun Xia, Jin Li, Renquan Zhou, Min Yang
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20567
- **Date:** October 2025

## Summary

Proposes a Multi-Agent Cognitive Decision Framework (MACDF) that shifts e-commerce search from passive retrieval-ranking to proactive decision support. The framework addresses limitations of query-item matching paradigms including semantic gaps in complex queries, high decision costs from cross-platform information foraging, and absence of professional shopping guidance.

Evaluated on JD.com search platform with offline and online A/B testing.

## Consumer Harm Implications

- **Search result manipulation:** MACDF's proactive approach could be exploited to manipulate search outcomes beyond traditional SEO.
- **Ranking opacity:** Multi-agent cognitive frameworks introduce additional layers of opacity in how search results are ordered.
- **Professional guidance concerns:** When agents provide "shopping guidance," consumers may trust recommendations without understanding the agent's incentives.

## Key Findings

- MACDF shows significant improvements in recommendation accuracy and user satisfaction, particularly for complex queries involving negation, multi-constraint, or reasoning demands.
- Online A/B testing on JD search platform confirmed practical efficacy.
- Multi-agent cognitive systems can redefine e-commerce search but introduce new transparency challenges.
