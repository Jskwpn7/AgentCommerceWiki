# FCA Consumer Duty and AI Regulatory Enforcement

## Current Position (April 2026 Update)

The FCA has adopted a **principles-based, technology-agnostic approach** to AI regulation in financial services. No bespoke AI rulebook — existing frameworks fully applied to AI systems. The April 2026 WilmerHale analysis confirms active enforcement posture using existing Consumer Duty framework rather than waiting for new legislation.

## Key Regulatory Frameworks Applied to AI

### 1. Consumer Duty as Primary Lens
Where AI influences product design, distribution, pricing, eligibility/creditworthiness, servicing or customer communications:
- Outputs must be **tested for fairness, suitability and foreseeable harm**
- Firms must document and monitor AI use, testing and remediation of identified issues
- Evidence-based supervision with close scrutiny of systems/processes

### 2. SM&CR Accountability
- Clear senior manager responsibility required for approving AI use cases
- Model/data risk management accountability
- Third-party AI service oversight
- Operational resilience requirements for AI components

### 3. Outsourcing and Third-Party Risk
Firms must manage third-party AI provider relationships to mitigate consumer harm risks:
- Determine whether arrangements constitute FCA-defined outsourcing
- Apply proportionate third-party risk controls and resilience planning
- Include third-party dependencies in IBS mapping and resilience assessments

### 4. Operational Resilience
AI components embedded in customer-facing processes (onboarding, payments controls, fraud detection) and back-office processing:
- Part of important business services (IBS) mapping
- Required for scenario testing and vulnerability remediation

## Seven Enforcement Risk Areas

The FCA has identified seven specific hazards where AI use can trigger enforcement action:

| Risk Area | Trigger Conditions | FCA View |
|-----------|-------------------|----------|
| Consumer harm from opaque/biased AI decisions | Credit, pricing, insurance underwriting produce unfair outcomes | Failures in governance/controls/accountability will be enforced |
| SM&CR accountability failure | No clear ownership or approval processes for AI systems | Senior Managers must evidence oversight |
| Poor data governance | Models trained on flawed data leading to misleading outcomes | Unfair treatment triggers enforcement regardless of intent |
| Over-reliance on third-party/black box models | Unable to evidence testing/validation/oversight of deployed models | Firms responsible for all AI outputs regardless of development path |
| AI-driven market abuse or disruption | Trading, pricing or execution contributing to market abuse | Market abuse controls must extend to AI components |
| Misleading AI disclosures | Overstating AI accuracy, neutrality or fairness | "AI-washing" comparable to green washing; subject to financial promotion standards |

## FCA's Own Use of AI — Transformative Development

The FCA has engaged a third-party technology provider to trial AI tools analysing:
- Suspicious activity reports (SARs)
- Customer complaints at scale
- Live case files and regulatory data

This development could "transform the regulator's financial crime and regulatory breach detection, disruption, and associated supervision and enforcement capabilities" — creating potentially significant competitive asymmetry between regulators and firms.

## Best Practice Requirements for FCA-Regulated Firms

1. **Governance** — Establish clear ownership, governance and accountability for AI risk aligned to SM&CR expectations
2. **Documentation** — Maintain evidence-based records of AI benefits/risks demonstrating systems meet regulatory expectations
3. **Third-party contracts** — Assessment per FCA outsourcing definitions with appropriate risk controls
4. **IBS integration** — Ensure AI components in operational resilience framework including impact tolerances, mapping, scenario testing
