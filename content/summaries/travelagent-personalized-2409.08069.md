## Overview

Dynamic real-world travel planning requires satisfying three objectives: rationality, comprehensiveness, and personalization. Existing systems struggle with multi-dimensional constraints.

## Key Contributions

1. **Four-Module Architecture**:
   - Tool-usage: External resource integration
   - Recommendation: Personalized suggestions
   - Planning: Itinerary construction
   - Memory: Context retention across planning sessions
2. **Dynamic Scenario Support**: Handles real-world constraints (availability, timing, budgets)
3. **Multi-Dimensional Evaluation**: Validated on rationality, comprehensiveness, personalization

## Implications for Consumer Protection

- **Booking Manipulation**: Personalized recommendations may favor higher-commission options
- **Transparency**: Consumers unaware how itineraries are generated or optimized
- **Data Collection**: Memory module stores extensive user preferences and travel history
- **Algorithmic Bias**: Personalization may create information cocoons limiting travel options
- **Accountability**: When itineraries are irrational or harmful, difficulty determining responsibility
