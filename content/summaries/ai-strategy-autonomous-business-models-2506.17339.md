# AI is the Strategy: From Agentic AI to Autonomous Business Models onto Strategy in the Age of AI

arXiv:2506.17339 (cs.CY / econ.GN), 24 June 2025 | Bohnsack & de Wet | Universidade Catolica Portuguesa | 17 pages, 6 figures, 3 tables

## Summary

Develops the concept of **Autonomous Business Models (ABMs)** — business models where agentic AI systems execute the core mechanisms of value creation, delivery, and capture with minimal ongoing human intervention. The core thesis: agentic AI should not be viewed as a tool to support strategy, but as the strategy itself.

## Key Concepts

**Three Core Mechanisms of ABMs:**

1. **Agentic execution of core logic** — AI systems are the primary agents executing the firm's value creation and capture mechanisms (not merely supporting human decisions)
2. **Minimal human intervention** — Human oversight becomes exception-based, activated only when the AI flags novel scenarios or strategic goals need updating
3. **Adaptive decision cycles** — Continuous feedback loops of sensing → deciding → learning without constant human direction

**Synthetic Competition:** A new form of competition where autonomous AI agents compete at machine-level speed and scale. Strategic interactions between firms occur in real-time through their agent systems, with no human in the loop for tactical responses. This challenges foundational assumptions in competitive strategy theory.

**The Strategy-Business Model-Tactics Distinction (revisited):** Builds on Casadesus-Masanell & Ricart (2010). In traditional models:
- Strategy = high-level plan/vision
- Business model = value creation/capture architecture  
- Tactics = fine-tuning within the model

In ABMs, AI progressively takes over tactics → then business model operations → and eventually can adapt strategy elements themselves.

## Illustrative Cases

**Swan AI (getswan.ai):** Israeli startup pursuing autonomy by design
- Plans to reach $30M ARR without hiring additional human employees beyond founding team of 3
- Deploys ~20+ autonomous AI agents instead of traditional departments
- Core offering: AI sales agents that autonomously identify prospects, draft personalized outreach, follow up, and schedule meetings once initial parameters are set
- Cost structure dominated by computing rather than linear headcount growth
- Approaches ABM edge: core processes are autonomous; humans handle quality assurance and business development

**Ryanair (hypothetical evolution):** European low-cost airline analyzed as incumbent case
- Current state: AI used extensively but within human-defined parameters (dynamic pricing, yield management, predictive maintenance, chatbot customer service)
- Proposed future: "AI Factory" continuously ingesting booking data, market prices, fleet telemetry, and customer behavior; autonomously driving operational decisions (fleet allocation, dynamic pricing, marketing optimization); management role shifts to setting guardrails and governance principles

## Key Findings

- The evolution from AI-augmented to autonomous business models follows a spectrum, not a binary switch
- Autonomous models achieve scale without proportional increases in headcount — fundamentally different cost structures
- Data network effects: more clients → more data → better AI performance → competitive advantage (concentration risk)
- Competition can emerge if algorithms become commoditized — proprietary AI capability becomes the differentiator, not just having an autonomous model
- ABMs require new forms of oversight and trust-building with customers

## Consumer Protection Implications

- **Synthetic competition:** If firms compete at machine speed using autonomous agents, consumers cannot exercise meaningful comparison or negotiation — they face algorithmically determined prices/outcomes without human alternatives
- **Governance gap:** The paper acknowledges guardrails are needed but doesn't specify consumer-protection mechanisms — the very governance challenge this wiki tracks
- **Platform concentration risk:** Data network effects may accelerate market consolidation in AI-enabled commerce
