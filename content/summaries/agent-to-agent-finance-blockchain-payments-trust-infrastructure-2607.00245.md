# Agent-to-Agent Finance: Blockchain Payments and Trust Infrastructure for Autonomous AI Agents

arXiv:2607.00245 (q-fin.GN), 30 June 2026 | Gong | UCL Institute of Finance & Technology | 22 pages, 3 figures, 4 tables

## Summary

Develops the concept of **agent-to-agent (A2A) finance** — the layer of machine-mediated financial interaction in which autonomous agents discover counterparties, purchase services, express transaction intent, execute payments and generate auditable evidence. The paper situates A2A finance as an emerging form of financial market infrastructure required for AI agents to function as economic actors.

## Core Thesis

Autonomous AI agents occupy a position between analytical tools and transacting counterparties:
- They can interpret goals, call external tools, negotiate with other agents
- Access data and computation
- In some settings, initiate payments or blockchain transactions

This creates an infrastructure need: if software agents act economically, markets require systems for identity, authorisation, payment, verification, reputation and accountability.

## Infrastructure Needs for Economic Agents

The paper identifies six essential infrastructure components:

1. **Identity** — verifiable agent identity on the blockchain
2. **Authorisation** — delegated payment authority and spending limits
3. **Payment** — programmable settlement via smart contracts
4. **Verification** — proof of service delivery and performance
5. **Reputation** — trust signals built from transaction history
6. **Accountability** — auditable evidence trails for disputes

## Technical Building Blocks Discussed

- Blockchain A2A payments (emerging protocols)
- ERC-8004 agent registries (decentralized identity standard)
- Provenance-based wallets
- Deterministic inference verification
- DeFi intent mining
- Official evidence on AI adoption in financial services

## Key Design Question: Bounded Autonomy

The paper argues the decisive design question is not whether agents should transact, but **bounded autonomy**: "how to let agents transact without making markets more opaque, fragile or unaccountable."

This is a consumer protection question at heart — how much autonomy to grant agents while preserving human oversight, dispute resolution capability, and market stability.

## Consumer Protection Implications

- A2A finance creates new counterparty risk scenarios: when an agent transacts autonomously, who is liable for errors or fraud?
- Smart contract-based payment systems reduce human oversight but create irreversibility risks
- Decentralized identity standards must balance agent autonomy with accountability and Know Your Customer (KYC) requirements
- The paper's caution against treating blockchain as a "universal substrate" aligns with the wiki's approach of evaluating specific use cases rather than technological determinism
