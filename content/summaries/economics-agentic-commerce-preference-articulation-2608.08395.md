# From Product Search to Preference Articulation: The Economics of Agentic Commerce

arXiv:2608.08395 (econ.TH / cs.AI / cs.GT), 9 August 2026 | Dong, Luo & Xu | Washington University Olin Business School, University of Connecticut

## Summary

A theoretical economics paper modeling the welfare implications of agentic search in digital commerce. It compares **manual search** (consumer directly evaluates products) against **agentic search** (consumer delegates discovery to an AI agent), showing how preference complexity fundamentally changes the search equilibrium.

## Key Findings

### 1. Complexity Collapse of Manual Search
As **preference complexity** grows (the number of satisfaction-relevant dimensions that are hard to articulate before search but easy to evaluate upon inspection):
- Manual search eventually collapses — consumers stop inspecting products
- Mismatch returns to no-search levels
- Platform revenue falls to zero

Agentic search avoids this finite complexity collapse. Once preference refinement becomes worthwhile, it remains worthwhile at every higher level of complexity. Articulation effort and mismatch may increase, but mismatch stays below the no-search level and revenue remains positive.

### 2. Platform vs Consumer Incentive Misalignment
The platform ranks search regimes by **conversion revenue**, whereas the consumer bears **search expenditure** (time, cognitive load). This creates a divergence: agentic search can become revenue-superior for platforms before it becomes privately optimal for consumers. Platforms have an incentive to push agents even when consumers would be better off searching manually.

## Key Concept: Preference Complexity
The number of satisfaction-relevant dimensions difficult to articulate before search but readily evaluated upon inspection (e.g., fashion fit, feel, aesthetics). This is the variable that determines whether manual or agentic search is effective.

## Consumer Protection Implications

This is a rigorously modeled paper with direct consumer protection implications:
- **Regulatory arbitrage risk:** Platforms may promote agentic search features because they increase conversion revenue even when consumer welfare declines
- **Preference opacity:** When consumers delegate search to agents, they may not understand how their preferences are being translated into product recommendations
- **Information asymmetry intensification:** Agentic search concentrates information-processing power in the hands of platforms that control the agent algorithms
