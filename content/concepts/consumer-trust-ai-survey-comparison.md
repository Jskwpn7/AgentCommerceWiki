# AI Agent Consumer Trust — Cross-Jurisdictional Survey Comparison

> **Synthesis** of recent consumer trust surveys across the US and UK, comparing trust levels, accountability understanding, and adoption readiness for AI agents in consumer contexts.

## Overview

No existing wiki page aggregates consumer trust data across the recent wave of surveys on AI agents and consumer protection. This page synthesizes findings from five major surveys to establish baseline trust metrics, identify cross-jurisdictional patterns, and highlight the gap between AI capability growth and consumer trust.

## Key Patterns

### 1. Trust Is Universally Low

The most consistent finding across all jurisdictions is that **consumer trust in AI agents remains critically low**:

- UK: 19% trust AI for purchasing, 26% trust AI for financial advice
- Global: 27% trust no organization with AI agent operation
- US: $35M Hopper settlement signals market punishment for trust violations

### 2. Accountability Gap Is Widespread

Only **40% of UK consumers understand who is liable** when AI makes bad recommendations. This is the single most important adoption barrier — consumers will not delegate to AI systems where accountability is unclear.

### 3. Trust Is Earning, Not Given

FCA data shows what drives trust:

| Trust Driver | Priority % |
|-------------|------------|
| "Clear protections if something goes wrong" | 32% |
| "Evidence of accuracy and reliability" | 30% |
| Speed / convenience | Not a top driver |

### 4. Sector Differences

| Sector | Trust Level | Key Concern |
|--------|------------|-------------|
| **Financial services** | Lowest (26%) | Accountability, loss of funds |
| **Everyday purchasing** | Low (19%) | Rule-following, manipulation |
| **Telecoms** | Declining | Deepfake voice fraud (38% switching readiness) |

## Implications for Consumer Protection

### The Trust Gap Is a Consumer Harm

Low trust in AI agents is itself a form of consumer harm:

1. **Underutilization of beneficial AI** — Consumers may reject helpful AI tools (e.g., financial planning agents) due to lack of trust, even when the risk is manageable
2. **Market distortion** — Low trust favors incumbent platforms that can "buy" trust through brand recognition rather than genuine safety
3. **Regulatory pressure** — As trust erodes, calls for restrictive regulation increase, potentially stifling innovation

### Regulatory Response Needed

The trust data supports several regulatory actions:

- **Fiduciary duties** for custodial AI agents (as proposed in the AI AGENT Act)
- **Clear liability frameworks** for AI-mediated transactions
- **Transparency requirements** for AI system limitations and training data
- **Enforcement against trust violations** (e.g., Hopper's $35M settlement)
