# EU Digital Fairness Act (2026)

**Status:** Proposed legislation — announced in the European Commission 2026 Work Programme (COM(2025) 870), expected in Q4 2026
**Relevant Regulators:** European Commission, European Parliament, national consumer protection authorities
**Primary Source:** European Commission Legislative Train 06.2026 (as of 20 June 2026) — [europarl.europa.eu](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/carriage/digital-fairness-act/report)

## Overview

The Digital Fairness Act is a proposed EU legislative initiative for Q4 2026, announced as part of the Commission's 2026 work programme under the priority "Protecting our democracy, upholding our values." It represents the most significant expansion of EU consumer protection law in the digital realm in over a decade.

## Scope and Objectives

The Act is expected to address the following problems:

- **Dark patterns** — deceptive or manipulative interface designs that undermine informed consumer choice
- **Addictive design** of digital products — features that exploit cognitive vulnerabilities to create compulsive usage
- **Influencer marketing** — commercial practices by social media influencers, particularly where they target minors or vulnerable audiences
- **Unfair personalisation** — algorithmic pricing and recommendation practices that exploit consumer vulnerabilities for commercial purposes
- **Subscription management** — difficulties with cancellation, renewal, and transparency in digital subscriptions
- **Unfair contract terms** — situations in which users are driven to accept terms they would not otherwise agree to

The Commission framed the initiative around findings from a 2024 fitness check of three EU consumer protection directives, which concluded that core EU consumer laws remain "relevant but [have] only partially achieved their objectives."

## Rationale

The Commission identifies several structural drivers:

1. Data-driven personalisation and recommender systems now directly shape consumer choices, often against consumers' own interests
2. Interface design increasingly exploits cognitive biases and emotional vulnerabilities
3. Enforcement has been insufficient, with fragmented national approaches creating regulatory arbitrage
4. Consumers face a "patchwork" of divergent Member State rules rather than harmonised protection
5. There are inadequate incentives for traders to voluntarily adopt the highest standards of consumer protection

## Public Consultation

The Commission held a public consultation on the forthcoming Digital Fairness Act until **24 October 2025**. Responses were sharply divided:

- **Consumer-protection organisations** called for tighter rules and broader scope
- **Online platforms** (e.g., TikTok) argued there is "only a very limited need for further regulatory intervention"

The European Parliament has already called for action in its December 2023 resolution on "addictive design of online services and consumer protection in the EU single market" (2023/2043(INI)).

## Consumer Harms Addressed

This regulation directly targets several consumer harm vectors relevant to the AI-driven commerce ecosystem:

1. **Algorithmic dark patterns** — Interface designs that exploit LLM-mediated consumer interactions
2. **Vulnerability exploitation** — Personalisation that targets known cognitive biases, emotional states, or socioeconomic vulnerabilities
3. **Addiction-by-design** — Features that engineer compulsive engagement, particularly relevant for AI-powered recommendation engines
4. **Influencer-mediated deception** — Undisclosed sponsored content in AI-mediated conversations and social platforms
5. **Subscription lock-in** — Dark patterns that make cancellation or price comparison artificially difficult

## Significance for AI Consumer Protection

The Digital Fairness Act represents the first comprehensive EU legislative framework specifically targeting the intersection of consumer protection, interface design, and algorithmic personalisation. Its scope is broad enough to encompass:

- **Dark patterns in AI-mediated interfaces** — including chat-based LLM interfaces, voice assistants, and agentic commerce systems
- **Algorithmic manipulation** — not just static interface designs but dynamic, personalised user experiences shaped by AI
- **Commercial persuasion** — including sponsored content embedded in AI-generated responses (relevant to papers like 2604.04263, 2604.08525)

The Act's consumer-focused framing makes it a critical companion to the EU AI Act, filling the gap between high-risk classification (AI Act) and consumer-facing manipulation (Digital Fairness Act).
