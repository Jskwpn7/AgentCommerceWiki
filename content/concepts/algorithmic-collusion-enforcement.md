# Algorithmic Collusion — Enforcement Phase

**Status:** Active enforcement (transitioned from theory)
**Key regulator:** CMA
**Whistleblower reward:** Up to £250,000

## Overview

Algorithmic collusion has moved from theoretical concern to active enforcement territory. The CMA's hotel investigation and leniency policy update mark a critical inflection point in UK competition law enforcement.

## The Four Scenarios (CMA Guidance, 4 March 2026)

1. **Algorithm as a messenger** — firms use pricing algorithms to communicate and implement collusive terms
2. **Algorithm as a tacit collusion facilitator** — algorithms enable tacit collusion through repeated interaction
3. **Algorithm as a collateral damage** — independent algorithm use leads to supracompetitive outcomes
4. **Algorithm as a common third party** — competitors use shared algorithm that facilitates coordination

## Active Investigations

- **Hotel algorithmic pricing** (launched 26 Feb 2026): Three hotel chains and their data analytics provider
- **Expected outcome:** H2 2026

## Leniency Policy

Updated to cover "exchanging competitively sensitive information through a shared algorithm." Whistleblowers can receive up to £250,000 for tips.

See: [[CMA Whistleblower Statistics]]

## International Context

- **US Third Circuit** (30 July 2026): Revived algorithmic pricing claims against casino-hotels
- **European enforcement:** Growing activity across EU member states

## Consumer Harm

- Supracompetitive pricing
- Reduced transparency
- Increased switching costs

## Business Compliance Requirements

- Audit algorithmic pricing systems for collusion risks
- Implement safeguards against information sharing via shared algorithms
- Review leniency policy implications
- Document algorithmic decision-making processes
