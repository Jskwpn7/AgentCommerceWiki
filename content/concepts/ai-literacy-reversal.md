# AI Literacy Obligation Reversal

## Definition

The Digital Omnibus modifies Article 4 of the AI Act on AI literacy, shifting responsibility from providers and deployers to Member States and the Commission. Instead of requiring companies to ensure staff operating AI systems have sufficient AI literacy, the amendment replaces the binding obligation with a non-binding encouragement for public authorities.

## How it Manifests in the UK

The UK's pro-innovation AI governance framework could adopt a similar "soft law" approach, replacing binding sectoral requirements with voluntary guidelines — reducing enforcement leverage and consumer redress rights.

## Examples and Evidence

- **From duty to encouragement:** The key difference between a duty to "ensure" and a general encouragement is "decisive for enforcement, liability and consumer redress" [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
- **Basic precondition for safety:** AI literacy is essential for safe development, deployment, and use of AI systems — staff who don't understand AI function, data reliance, and risks are less able to prevent misuse, bias, discrimination, or harmful outcomes [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
- **Problematic market signal:** Transforming a binding obligation into non-binding encouragement "sends a problematic signal to the market which weakens accountability" [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
- **Consumer-facing contexts:** Particularly concerning for deployers integrating off-the-shelf AI systems without sufficient internal expertise to assess risks and impacts [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
- **No early-stage justification:** Current lack of enforcement cases reflects the early stage of implementation — "removing this safeguard now risks preemptively lowering standards before its importance can be properly assessed" [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
