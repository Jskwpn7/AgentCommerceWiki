# Agentic Supply Chain Asymmetry

**Also known as:** Four-agent orchestration asymmetry, retailer vs. consumer power asymmetry in agentic AI

## Definition

Agentic supply chain asymmetry describes the structural imbalance created when retailers deploy multi-agent orchestration systems that optimize for retailer objectives (stock turnover, margin maximization) while consumers' personal agents compete against these enterprise-scale systems with fundamentally less information and capability — creating a **retailer-vs-consumer power asymmetry** at the agent level.

## The Four-Agent Model (Tredence 2025)

The dominant agentic supply chain architecture uses four coordinated agents:
1. **Forecasting Agent** — Updates demand predictions in real-time
2. **Inventory Agent** — Redirects stock allocation dynamically
3. **Procurement Agent** — Adjusts supplier orders based on forecasts
4. **Logistics Agent** — Reroutes shipments mid-transit

Gartner identifies **2026 as the inflection point** for multi-agent systems moving from pilots to enterprise-wide deployment, with supply chain management software spending expected to grow from <\$2B in 2025 to **\$53B by 2030**.

## Consumer Harm Vectors

| Vector | Mechanism | Impact |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| Stock hoarding | Retailer agents manipulate inventory signals to create artificial scarcity | Consumers face artificially inflated prices or unavailability |
| Price optimization asymmetry | Enterprise agents have full demand/cost data; consumer agents have only public-facing prices | Systematic disadvantage in price negotiation |
| Supply chain opacity | Multi-agent decisions cascade with no human oversight or explainability | Consumers cannot trace why products are unavailable or overpriced |

## Regulatory Gap

No UK regulatory framework currently addresses:
- Agent-mediated market distortions that do not involve explicit collusion
- Information asymmetry between enterprise agents and consumer agents
- Algorithmic stock allocation as a consumer harm vector

This gap is relevant to the **DMCCA 2024** enforcement pipeline and the DRCF's "Consumer interest and AI" Phase 2 consultation (closing September 2, 2026).
