# AI Act Self-Exemption Loophole (Article 6(3))

## Definition

The self-exemption regime under Article 6(3) of the AI Act allows providers to unilaterally conclude that an AI system meeting the criteria for high-risk classification does not pose a significant risk to health, safety, or fundamental rights. This self-assessment mechanism was highly contested during the legislative process as a critical loophole.

## How it Manifests in the UK

The UK's post-Brexit AI governance approach (pro-innovation, sector-specific regulation) creates parallel risks. Without a centralised UK equivalent of the EU AI Office and registration database, self-exempted high-risk AI systems deployed in the UK market would have minimal oversight.

## Examples and Evidence

- **Self-assessment mechanism:** Providers conclude their own AI system is low risk without independent verification [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
- **100+ civil society concern:** In October 2023, BEUC and more than 100 civil society organisations signed an open letter on the risks of AI deployers exempting themselves from rules [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
- **Registration safeguard (Article 6(4)):** Providers are required to submit basic information and reasoning for their exemption in the EU database — this was the minimum safeguard against misuse [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
- **Proposed deletion:** The Digital Omnibus proposes removing the registration obligation (Article 49(2) + Article 6(4)), which would be "the only transparency and accountability mechanism" attached to the self-exemption regime [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
- **Consequences of removal:** Without registration, authorities and civil society cannot monitor how frequently exemptions are used, in which sectors or Member States, or whether patterns of systemic misuse are emerging [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
- **Competition distortion:** Providers complying fully would be disadvantaged compared to those exploiting the exemption without scrutiny [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
- **Impact on collective redress:** Undermines effectiveness of collective redress mechanisms including actions under the Representative Actions Directive (RAD) [[raw/papers/BEUC-X-2026-007-Digital-Omnibus-AI.pdf]]
