# The Mills Review — AI and Consumer Protection

> **Publisher:** Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) — Andrew Bailey (Governor)
> **Date:** July 9, 2026
> **URL:** [Executive Summary](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/corporate/2026-07-09-mills-review-executive-summary.pdf)
> **Type:** Regulatory review — UK

## Overview

The Mills Review is the most significant UK regulatory assessment of AI's impact on consumer protection in 2026. Commissioned by FCA Governor Andrew Bailey and conducted by Dame Anne-Marie Mills (former CEO of the Competition and Markets Authority), the review examines how AI systems are reshaping financial services and what regulatory frameworks are needed to protect consumers.

The review is informed by the FCA's own consumer research (1,000 UK adults, July 2026) demonstrating critically low trust in AI financial advice, widespread misunderstanding of liability, and a significant gap between AI capability growth and consumer confidence.

## Key Findings

### Consumer Trust — Critically Low

| Metric | Finding |
|--------|---------|
| Trust in general-purpose AI for financial advice | **26%** of UK adults trust AI tools for financial advice |
| Trust in AI for simple financial decisions | **40%** comfortable with AI making simple financial decisions |
| Understanding of AI liability | Only **40%** understand who is liable if AI makes bad recommendations |
| Adoption readiness | Only **20%** (≈11M adults) likely to use agentic AI for finance within pre-set goals |
| Trust drivers | 32% prioritise "clear protections if something goes wrong"; 30% want "evidence of accuracy and reliability" |

### Accountability Gap

The review identifies a fundamental accountability gap: consumers will not delegate to AI systems where liability is unclear. The dominant assumption among consumers is that the AI provider — not the regulated firm deploying the AI — bears responsibility for AI-generated advice. This creates a misalignment with UK regulatory frameworks that assign responsibility to authorised firms.

### AI-Generated Financial Fraud

The Mills Review found that AI is enabling new fraud vectors:
- Deepfake voice cloning used in "grandparent fraud" and business email compromise
- AI-generated synthetic identities for fraud
- AI-powered personalised scam targeting based on financial data
- Synthetic media used in identity verification bypass

### Regulatory Gaps Identified

1. **AI deployment liability:** No clear framework for assigning liability when authorised firms deploy third-party AI systems
2. **Adaptive AI monitoring:** Traditional supervisory approaches insufficient for AI systems that change behaviour post-deployment
3. **Consumer protection enforcement:** FCA enforcement powers tested by AI-mediated harm where the chain of causation is complex
4. **Standards and certifications:** Absence of sector-specific AI quality standards for financial services

## Recommendations

The Mills Review recommends:

1. **Fiduciary-style duties** for custodial AI agents handling consumer assets
2. **Ex ante testing requirements** before AI systems are deployed in regulated activities
3. **Clear liability attribution** between AI providers and regulated firms
4. **AI quality standards** specific to financial services
5. **Consumer education programmes** to improve financial AI literacy
6. **Supervisory technology upgrades** to monitor adaptive AI systems
7. **International coordination** on AI regulatory standards
