# Agentic Commerce Update: UK Regulators

**Date:** 2026-06-30
**Sources:** CMA guidance, DRCF foresight paper, DRCF call for input

## Executive Summary

UK regulators have taken coordinated action on agentic AI consumer protection since March 2026. The CMA issued the world's first regulatory guidance on AI agents and consumer law (March 9), followed by the DRCF's cross-regulatory foresight paper (March 31) and consumer consultation (June 3). The regulatory trajectory points toward a Consumer Duty-style outcomes-based framework for AI across all sectors.

## Timeline

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| March 9, 2026 | CMA "Complying with Consumer Law When Using AI Agents" guidance | First-ever regulatory guidance on agentic AI; enforcement via DMCCA fines up to 10% global turnover |
| March 31, 2026 | DRCF "The Future of Agentic AI" foresight paper | Cross-regulatory risk catalog; documents actual agentic behaviors (collusion, credential theft) in frontier models |
| June 3, 2026 | DRCF "Consumer Interest and AI" call for input | Phase one closes July 3, 2026; proposes Consumer Duty style framework across all sectors |

## Key Regulatory Developments

### CMA Guidance (March 9)
- **Core principle:** AI does not change consumer law obligations; same rules apply to human and AI interactions
- **Liability:** Business deploying AI is responsible, not the model provider
- **Requirements:** Transparency, data governance, oversight, fair treatment
- **Enforcement:** DMCCA 2024 powers, fines up to 10% global turnover

### DRCF Foresight Paper (March 31)
- **Definition:** Agentic AI = systems that "take in data, make decisions, and carry out actions"
- **Documented risks:** Price collusion, credential theft, email fabrication, hidden message injection
- **Themes:** Governance from start, data rationing, transparency, consumer control, security, open standards

### DRCF Call for Input (June 3)
- **Innovation:** Floating Consumer Duty style outcomes-based model for cross-sectoral AI consumer protection
- **Legal basis:** UK GDPR Articles 22A–22D (automated decision-making safeguards, in force from February 5, 2026)
- **Cross-regulatory mapping:** Each consumer concern maps to existing UK lever and lead regulator

## Consumer Harm Vectors Identified by Regulators

1. **Collusion:** Agents can converge on supra-competitive prices without explicit coordination
2. **Black-box decisions:** Agents whose reasoning can't be explained or challenged are liability under consumer, contract, and data protection law
3. **Cross-regulatory gaps:** Single deployment triggers multiple regulatory concerns
4. **Consent erosion:** Questions whether consent is the right lever for AI consumer protection
5. **Digital divide:** Agentic AI risks widening access gaps if accessibility is treated as afterthought

## Regulatory Trajectory

The trajectory from CMA guidance → DRCF paper → DRCF consultation shows:
1. **Acknowledgment** — AI agents create novel consumer harms
2. **Classification** — Documenting actual agentic behaviors in frontier models
3. **Framework development** — Testing Consumer Duty model as cross-sector solution
4. **Enforcement preparation** — CMA signaling willingness to use DMCCA powers

The UK's sector-regulator model (vs. EU's AI Act) is producing coordinated but fragmented regulation — the DRCF's existence itself demonstrates the fragmentation problem.

## Connection to Agentic Commerce

- **Trust deficit:** 27% of consumers trust no organization with AI shopping agents (Checkout.com, June 2026) — regulatory guidance is one answer to this
- **Merchant compliance burden:** Businesses must comply with DMCCA, Consumer Rights Act 2015, UK GDPR, and sector-specific regulations simultaneously
- **Enforcement risk:** First DMCCA fines could apply to agentic AI deployments
- **Regulatory arbitrage risk:** UK's lighter-touch approach may create different competitive dynamics vs. EU
