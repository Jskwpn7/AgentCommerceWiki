# Checkout.com Agentic Commerce Survey 2026

## Document Details

- **Publisher:** Checkout.com (Global payments network)
- **Type:** Consumer sentiment research report
- **Date:** June 9, 2026
- **URL:** https://www.checkout.com/newsroom/consumer-demand-for-ai-shopping-is-forming-fast-but-trust-for-agentic-commerce-is-still-catching-up

## Summary

Checkout.com published a consumer survey on attitudes toward AI-driven purchasing, revealing a significant gap between consumer expectations for agentic commerce and trust in the underlying technology. The survey shows that while consumer demand for AI shopping is forming rapidly, merchant readiness and consumer trust lag behind.

## Key Findings

### Expectations

- **33%** of consumers expect at least 10% of their purchases to be AI-driven within a year
- Younger demographics are significantly more comfortable with AI purchasing
- **72%** of merchants agree that consumers will adopt AI shopping faster than businesses are ready for it

### Trust and Resistance

- **24%** of consumers say they will **never** delegate purchases to AI
- **27%** trust **no organization** to operate an AI shopping agent
- **72% of Millennials** are comfortable allowing AI to make purchases
- Only **25% of consumers aged 55+** are comfortable with AI purchases

### Merchant Readiness Gap

- Merchants acknowledge consumer demand is outpacing their preparedness
- The gap between consumer expectations and merchant readiness represents a market risk: premature deployment could damage consumer trust

## Relevance to UK Consumer Harms

This survey provides critical **quantitative evidence** for the trust gap theme across multiple wiki entries:

1. **[[ai-shopping-agents]]** — The 33% expectation vs. 27% trust gap demonstrates the market fragility of agentic commerce. If AI shopping agents gain traction, a single high-profile failure could reverse adoption.

2. **[[agentic-commerce-tokenization]]** — The 24% "never delegate" figure represents consumers who will resist tokenized commerce, creating friction for Visa/Mastercard's infrastructure rollout.

3. **Consumer protection implications:** The significant generational divide (72% Millennials vs. 25% 55+) raises questions about whether older consumers are being adequately protected from AI-driven purchasing they may not fully understand or consent to.

4. **Merchant trust gap:** 27% trust no organization with AI shopping agents suggests a fundamental legitimacy problem. Which organizations could earn this trust? Payment processors? Retailers? Platform companies?

## Limitations

- Methodology details (sample size, fieldwork dates) not specified in the press release
- US/global focus; UK-specific data is less granular than the companion ACI/YouGov survey
- Self-reported willingness to delegate purchases does not equal actual behavior
- Merchant readiness data is based on merchant opinion, not objective assessment
