# When Pricing Agents Meet Buying Agents: Personalized Pricing and Verifiable Trust

## Paper Details

- **Authors:** Chupeng Xie
- **arXiv ID:** 2607.16343
- **Published:** 2026-07-12
- **Category:** Theoretical Economics (econ.TH)
- **Length:** 67 pages + online appendix
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16343

## Summary

This theoretical economics paper studies personalised pricing when both seller and buyer principals delegate to agents that receive different value signals and execute machine-enforced mandates. It examines the conditions under which personalised pricing can be implemented, the role of verification mechanisms, and the strategic behaviour of delegated agents in repeated transactions.

## Key Findings

1. **Incompatibility of equal nominal surplus rules:** When each agent applies equal surplus rules to its own posterior beliefs (rather than the principal's), the resulting pricing is incompatible — each agent interprets fairness differently from its own information set.

2. **Signal attestation enables high prices:** Requiring evidence of a high seller signal can legitimate high prices by providing verifiable information to the buyer's agent.

3. **Execution attestation prevents extraction:** Requiring evidence of restrained pricing rules prevents unauthorised value extraction by the seller's agent.

4. **State-dependent Markov policies in repeated transactions:** Offers above a buyer's reference cap depreciate relationship capital, producing state-dependent pricing strategies.

5. **Verification is privately underprovided:** When the seller doesn't internalise avoided buyer inspection, verification is underprovided from a social welfare perspective. However, it can be overprovided when it facilitates extraction.

6. **Seller can delegate valuation inference:** A seller can delegate both valuation inference and price selection to a pricing agent, creating a new form of strategic behaviour.

## Relevance to UK Consumer Harms

This paper provides the first formal economic treatment of personalised pricing harm when AI agents mediate both sides of a transaction. Key consumer protection implications:

- **Information asymmetry:** Agent delegation creates new asymmetries — the buyer agent may not accurately represent the consumer's true valuation, while the seller agent can extract value through strategic signal manipulation.
- **Verification failure:** Consumers cannot easily verify whether their agent is faithfully representing their preferences, creating a hidden harm channel.
- **Reframing personalised pricing:** The harm is not just one-sided seller exploitation but a complex interaction between two delegated agents with misaligned incentives. This fundamentally challenges the view that consumer-side AI agents reduce personalised pricing harm.
