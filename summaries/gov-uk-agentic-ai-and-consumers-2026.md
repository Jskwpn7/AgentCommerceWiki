# Agentic AI and Consumers — UK Government (GOV.UK)

**Source:** GOV.UK, published 9 March 2026 (CMA)  
**URL:** https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/agentic-ai-and-consumers/agentic-ai-and-consumers

## Executive Summary

The UK government (CMA) published formal guidance on agentic AI and consumers. Key findings:

- **Current state:** AI is embedded in search, recommendations, fraud detection, customer service and decision-support tools. Most consumer-facing AI operates as a tool supporting decisions, while coordination and action remain with the user.
- **Future state:** Agentic AI could drive a step change — agents that sense, decide and act autonomously, potentially executing transactions on behalf of users. Benefits include reduced friction, better personalisation, lower prices, and improved market participation for vulnerable consumers.
- **Risks:** Greater autonomy increases consequences of errors, heightens risks of manipulation and loss of consumer agency, and could lead to worse outcomes. People may be steered towards more profitable but less suitable products, potentially paying higher prices.
- **Consumer law:** UK consumer law applies whether decisions are made by people or by AI. The CMA's foundation model principles (transparency, accountability) remain directly relevant.

## Key Points

| Dimension | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Definition | AI agents instructed in natural language to achieve goals autonomously, navigating complexity, planning, coordinating, and taking actions across multiple services |
| Capabilities | Assess goals, retrieve real-time data (including personal data), execute actions autonomously (payments), store memory of past interactions |
| Regulatory framework | UK consumer law applies regardless of whether consumer interacts with people or AI systems |
| CMA guidance | "Complying with consumer law when using AI agents" — published March 9, 2026 |
| DMCCA enforcement | April 6, 2026 — Chapter 4 (ADR for consumer disputes) came into force |
| Penalties | Up to 10% of global annual turnover for DMCCA breaches |

## Consumer Law Application

- CMA's **foundation model principles** — transparency and accountability — remain directly relevant
- Businesses should focus on robust training, monitoring, and refinement, supported by appropriate human oversight
- Wider enablers needed: smart data schemes, secure digital identity, strong interoperability standards
- The UK has an opportunity to position itself at the forefront of trusted agentic innovation

## Illustrative Case Study: Personal Shopping and Finance Agent

A consumer signs up to manage shopping, subscriptions, and everyday finances across multiple platforms. The business uses an AI agent to monitor prices, negotiate offers, switch providers, and execute purchases within user-defined constraints.

### Key Developments Since March 2026

- **July 2026 — CMA "AI and Consumer Markets" Discussion Paper:** Explicitly identifies agentic AI as a "potential step change" with consequences for competition, consumer welfare, and digital ecosystem structure. Flags concentration risk at the platform layer where AI agents control purchasing decisions.
- **July 2026 — OBR Fiscal Context:** The Office for Budget Responsibility published a briefing on "Governed automation" examining how AI agents reshape public service delivery and fiscal sustainability, providing context for the CMA's competition-focused analysis.
- **December 2026 — FCA Mills Review:** Published the first comprehensive regulatory assessment of AI in retail financial services, introducing the Agentic Supervisory Model and five-stage AI Autonomy Spectrum directly relevant to the March 2026 guidance.
- **January 2027 — CMA Priority Letter:** Joint letter from CMA Chief Executive Sarah Cardell and DG Digital and Competition Dr Megan Holmes to the Secretary of State for Business and Trade, identifying agentic AI as a key competition enforcement priority.
