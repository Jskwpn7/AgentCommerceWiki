## Overview

Autonomous stores enable cashier-less shopping, real-time inventory tracking, and seamless customer interactions but face challenges including occlusion, scalability, theft prevention, and real-time data processing.

## Key Contributions

1. **Comprehensive Technology Survey**: Categories of sensing technologies across four dimensions:
   - Inventory tracking
   - Environmental monitoring
   - People-tracking
   - Theft detection
2. **Multi-Modal Integration**: Analysis of integrating computer vision, RFID, weight sensing, vibration detection, and LiDAR
3. **Privacy-First Framework**: Recommendations for privacy-conscious autonomous store design

## Technology Categories

| Technology | Use Case | Strengths | Limitations |
|------------|----------|-----------|-------------|
| Computer Vision | People-tracking, theft detection | High accuracy | Occlusion issues |
| RFID | Inventory tracking | Scalable | Cost for low-value items |
| Weight Sensing | Item verification | Simple | Limited item identification |
| LiDAR | 3D spatial awareness | Detailed mapping | Expensive |

## Implications for Consumer Protection

- **Privacy Concerns**: People-tracking systems may collect biometric data without explicit consent
- **Surveillance Creep**: Technologies deployed for theft prevention may enable broader consumer surveillance
- **Data Security**: Multi-modal sensing generates sensitive location and behavioral data
- **Algorithmic Bias**: Computer vision systems may have differential accuracy across demographic groups
- **Transparency**: Consumers often unaware of sensing technologies deployed in "cashier-less" stores
