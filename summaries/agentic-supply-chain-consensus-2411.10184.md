## Overview

Supply chain management requires frequent consensus decisions on inventory levels and delivery times across multiple companies. Traditional human consensus processes are time-intensive and costly, and existing automation solutions face high entry barriers for SMEs.

## Key Contributions

1. **Autonomous LLM Agents for SCM**: Novel framework using LLM agents for consensus-seeking in supply chain decisions
2. **Lowered Entry Barriers**: LLMs overcome SME exclusion from automated coordination tools
3. **Supply Chain-Specific Frameworks**: Tailored consensus-seeking mechanisms for different SCM scenarios
4. **Open-Source Foundation**: Code released for community advancement

## Mechanism

- LLMs leverage training on vast datasets to negotiate and reason
- Near-human-level consensus at scale with minimal entry barriers
- Addresses bullwhip effect through automated coordination
- Case study in inventory management validates effectiveness

## Implications for Consumer Protection

- **Supply Chain Transparency**: Autonomous agent coordination may obscure decision trails
- **SME Exclusion**: Automation benefits may concentrate power among large players
- **Consumer Impact**: Supply chain coordination changes affect pricing, availability, and product quality
- **Accountability Gaps**: When agents reach consensus autonomously, responsibility for supply chain decisions becomes diffuse
