# BEUC Digital Omnibus Summary

## Overview

BEUC (the European Consumer Organisation) published a **Digital Omnibus** proposal aimed at simplifying and strengthening EU consumer law in the digital context. The proposal seeks to modernise consumer protection rules to address harms created by digital platforms, AI systems, and algorithmic decision-making.

## Key Proposals

### 1. Expanded Definition of Traders
- Would cover platforms acting as "traders" when they facilitate transactions
- Addresses the "who is the trader?" gap in algorithmic marketplaces
- Relevant to [[ai-agents-in-markets]] where platforms and AI agents blur trader/consumer boundaries

### 2. AI-Related Transparency
- Requires disclosure when consumers are interacting with AI systems
- Mandates explanation of algorithmic decision-making affecting consumers
- Addresses [[conflict-of-interest-in-llm-deployment]] concerns

### 3. Dark Pattern Prohibition
- Explicit prohibition of dark patterns in digital interfaces
- Aligns with [[online-choice-architecture-and-dark-patterns-in-the-UK]] analysis
- Would provide clearer enforcement basis than CPRs 2008 alone

### 4. Algorithmic Bias Protection
- Requires impact assessments for algorithmic systems used in consumer-facing contexts
- Creates accountability for [[systemic-fragility-in-ai-economies]]
- Mandates data minimisation in AI processing

## UK Implications

The Digital Omnibus proposal highlights the regulatory gap between the EU and UK. Post-Brexit, the UK lacks a comprehensive digital consumer protection framework equivalent to the DSA or the proposed Omnibus measure.
