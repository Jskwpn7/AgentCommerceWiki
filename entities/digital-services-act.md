# Digital Services Act

## Overview

The **Digital Services Act (DSA)** is an EU regulation that establishes a comprehensive framework for the responsibilities of digital platforms, including content moderation, transparency, and user rights. It entered into force in November 2022 and applies progressively from February 2024.

## Key Provisions Relevant to Consumer Protection

### Dark Patterns (Article 25)
- Prohibits design patterns that deceive or manipulate user behaviour
- Requires transparency in recommender systems
- Gives users choice over recommender system parameters

### Transparency Requirements
- Monthly active user disclosure
- Ad library with targeting information
- Risk assessment obligations for Very Large Online Platforms (VLOPs)

### Algorithmic Accountability
- Risk assessments for systemic risks (misinformation, harm to minors, civic discourse)
- Independent auditing requirements
- Data access for researchers

## Relationship to UK Law

The UK does not have an equivalent to the DSA. Post-Brexit, the UK has the Online Safety Act 2023 (focused on harmful content) but lacks the DSA's focus on:
- Algorithmic transparency
- Dark pattern prohibition
- Recommender system regulation

## DSA Enforcement Actions (2026)

The DSA enforcement wave has significant implications for UK consumers who use EU platforms:

| Date | Entity | Fine/Action | Reason |
|------|--------|-------------|--------|
| 2026-07-20 | AliExpress | €550 million (second-largest DSA fine after X's €120M) | DSA non-compliance — product marketplace safety |
| 2026-02 | TikTok | Enforcement action (preliminary findings) | Addictive design |

The AliExpress fine is particularly significant as it relates to product marketplace safety — directly relevant to UK consumers shopping on EU platforms. The cross-jurisdictional implications may inform future UK regulatory approaches.
