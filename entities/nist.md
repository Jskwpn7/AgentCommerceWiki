# NIST — National Institute of Standards and Technology

## Overview

The **National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)** is a non-regulatory agency of the U.S. Department of Commerce. It is the primary U.S. government body for developing technical standards, measurement science, and technology guidelines. NIST operates under the Secretary of Commerce (currently Gina M. Raimondo) and is directed by the NIST Director and Under Secretary of Commerce for Standards and Technology (currently Laurie E. Locascio).

## AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)

NIST published the AI RMF in January 2023, directed by the National Artificial Intelligence Initiative Act of 2020 (P.L. 116-283). It is described as a **living document** with a two-number versioning system, expected to undergo formal community review by 2028. The AI RMF Playbook is updated on a semi-annual basis via comments sent to AIframework@nist.gov.

Development involved engagement with the AI community through:
- Formal Request for Information
- Three widely attended workshops
- Public comments on a concept paper and two drafts of the Framework
- Discussions at multiple public forums
- Many small group meetings

The AI RMF was developed in collaboration with the private and public sectors and is consistent with the National Security Commission on Artificial Intelligence recommendations and the Plan for Federal Engagement in Developing Technical Standards and Related Tools.

## Governance Model

The AI RMF's four-function architecture (GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE) has been influential in shaping international approaches to AI governance. Its voluntary, principles-based, non-sector-specific character makes it directly comparable to the UK's current regulatory stance (pro-innovation, sector-led regulation under the DMCC Act 2024).

## Connection to UK Consumer Harms Research

NIST's AI RMF is a key reference for the UK consumer harms wiki because:

1. Its seven trustworthy AI characteristics provide a comprehensive harm taxonomy applicable to all UK consumer sectors
2. The GOVERN function offers a structured approach that UK regulators could adopt or adapt for AI oversight
3. Its recognition that AI risks differ fundamentally from traditional software risks validates existing wiki analysis of [[regulatory-failure]]
4. The framework's emphasis on residual risk and lifecycle-stage risk variation is directly relevant to consumer protection challenges
5. The framework's attributes (risk-based, resource-efficient, pro-innovation, voluntary, consensus-driven, plain language, outcome-focused, law-agnostic) mirror the UK government's current AI regulatory philosophy
