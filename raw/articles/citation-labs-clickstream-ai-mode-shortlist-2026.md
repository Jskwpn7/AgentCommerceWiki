# BOFU Shortlists in Google AI Mode: 48-Participant Behavioral Study

**Authors:** Citation Labs / Clickstream Solutions; Kevin Indig (Growth Memo)
**Published:** March 2026 (blog post 2026-06-30)
**URL:** https://citationlabs.com/funnels-new-gatekeeper-google-search-ai-mode-shortlisting-behavioral-study/
**Reading time:** ~20 min

## Abstract

Third in a series of usability studies examining how consumers behave in Google Search vs. Google AI Mode. This study focuses on bottom-of-funnel (BOFU) purchase research, comparing how buyers build a candidate set for major purchases in AI Mode versus standard Search. Core finding: AI Mode intensifies shortlist acceptance — 88% of shortlists come directly from the AI output.

## Source Details

- **Organization:** Citation Labs (sponsor/researcher) + Clickstream Solutions (fielding partner)
- **Platform:** UXtweak (remote unmoderated usability)
- **Panel:** Prolific (U.S.-based, vetted)
- **Sponsoring author:** Kevin Indig (Growth Memo) — previously published two studies in this series on Search Engine Journal

## Methodology

- **Design:** Remote, unmoderated think-aloud usability study
- **Participants:** 48 U.S.-based consumers (21 excluded: 7 technical, 14 insufficient engagement)
- **Tasks:** 185 major-purchase tasks across 4 categories
  - Insurance (n=27), Television (n=46), Laptop (n=29), Washer/Dryer (n=47)
- **Duration:** 23 hours total, ~30 min per participant
- **Data:** Screen recordings + speak-aloud audio + post-task surveys + 275+ pages of transcribed audio
- **Analysis:** Trained analysts coded micro/macro behaviors (hovers, clicks, follow-up queries, rank overrides, reading patterns, trust signals)

### Task Protocol
For each task, participants completed a major-purchase research session in either AI Mode or standard Search, then reported their final shortlist.

### Study Design
Remote unmoderated think-aloud protocol — participants spoke their thoughts aloud as they completed tasks. Captured both behavioral and attitudinal data.

### Participants
Curated panel of 48 from Prolific. Sessions lasted ~20-30 minutes with monetary incentive. 21 excluded for technical issues or insufficient engagement.

### Data Capture
Sessions set up and conducted via UXtweak. Trained analysts systematically annotated each recording for quantitative and qualitative markers.

## Statistical Confidence

> With 48 participants, our confidence intervals are wider than they would be in a large-scale survey. Our findings are best understood as directional. Several factors strengthen confidence: 185 task-level observations, behavioral patterns (more stable than self-reported attitudes), think-aloud protocols yielding rich qualitative material, and 275+ pages of transcribed audio.

## Key Findings

### Shortlist Origin
| AI Mode | Search |
|---|---|
| AI Adopted: 88% of 149 tasks | Nearly 50% built from multiple sources |
| No-click rate: 64% overall | ~89% clicked something |
| Insurance no-click: 85% | — |

### Position Predicts Choice
- 74% chose rank 1
- Mean rank of first choice: 1.35
- 26% overrode rank, but 81% of overrides kept the AI shortlist
- Only 10% chose rank 3 or lower

### Trust Signals
- AI framing: 37% AI Mode vs 6% Search (59% for insurance)
- Brand recognition: 34% AI Mode vs 23% Search
- Multi-source convergence: 5% AI Mode vs 37% Search
- Source trust: 17% AI Mode vs 23% Search

### Brand Outcomes (Concentrated in AI Mode)
- Insurance: top 3 brands = ~64% of choices
- Laptop: ThinkPad + MacBook = ~86%
- Television: LG + Samsung + Sony = ~45%
- Washer/Dryer: LG + Electrolux + Whirlpool = ~48%

### External Visits
- AI Mode: 23% visited external sites (verification)
- Search: 67% visited external sites (discovery/comparison)

### Reading Behavior
- 70% skimmed, 18% scanned, <7% read comprehensively
- Among deep readers: 88% still adopted the AI shortlist

### Narrowness Frustration
- AI Mode: 15%, Search: 11% — no statistically meaningful difference

### Follow-up Query Types (AI Mode)
| Type | % Of Tasks |
|---|---|
| Refine (narrow by specs/price) | 42% |
| Clarify (ask why recommended) | 35% |
| Delegate (ask AI to choose) | 30% |
| Reframe (shift to different category) | 26% |

### Reading Pattern (AI Mode)
| Pattern | % Of Tasks |
|---|---|
| Skim (glanced for takeaways) | 70% |
| Scan (searched for specific) | 18% |
| Comprehensive | 7% |
| Multi-pattern (skim + scan/deeper) | 9% |

## Consumer Harm Vectors (This Wiki's Analysis)

1. **Structural brand visibility bias** — brands missing from the AI shortlist become invisible at the decision stage
2. **Consolidation risk** — top 3 brands capture 45–93% of outcomes
3. **Opaque recommendation ranking** — functions like sponsored placement without disclosure
4. **Trust signal capture** — AI framing replaces independent evidence evaluation
5. **Insurance sector** — highest risk category (85% no-click, 59% AI framing)

## Kevin Indig's Three Takeaways

1. "If your brand is not in the AI's output, it's invisible"
2. "How the AI presents your brand matters as much as whether it appears"
3. "Challenger brands need recognition before the AI moment"

## Study Limitations

- Sample size (48 participants) = wider CIs than large-scale surveys
- Findings are directional, not population-level estimates
- U.S.-only participants
- Only 4 product categories studied
- No systematic coding of whether every external destination first appeared in the AI answer

## Author/Sponsor Bias Note

Study sponsored by Citation Labs (helps brands improve AI visibility). Authors have a clear commercial interest in demonstrating AI Mode's narrowing effects. Treat magnitude of effects (percentages) with caution; directional findings are consistent with related research.
