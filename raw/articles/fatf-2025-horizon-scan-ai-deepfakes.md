# FATF: Horizon Scan AI and Deepfakes (Summary from TLT Analysis)

## Source
- **Original**: FATF (Financial Action Task Force)
- **Report**: *Horizon Scan: AI and Deepfakes*
- **Published**: 22 December 2025
- **Authors**: Ben Cooper and Tamara Raoufi (TLT LLP analysis, 5 January 2026)
- **Type**: Horizon scan on emerging AML/CFT/CPF risks and recommendations

## Purpose
Forward-looking perspective on current and potential AI-related risks and trends. Part of FATF's staged approach to emerging technologies. Aims to identify and explain developing risks and vulnerabilities associated with AI through the lens of Anti-Money Laundering, Countering the Financing of Terrorism, and Countering the Financing of Proliferation (AML/CFT/CPF).

## Key Findings

### Deepfake-Enabled Identity Fraud Is Surging
Synthetic audio, video, or images allow criminals to convincingly impersonate individuals, defeating KYC, remote onboarding, biometric verification, and liveness checks. FATF highlights impersonation of senior staff in real-time scams, pressuring staff to authorise payments.

### AI Automates Transaction Laundering
Criminals use AI to execute complex, high-volume laundering schemes — patterning transactions in ways that traditional rules-based systems can't detect. Autonomous AI agents may orchestrate these flows without human supervision.

### Detection Lags Behind Creation
Fraud detection tech hasn't kept pace with generative AI. Deepfakes can pass through liveness and biometric checks and only trigger alarms later — creating a dangerous window for fund diversion.

### Cross-Border Vulnerabilities Magnified
Deepfake onboarding and cross-border transactions often route through jurisdictions with inconsistent AML regulations or weaker digital ID frameworks, making tracing and enforcement more difficult.

### Regtech: A Crucial Defence Mechanism
FATF emphasises using AI also for compliance, including deployment of anomaly detection systems, biometrics verification, deepfake detection, and automated screening — turning AI into part of the solution.

### Strengthening Governance & Controls
FATF signals that supervisors will intensify scrutiny of AI-specific AML/CFT controls. Firms should create structured AI risk governance frameworks — defining ownership, risk appetite, monitoring and assurance processes.

### Public–Private Information Sharing
Effective defence requires collaboration: FATF recommends mechanisms for sharing intelligence on emerging AI-enabled threats, enabling institutions and authorities to respond quickly.

## Recommendations for Firms
1. Conduct an AI deepfake risk assessment — map where AI-deepfake risk intersects with operations
2. Upgrade tech stack — pilot tools to detect synthesised media in real time
3. Fortify onboarding & verification — enhance multi-factor protocols with human validation
4. Build AI risk governance — establish clear ownership structures with board-level oversight
5. Enhance training & awareness — educate front-line teams on deepfake/AI-facilitated fraud typologies
6. Form public-private partnerships — engage with regulators, law enforcement, peers
7. Prepare for regulatory exams — document risk assessments, AI control architecture, technology trail
