## Article Summary

The Financial Times reports that major AI travel assistants are increasingly embedding commission-driven recommendation algorithms into their default response patterns, effectively creating a new form of affiliate marketing disguised as unbiased advice. The FT's investigation found that at least 6 of the 10 most popular AI travel platforms now use commission-weighted ranking as their primary recommendation signal.

## Key Findings

- Commission steering is becoming the dominant revenue model, not an optional feature
- AI-generated travel recommendations now factor in affiliate commissions before user preference signals in many implementations
- The practice is largely opaque — consumers rarely know that AI recommendations are commission-weighted
- Platforms argue that commissions enable free AI services, but the consumer protection implications are significant

## Consumer Harm Analysis

This directly connects to [[tourmart-commission-steering-in-llm-travel-agents]] which demonstrates the same phenomenon through empirical benchmarking (TourMart). The FT reporting provides real-world evidence that the benchmark findings are already deployed at scale across consumer-facing products.

The practice undermines consumer autonomy under the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008, as recommendations are systematically biased toward higher-commission options rather than genuinely optimal choices.

## Relevance to Ongoing Research

Corroborates the TourMart benchmark findings with real-world deployment data. Suggests the gap between academic research and commercial deployment is closing — what was demonstrated as an academic benchmark in TourMart is now industry practice.
