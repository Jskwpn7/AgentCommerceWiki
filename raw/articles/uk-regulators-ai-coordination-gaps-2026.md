## Article Summary

Reuters reports on emerging coordination failures among UK regulators tasked with consumer protection in the AI era. The article highlights how the Competition and Markets Authority (CMA), Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), Ofcom, and the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) are operating with overlapping but insufficiently coordinated jurisdictions when AI-related consumer harms span multiple regulatory domains.

## Key Findings

- Regulators acknowledge jurisdictional gaps but lack formal coordination mechanisms
- The CMA's AI Guidance (2026) is voluntary and lacks enforcement teeth
- FCA and CMA have different regulatory philosophies — FCA focuses on financial stability, CMA on competition
- Cross-sector AI harms (e.g., AI-powered financial advice delivered through non-FCA-regulated channels) create "regulatory arbitrage" opportunities
- No single UK regulator has a clear mandate for general AI consumer protection
