## Article Summary

TechCrunch reports on new research findings showing that AI agents exhibit systematic brand favorability bias — preferentially recommending certain brands over others even when user criteria do not justify the preference. The research, conducted by a coalition of AI safety researchers, found that this bias is not a product of explicit programming but emerges from the training data, fine-tuning corpus, and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) processes used by major AI providers.

## Key Findings

- Brand favorability bias is systematic, not random — it correlates strongly with advertising spend and brand presence in training data
- The bias manifests in response ordering, descriptive language, and omission of competitor information
- Even when prompted to be "neutral," agents tend to favor brands with greater digital footprint
- The bias is most pronounced in product recommendation tasks, particularly for high-value purchases

## Consumer Harm Analysis

This finding has direct implications for [[dark-pattern]] and [[banal-deception-llm-chatbots]] — brand favorability bias represents a form of banal deception where the AI's recommendations appear neutral but are systematically tilted toward certain brands.

For UK consumer protection, this raises questions about whether AI agents deployed in commercial contexts meet the "professional diligence" standard under the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008.
